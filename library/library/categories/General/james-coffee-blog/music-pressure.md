The sun is shining through the big and small clouds in the sky. The sun shines with such power that the areas that most catch my eye are the periphery of the darkest clouds, illuminated by the yellows of evening. The light coming through the window ebbs between faint and full, casting a shadow of four keys on my typewriter that rests on the coffee table.

I looked to the skies after a piano session, one in which I played along to some of my favourite songs to play on piano. These songs in particular bring out a degree of emotion that few pieces of music do, a degree of emotion that makes me want to press the keys with great intensity in the peaks of the song and, subsequently, with the lightest touch as the song flows. I live with the intensity of the music: slow, fast, slow.

On the piano I can let my thoughts fall away for a moment. I can press the same notes ten times over along to the song to which I am listening, feeling the significance of every one, enjoying the repetition and also knowing there is a moment when the notes I need to play will change, and knowing that my favourite part of the song is soon coming up. When I play, the pressure of the world is in the keys, expressible, musical.

⁂ ⁂ ⁂

I am good at putting pressure on myself, of making lists of things to do that I have to do, and the wiring in my mind is such that “good” feels like the right word, even though I know it is not the proper word to use; it should not be good to put pressure on one's self. I wish I were better at knowing when to slow down, to pause, particularly at those moments when there are more notes in the music than I know how to play, or want to play, the moments I know there is a song that brings me joy on the periphery but I persist with the one that brings me pressure.

In darker moments I feel that my days are lived by TODOs: a never-ending list of things to do. But when I go to the piano, the TODOs fall away and I only feel the notes, and the pressure between my finger and the keys. I feel all the emotions of the music; my mind can cry without feeling tears, while finding joy in approximating songs I love.

Today was one of those weeks where I kept trying to do more and more, when I didn’t slow down, when there was no regulating rhythm – music – to tell me that I was at a moment when it’s okay to take a breath, to pause, and so I kept going until as many of the TODOs were done as possible. But now, on this sunny evening, where, from my chair, I see the beauty of the blue sky and the silver of the clouds, I feel like I can slow down: to appreciate the parts of the music where everything is slow, to appreciate the pauses between the songs. Rests – space – make music as much as notes.
