When I last visited the National Museum of Scotland, they had an \~A5 booklet of 10 ideas of things to do in an art gallery. I love the idea of a guide that helps you navigate a gallery space.

Yesterday, I decided to make a zine in a similar vein, except with some of my own tips for exploring galleries. In the process of making this zine, I realised that I may one day want to write another version that is more edited. There may even be space for me to make multiple zines: one for admiring the architecture of and spaces in galleries, and another for visually analysing paintings, another for analysing statues, and more. <sup class="footnote-reference" id="f-1"><a href="https://jamesg.blog/longform-feed#1">1</a></sup>

Below is the tiny Gallery Guide I made. On the cover, I drew a version of Cézanne’s Montagne Saint-Victore, sketched with reference to [this photo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mont_Sainte-Victoire_(Cézanne)#/media/File:Paul_Cézanne_-_Montagne_Saint-victoire_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg), which I think is of [this painting](https://www.musee-orsay.fr/fr/oeuvres/montagne-sainte-victoire-1469) (the attribution on Wikipedia is unclear at the time of writing this post). Looking at the Wikipedia page, I had no idea Cézanne had painted the mountain scene several times. Fascinating!

Within the zine, I included advice that takes you from first entering a gallery all the way to appreciating a piece of art, plus a bullet point list of a few things to look out for when visually analysing a painting. I wanted this zine to act as a way to welcome someone to a gallery, and to help someone find art that they might like.

I think I could have made this zine better if I had written all the words out before making the zine, but I like what I made so I thought I’d share it.
