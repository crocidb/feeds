The Macbook Air in [Carlos](https://carlosacollazo.com/re-my-perfect-laptop/)‘ response to Michael’s post “[My perfect laptop](https://michaelharley.net/posts/2026/08/17/my-perfect-laptop/)” caught my eye as soon as I saw it. The Macbook (which I will refer to as a “Mac” going forward) in the blog post looks similar to my first Mac. I originally wanted a Mac because I had been following [CS193p](https://jamesg.blog/2023/04/15/cs193p) on YouTube and I wanted to build something along with the course. When I got a Mac I didn’t end up following the course, but my love for programming – specifically for the web – continued to bloom.

Here is a photo of my Macbook Air, next to a French Press and in front of a funnel for use with the Aeropress (I had a coffee decoration motif going on at the time), and next to an artificial plant, which brings a bit of colour to the picture:

![](https://editor.jamesg.blog/content/images/2026/08/laptop.jpeg)

This isn't my "perfect laptop", but rather one I loved. I don’t think a “perfect laptop” can exist. For me, the more prudent question is “does this computer work and, importantly, does it bring me joy when using it?” Since my first Mac fits the bill for both, I thought I'd write about it here.

I remember thinking that my Mac, which, like Carlos, was likely a 2014 model, was all that I would need in a computer. I sometimes think back (with rose-tinted glasses, most likely) about how much I liked that computer – its design, its function – which I used until the battery would no longer hold a charge.

When I got my Mac the operating system pre-installed was OS X Mavericks, which I used for a tiny while – likely a few moments – before upgrading to the latest operating system version, OS X Yosemite.

Since then, I have used Mac computers, except for a period of two years or so when I used a laptop on which I had installed Linux. I bought that computer when my Mac was verging on unusable due to the battery life and I needed a new computer quickly. I loved using Ubuntu on the laptop, but, a few years later, I had an incident where I cleaned the keyboard and the cleaning agent stopped the keyboard from working. I used an external keyboard for a while, but I knew I needed a new computer. I decided on a Mac, having had such a good experience with my first one.

Today I continue to use a Mac. It is a great computer, powerful enough for all the videoconferencing and programming that I do (although I am not doing any programming that is computationally intensive; my computer is likely over-powered for my needs). I do miss the bezel edges on the 2014 Macbook, but perhaps today I may not appreciate them as much as I did back then. Indeed, I don't want to paint too rosy of a picture; I prefer to avoid romantic reminiscence when it comes to technology.

I might use a Linux computer again in the future, but I'm not sure; that’s a decision for hopefully years in the future when my current computer is no longer usable. I would love to use a Thinkpad one day, but all the ones I have seen are expensive. Perhaps a second-hand Thinkpad? Again, I’m not sure. I don’t need a new computer right now. For now, I am more than delighted with my Mac.
