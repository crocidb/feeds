*A warm glow from the still-on kitchen light passed through the hallway, illuminating the floral book, opened, on my lap. The night’s task: to start making the book my own.*

My impulse to create new things – music, stories, memories – is not dampened by the absence of daylight. Every few months, I sit at my piano, playing whatever music I am in the mood for; sometimes, I write a story under the cover of moonlight and my fairy lights. Tonight, I felt it was a good moment to start my new scrapbook, to do something calming to ease me out of one day and into the next.

I have had mementos from day trips and travel and stickers sent to me from friends sitting on my coffee table for the last few days, the first step in moving these memories from the paper bag I got from the British Library into somewhere easier to spend time with, somewhere more visible. I have tickets from the National Gallery of Scotland next to tickets from the Berlin Cathedral next to postage stamps I have cut out from letters. Many of these will go in my new scrapbook.

I started last night by collecting all the stickers I have received or picked up and had not yet put into my scrapbook. I keep most of my stickers on the back pages, so that I have plenty of room to add more. With a stack of stickers next to me, I started deciding where each should go, placing all my breakfast stickers that I received from a friend on one part of the page, and stickers from web communities on another part of the page: my favourite things – breakfast, the web, friendship – in sticker form, all in the same place.

There was no clock so I could not see the time. I was instead tuned into the memories associated with each object. Where did I collect this sticker? I sat with more questions. Where should each sticker go on the page? Do I have any duplicate stickers that I can send to friends? As the blank page became more colourful, I felt a little moment of joy.

I still have many more mementos to sift through: museum maps and programmes and the kinds of objects that don’t fit into a scrap book (floppy disks from conferences?). Each one transports me back in time a little bit. I think of the people, the places, the spaces, the conversations, the hopes, the dreams: adventures through the years. And then I think about all the blank pages, ready to be filled with colour.
