*Natural light bathed the Royal Mile on the warm, summer’s morning. The Fringe is close to being finished, but there are still a few days of shows left. I was standing amid a city wakening up: where, hours later, there would be music and singing and crowds.*

I gave my day to serendipity. Having no plans for the day, I let my feet take me where they wanted to go. I had a coffee and, because of the warm sunlight beaming through the window, left the cafe early and decided to go for a long walk to see more of the city. I wanted to see trees and beautiful old buildings, all sights on my way to my eventual destination: a panoramic view of the city.

When I look over Edinburgh from one of its hills, I get lost in detail. There is so much I could look at. I usually look forward, and then scan around, appreciating the rows of houses and the trees and the other hills that stand tall throughout the city. I also listen. There were several tourists at the top of the hill, some of whom had friends who were showing them around. I revelled in hearing Edinburgh described to people from other places, who I hope, even though they may only be visiting for a little while, will always remember their time here.

The cool breeze and the conversation in the background and the panoramic views helped me slow down after a long walk. I looked up to the clouds and thought how still they looked. I looked over to the silver sea in the bay, with Bass Rock in the distance. I turned around and looked at hills, and turned back to see more. Green, all around. Life, all around.

On the long walk back, I listened to songs in my earphones that I heard performed live earlier this week. The artist's music had a newfound resonance. “I have seen them before!” I sang along to myself as I walked, past the sandstone buildings and the green trees that line the streets. In no time, I was back in the city centre.

The joy of the Fringe is that you don’t need to do any planning. If you open your ears, you are bound to hear something. An electric guitarist stood out to me. Approaching the music, I saw he was performing solo on a bright yellow guitar. He had a few pieces of equipment – synthesisers(?), pedals – that he used to mix his music. It was a true delight to watch. During his closing riff, he lay on the cobble street with his guitar held over his chest and played with the intensity I imagine you can only play after practicing for years.

I caught the tail end of his performance, after which point he gave a charismatic speech and I kept walking. Coming from a park, I heard an acoustic guitar and a woman’s voice. I recognised the song. Valerie. Bagpipes started to play in the background. *This is Edinburgh.* Yet the artist’s soft voice and acoustic guitar, which she played without a plectrum, had my full attention. She finished her song, and ended her performance with applause. I joined in with the applause: it’s the least I can do for someone who has the courage to stand in the middle of the city and bring music.

*On reflection, today was not the first time I have heard guitars and bagpipes simultaneously, and it probably won’t be the last. Walking around the city at this time of year, you never know what you might see and hear and feel. Therein, the serendipity of Edinburgh: a city of Nature and music.*
