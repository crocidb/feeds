The sun shines through the silvery sky. I look up and around to see where blue might peek through the blanket that covers the sky. There is one space where I can see shades of blue through the silver. I think about how the sky invites me to look up, and then around: to be curious. What can I see today? I do this in all seasons and skies. On the warmest summer days, my eyes focus on the contrast between the green hills and the blue skies. On the coolest, clearest winter days, where the beauty of the clear sky warms the heart despite the cold, I search to see if I can spot a cloud. What can I see today?

A welcome cool air has been blowing over the last few days; the breeze has characterised my walks, the way it blows on my cheeks with every step and gust. I think about how much of walking is not only about what I see, but about what I feel: the temperature, the feeling of putting one step in front of another, the thoughts I have that make me smile or sing or curious or inquisitive; the feeling of joy from seeing and hearing animals – rabbits sitting still on open planes of grass, the calls of pigeons – and how they remind me I am one small part of the community that lives here.

⁂ ⁂ ⁂

My morning was warmed by a message from a friend, a video that shared a fusion of musical styles I didn’t expect to find myself listening to. I thought about how much I love sending messages to people of things they find interesting, and how much I enjoy receiving them, too; tiny gifts that brighten a moment.

⁂ ⁂ ⁂

Sitting here hours later under the silvery sky of evening, I think back to the morning – the cool breeze under the shade of fully-grown trees, the warm sun, the music – and smile. And I look around to evening, and how the green tees look compared to the white sky above; how the clouds look lighter than light. I look forward to whether I will hear the call of a pigeon this evening – Oh! one just started to call! – and what colour the sunset will be. The amber of yesterday? Or the gold of today?
