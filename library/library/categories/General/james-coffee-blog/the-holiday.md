**Emily**: Alice, Dave, remember not to book any meetings on the 21st. It’s a bank holiday.  
**Dave**: I thought it was the 31st?  
**Emily**: We have one on the 31st too.  
**Dave**: Another one?  
**Alice**: What’s the one on the 21st for?  
**Emily**: It says here, ”May Bank Holiday”.  
**Alice**: One day we’ll start giving our bank holidays proper names.  
**Emily**: I’m glad we’re getting the day off. It’s International Tea Day. I have to celebrate.  
**Dave**: International tea day?  
**Emily**: I’m planning a big party. You’re all invited!  
**Alice**: Aw, thanks! I wasn’t sure what to do with the day. Dave, are you free?  
**Dave**: I suppose.  
**Emily**: Great! We’re having a spread. Cakes, sandwiches, tea – all in honour of the day.  
**Dave**: You’re very excited about this?  
**Emily**: Well, I’m hoping to build excitement for days like this in pursuit of one of my life-long goals.  
**Dave**: Which is?  
**Emily**: I’d like the 1st of October to be a bank holiday. It’s international coffee day. Surely banks need to celebrate coffee too?  
**Alice**: You could start a petition!  
**Dave**: What are the chances of that succeeding?  
**Alice**: Well, on some years, “Spring bank holiday” happens on a weekend, and can be moved to the 29th of May. That’s the same day as National Biscuit Day. Coincidence? I think not.
