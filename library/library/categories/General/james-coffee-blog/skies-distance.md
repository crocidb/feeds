My words flow from the blank, full, blue sky that rests above – a sky in which the more I look, the more texture I see; there are ripples and layers and colours; the periphery of individual clouds emerge. *The more I look, the more I see.*

When I am in art galleries, I see something new the more I look at a painting. I might notice a particular detail that stands out, or a colour that I didn’t see on my first impression. With so much detail in an individual painting, it feels like there is always more to see. And there are questions to which I may never know the answer. How did the artist feel when they were painting? What were they thinking? What was their world like? What did they have for dinner? *What boundaries exist between the work and the world?*

⁂ ⁂ ⁂

I have been carrying the weight of concepts and themes that I want to think about and perhaps to write about – concepts like change and home and space and distance. I feel ideas related to these concepts come up every week. This week, I realised how much I love living in the countryside: a thought that continues along the lines of months of thinking, catalysed and captured so well by an extended family member who, several weeks ago, after only a few minutes of conversation, encapsulated how much I love the countryside.

The blank, full sky represents how I feel about change: I feel myself changing, but I am still figuring out how to characterise the changes I am going through. Perhaps I feel not like I am changing, but I am growing. I am two months away from completing my first year in university. I am seeing and feeling how much joy I find in learning in areas entirely new to me. I am becoming more acquainted with how change is part of life and it’s okay – that life changes but, in the words of Thomas Rhett, “I wouldn't change it for the world”. I first heard his song *Life Changes* years ago but never has it had so much meaning as it has today, years after the era in which I mostly listened to country music, as I sit under the sky of evening and look out to the hills and wonder about the world.

I find myself wondering about the distant impact of creative works – how Thoreau, Monet, Cézanne, Turner, and more all wrote works that, while I am not looking at or reading them right now, still have an impact; how Monet’s Water Lillies make me reflect on peace, how Cézanne’s hills make me think of whether there are any areas of the French countryside that have hills like the ones here; how Turner’s towering painted train stays with me as I think about the world.

I look to the sky in the evening and see colour, the faint orange from the setting sun peeking through the clouds that have characterised the sky for so long. I think about how far away those clouds are, and yet how close they are – their presence and impact transcend distance. The clouds move and change, bringing with them new colours and shade and blankness and clarity, and therein, the essence of change.
