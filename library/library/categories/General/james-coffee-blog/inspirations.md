I remember the moment I was standing before four or five of Monet’s Haystack paintings, each depicting a different season. I was in awe of the colour. I could move between each of them and see the same scene from a new perspective, from the warm sunsets of summer to the frosted grasses of winter. This is one of many moments, in retrospect, where I fell in love with Impressionism.

This morning I was thinking about inspirations for and what has had an influence on my writing. What influences how I write? The first answer that came to mind was Impressionist art. There may be some recency bias in this answer since I just wrote about Turner’s Sunrise which had a haziness that reminds me of Impressionism, but, on reflection, I see significant influences. Impressionists like Monet helped me realise the joy of capturing a moment in time, and of how much there is to see in a single place. Monet turned one haystack into several vivid images. Each one tells a story – of a specific point in time – and, in aggregate, the paintings tell another story: of changes between the seasons.

Lately I have been thinking about how I write “en plain air”. I didn’t explicitly start writing outdoors because I learned about the concept from Impressionism, but the term “en plain air” has more resonance now that I am thinking about it in the context of writing. When I am out and about I like to capture moments and write them down, just as some Impressionists worked outdoors to paint scenes onto the canvas.

Art galleries, more broadly, inspire me. The spaces make me think of architecture and design. The artworks within each tell their own stories. There are several art galleries I have visited many times, in part because they are so big but also because I know that I am going to see something new no matter how many times I visit.

Nature painting makes me reflect on how much I love the countryside, Medieval art unravels an era with which I am less familiar, modern art encourages me to sit with abstraction – every painting feels like a window into a new world. Art makes me think about movement and speed and time and eras through history and colour and stories and life.

Outside of the art world, mew rising has been influenced by translated Japanese fiction. The “Before the coffee gets cold” series was my entryway into Japanese fiction. The series, set in a small, cosy cafe, is a collection of short stories of people travelling through time. I love how a seemingly everyday place – a coffee shop – can be transformed to be home to so many worlds of stories. Other Japanese fiction books like Convenience Store Woman and The Great Passage similarly introduced me to the idea that everyday places – a supermarket, an office where dictionaries are made – can be transformed into an engaging story.

Also from the world of literature, Walden has inspired me. I didn’t start writing about Nature specifically because I had read Walden <sup class="footnote-reference" id="f-1"><a href="https://jamesg.blog/longform-feed#1">1</a></sup> but, over the years, I have found myself increasingly fascinated by the Natural world, by all there is to see when I look at flowers and plants and trees and hills.

Scotland is full of Nature, and so there is always plenty for me to study and see. All I need to do is look out the window and see something new, like the fog that lingers on part of the horizon, covering some trees, as I write this blog post. Indeed, the Natural world here in Scotland – and all of my experiences living here – are abundant sources of inspiration.

In terms of poetry, the most prominent influence in my mind is Dickinson. I haven’t studied too many poets in depth, neither have I studied Dickinson beyond reading a few poems; indeed, most of my exposure to poetry is from single poems and collections of poetry on a theme. Dickinson, famous for her use of punctuation, encouraged me to be more free with my use of language and syntax and punctuation. I don’t necessarily use dashes like Dickinson, but I do hold in my heart that language is a place where I can experiment – where I can try new things.

I would be remiss if I didn’t cite the web as a source of inspiration, too. The web gives me a place to share my writing, and to read the works of others. Before this piece, I [read a blog post about Cubist poetry](https://dversepoets.com/2016/09/29/meeting-the-bar-as-a-cubist-poet/), which I found when trying to find an answer to the question “if there can be Impressionist writing, could there be intersections between writing and other artistic genres?” The web satisfied my curiosity and got me thinking. Indeed, I have been inspired to write based on many things that people have written on the web; the Blaugust challenge encouraged me to write more in August, for instance. Friends’ blog posts, too, sometimes encourage me to start writing something.

I am sure that there are endless sources of inspiration I could cite. This post doesn’t list all of them: travelling, monostich poetry, Scottish writers, essays that stick in my mind, and more. With that said, the sources I have mentioned above are what first came to mind when I considered what may have had an influence on how I write. In some cases, the inspiration is more direct: a painting can inspire me to write about the painting. In other cases, the influence is more subtle: Walden is a testament to the value and joy of Nature writing.

If you like to write, who inspires you and why? I would love to read others’ inspirations and influences.
