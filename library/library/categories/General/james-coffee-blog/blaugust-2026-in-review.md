I am sitting with a cup of tea by my side as I have been on many mornings this month. This morning, my tea cup, instead of being precariously balanced on the arm of the chair, rests between the right arm of the chair and my right leg, keeping me warm. I love being cosy.

In “[Countryside; Blaugust](https://jamesg.blog/2026/08/01/countryside-blaugust)”, I noted how much seeing my friends participate in [Blaugust](https://nerdgirlthoughts.game.blog/2026/07/15/blaugust-2026-is-coming/) encouraged me to participate. This year, the minimum requirement to participate was to write five posts in the month. I didn’t write down a specific commitment, but in my mind I had a private goal of trying to write every day. I didn’t want to write this down in case something came up that made it harder. I was able to achieve this goal. I technically published a blog post every day except for last Tuesday, when I saw Lorde perform <sup class="footnote-reference" id="f-1"><a href="https://jamesg.blog/longform-feed#1">1</a></sup>. On that day, I wrote most of the observations that, on the next day, I developed into my [Summer Sessions post](https://jamesg.blog/2026/08/26/edinburgh-summer-sessions).

My contributions to Blaugust embodied a mix of genres. At the start of the month, I thought about trying to write about Nature every day, the goal being to have a log of writing that represented how I saw Nature this August that I could look back on. With that said, my writing naturally gravitated toward lots of different genres. I followed this rather than my goal to write about Nature every day because, as long as I was writing and content with what I was writing, I felt good. This month, I wrote about [art](https://jamesg.blog/2026/08/24/art-technology-change), [music](https://jamesg.blog/2026/08/17/im-old-fashioned), [organising](https://jamesg.blog/2026/08/26/how-i-organise-my-bookshelf), [code](https://jamesg.blog/2026/08/21/html-element-letter-counts), [HTML Day](https://jamesg.blog/2026/08/08/html-day-2026), [Nature](https://jamesg.blog/2026/08/30/early-autumn), and more. I wrote a tiny bit of [fiction](https://jamesg.blog/2026/08/14/edgar) and [poetry](https://jamesg.blog/2026/08/11/how-the-poem) too.

I thoroughly enjoyed the secondary Blaugust goal of trying to write posts that involve collaborating with others. This month I did art and music exchanges with Thamara ([art](https://jamesg.blog/2026/08/30/boat-bridge), [music](https://jamesg.blog/2026/08/17/plainsong)) and V.H. ([art](https://jamesg.blog/2026/08/18/the-hay-wain-by-john-constable), [music](https://jamesg.blog/2026/08/28/lost-in-yesterday-satisfied-mind)), where we each chose pieces of art and music for each other to write about. I did a [music exchange with Ruben](https://jamesg.blog/2026/08/17/im-old-fashioned), too, and an [art exchange with Thomas](https://jamesg.blog/2026/08/19/the-tower-of-babel-painting). I also did a [title swap with Zachary](https://jamesg.blog/2026/08/20/happy-day-of-the-week), where we gave the other titles for a blog post that we had to write (tailored to our own interests), and a [blog post swap](https://jamesg.blog/2026/08/06/i-thought-it-was-a-tire) where we each wrote a piece for each other’s website. Indeed, the spirit of writing with others is what made this Blaugust special. I definitely plan to do more writing with friends in the future.

With that said, this month was challenging. Blaugust is supposed to be a challenge so this is by definition, but certainly there were days when I was pushing myself to try and come up with a topic to write about. Trying to come up with new ideas every day and develop them was hard, and I was starting to feel a bit less motivated toward the end of the month. Since this academic year is starting to wind down I had more free time to work on blog posts so there wasn’t as much pressure, but certainly I did feel under pressure to write at points. I had other stressors that made it harder to write on some days too. I could have “saved” posts to publish on future days, but I had the personal goal of writing something every day.

This month made me think how I do write every day, but I don’t publish everything I write. On almost every day I write notes. I may only write a few sentences, but they are writing nonetheless – thoughts, observations, ideas, records of my days. These notes often grow into blog posts, but sometimes they don’t: not all ideas are compelling to enough to develop further. This feels like part of the practice for me though. Sometimes things stand out that I want to write about; other days, I need more time to think.

⁂ ⁂ ⁂

One of the highlights of Blaugust this year was not only writing more, but also being able to read what others wrote. It was a delight being able to read more from writers whose writing I enjoy reading. In particular, I read a lot from [Frances](https://francescrossley.com/), [Juhis](https://hamatti.org/), [Jedda](https://notes.jeddacp.com/), [Tantek](https://tantek.com/), [Naty](https://burgeonlab.com), [Thomas](https://vanderwal.net/), and more.

I have lost track of how many blog posts I have read this month, and across how many sites. With that said, I do feel like I spent more time reading blogs this month. On that note, I finally wrote down my thoughts on [reading as participation in the indie web](https://jamesg.blog/2026/08/27/reading-as-participation-in-the-indie-web) this month. Reading others’ work was definitely an important part of how I participated in Blaugust this year.

⁂ ⁂ ⁂

Today is the last day of Blaugust. I am looking forward to taking a little bit of a break, even if that break is only the breathing space that comes from realising I can take a day off. With that said, Blaugust 2026 was a delightful experience. I am sincerely thankful to [Mark Temple](https://aggronaut.com/) (rest in peace) for starting Blaugust. I am thankful too for all people who helped organise this year's event, and to everyone who wrote a post on their website in the spirit of Blaugust.
