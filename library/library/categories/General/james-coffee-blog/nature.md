*Breathe in. Breathe out.* Standing in the fresh warm summer’s air, I found my breath slowing, and deepening. I wandered as I transitioned from the feeling of indoors to the Nature of outdoors. Trees and bushes and the light sounds of Nature accompany me as I put one foot forward in front of another.

The fresh smell of Nature in the air permeates the air on the quiet country path. I hear a calm rustle in the leaves. A bird is quietly walking through the grass, in search of food. Families are out together, enjoying the summer day; conversation and birdsong fills the middle distance.

I stop and look down a long path. I wonder about the nature of Nature. What is Nature, the thing with which I have spent my life to varying degrees of distance, that which I see every time I look outward? Is Nature a force? But what about when it is still, like today, when the breeze barely moves the trees? I think back to my philosophical studies. How would Socrates approach this?

I think about how long it takes us to find the questions we want to ask – to know what to ask. I see so many questions in Nature – some potentially unanswerable, but it is from the question that I can begin to imagine.

I keep walking, letting the question accompany me until a lone butterfly fluttering over the grey path catches my eye. I find shade, shielding me, temporarily, from the heat. I take another deep breath. I notice a black beetle on the country path, which reminds me of late evening walks in the countryside as a child.

A children’s book with a taped spine is left at the silent bus stop. The book could be thirty years old, or more – oh! how stories last through the ages. Did someone leave the book here for the next person waiting for the bus? Or did they forget it? I think about all the stories we can’t understand in full. *How much can we understand Nature?*

A loud tractor passes by. *Farmers will be busy.* Walking a bit further, I then stop for a moment at the foot of another long path. I look forward and around. I think about how observations are a stepping stone to understanding – what I see helps me understand. Maybe I will ponder this on another walk; an idle thought that may take years for me to fully appreciate.

Going further, I see the lightest blue flowers I have seen. Orange flowers are in the periphery, too. I take a breath and feel life. People on bicycles quietly ride down village paths. Nature is ahead and behind; Nature is within and around.
