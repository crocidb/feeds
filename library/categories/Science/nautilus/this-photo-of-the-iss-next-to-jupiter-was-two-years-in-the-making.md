Part of the art and science of astrophotography is figuring out when certain flying objects in the sky are going to line up for a photo opportunity. 

And few photographs demonstrate it better than [AJ Smadi](https://www.instagram.com/aj.smadi/?hl=en)’s shot of the International Space Station (ISS) hurtling in front of Jupiter. There’s an incredible symmetry in the dimensions here: Jupiter is about 1.3 million times bigger than the ISS, but it’s also around 1.3 million times further away.
