I’ve been bemusedly reading and watching several iPhone Duo reviews over the past day or so (besides [John’s piece](https://daringfireball.net/2026/10/the_iphone_duo?utm_campaign=unsolicited_traffic&utm_medium=web&utm_source=taoofmac.com), [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1015028/iphone-duo-review?utm_campaign=unsolicited_traffic&utm_medium=web&utm_source=taoofmac.com) has a [series of excellent pieces](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1015580/heres-some-of-my-favorite-iphone-duo-reviews?utm_campaign=unsolicited_traffic&utm_medium=web&utm_source=taoofmac.com) and a [thorough video review](https://www.youtube.com/watch?utm_campaign=unsolicited_traffic&utm_medium=web&utm_source=taoofmac.com&v=g5gptFkgBlI)), and the consensus seems to be that it’s a polished, chunky prototype that hasn’t really figured out what the user experience should be. Being a [Mr. Mobile](https://www.youtube.com/watch?utm_campaign=unsolicited_traffic&utm_medium=web&utm_source=taoofmac.com&v=P5_GBTMAbFI) fan and watching his videos on Android foldables for years now, it comes as no surprise. But it also will come as no surprise that two years down the road this thing will have solved most of its teething issues–except the price, of course.

And I do find it amusing that we’ve managed to get to a point where a premium phone actually makes you want to use it *less* as a phone. I, personally, count that as a good thing.
