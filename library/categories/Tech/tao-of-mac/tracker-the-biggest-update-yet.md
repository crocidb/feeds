Although I never had the good fortune to test one of Polyend’s products (not for lack of wanting), I can’t help but applaud this–the popularity of the [FM-1](/space/reviews/2026/10/11/2100#the-m-vave-fm-1) and its openness is certainly having an impact, and Polyend is unusually well positioned to make a similar move.

The [firmware release](https://github.com/polyend/tracker-ce?utm_campaign=unsolicited_traffic&utm_medium=web&utm_source=taoofmac.com) covers the original Tracker as well as the Tracker+ and Mini, and the original even gets SD card sample streaming. There are SDKs for Sample Editor effects and, on the newer models, synth engines–which gives people a way to add their own sounds and tools to hardware they already own. A documented recovery procedure should also make experimenting with firmware a little less nerve-racking.

There is a licensing catch: the firmware is *source-available*, under PolyForm Noncommercial with explicit permission to make commercial music, and some synth and DSP libraries are still proprietary binaries. The effects SDK is MIT-licensed, though, so people can sell their own plugins. I’d have preferred fewer restrictions, but giving owners the code and tools to keep improving their instruments is very welcome.
