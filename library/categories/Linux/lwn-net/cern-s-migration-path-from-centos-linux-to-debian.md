The European Laboratory for Particle Physics, usually just called [CERN](https://home.cern/), is not only the [birthplace of the World Wide Web](https://home.cern/science/computing/the-birth-of-the-web/), it is home to the [Large Hadron Collider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_Hadron_Collider) (LHC), the world's largest and highest-energy [particle accelerator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particle_accelerator). As such, its computing environment is both truly unique and of great interest to people outside of CERN who hope to find lessons applicable to their own computing needs. The upcoming migration of some of CERN's systems from CentOS Linux to Debian, which was the topic of a talk at the recent [MiniDebConf Winterthur 2026](https://ch2026.mini.debconf.org/), is of particular interest.
