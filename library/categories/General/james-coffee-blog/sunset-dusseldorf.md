The moment when the sun crosses the horizon feels like it should last longer than it does. Within a few minutes, the sun has moved out of view; the red glow — a moment of transition (is all of life a moment, or moments, of transition?) — tells us where the sun once was. The glow: an ephemeral impression of the day. A world of energy will dance into the contemplative rhythm of dusk; not before using every last semblance of energy: to seize the day.

It was the kind of sunset that was so beautiful I forgot I was still wearing my jacket and carrying my backpack; the power of the sun.

⁂⁂⁂

When you look at the evening sky, sometimes you see a star. There is a joy in looking upward and seeing a star — of seeing light shining with a brilliance that carries millions of miles.

⁂⁂⁂

Some of the planes look like slow rockets heading to the stars at sunset — the way the pink lives on in the trails.

At sunset, I realise I want to live with the colour for as long as possible; to be with the pinks and the reds and the blues. There are enough colours and gradients to fill a lifetime.

⁂⁂⁂

I feel the disconcerting melancholy that comes with a good thing coming to an end. But a new day comes — the yellow dominates the darkness at dawn. The world can be seen again once more from a perspective so wonderful that each day feels uniquely special.

And then, fireworks — greens and reds and whites — made the sky sparkle with fluorescence. I wish I could share this moment with someone. I can: with the world of words; with you, dear reader.

Just like that the sparks have stopped, but the beauty of fireworks is in their ephermality — of creating a short moment of colourful delight.
