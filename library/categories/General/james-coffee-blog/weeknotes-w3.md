Today I got to see one of my favourite places in the world – a park I really like – in its full autumn colours. The paths were lined with autumn trees and scattered with yellows and oranges and reds. I heard crisp sounds with almost every step on certain straights of the walk. *I forgot how beautiful the trees get here.* The colours get more beautiful by the day, even though the trees are more sparse.

For the first time this autumn, I thought to myself that it will only be a few weeks before the oranges and reds will be mostly gone. I will have to make the most of the next few weeks and soak myself in the colours of the season for as long as I can.

The highlights
----------

After last weekend’s storm had me inside for the weekend, I was especially excited to get out this weekend. I went for a long walk – one of my longest this year – and enjoyed the weather. I was surprised by how warm it was, though; I was expecting a chillier air. I went to places I haven’t visited in weeks, visited a new bookshop (where I bought a poetry zine with a layout that caught my eye), and generally appreciated all the time outside, breathing in the fresh air of day.

Yesterday, I spent some time in a coffee shop and started journaling. I am still not in the mood for reading on the weekends, but I was in the mood for writing. I let my stream of consciousness flood onto the pages, writing about all that came to mind while I looked around and watched the world go by. I haven’t written as much prose by hand in quite some time, even though the sum of my writing was less than a page in my writing journal. I wonder if I will write more on paper in the coming weeks. I did enjoy the experience.

In terms of studying, I got back my final grades for last year. I am pleased with how I did. Admittedly I am a bit nervous for my first art history assignment this year, but I am sure I will get through it. This is the first time I have felt nervous about an assignment weeks before it is due, and before we have even been fully introduced to it. I have a lecture about the topic of the assignment tomorrow, on Monday, in which I expect to take many notes. I am already feeling more confident by the day.

In the creative writing section of my studies. I submitted my assignment for the first block last week, marking the end of our block on defining ideas. I am already in love with the next module, fiction writing. Next week I will be starting to study character building, for which I am excited. I have found my confidence in fiction writing has come on leaps and bounds this year, owing, I think, in large part to my studies. We also had a lecture on poetry that I really enjoyed, studying Hayden’s [Full Moon](https://www.afropoets.net/roberthayden2.html).

On that note, this is [Writing Month](https://writingmonth.org/). I am not sure whether I will participate, but this morning I had a burst of creative inspiration and ended up writing a few paragraphs toward a story. I am unsure whether what I wrote has a strong enough foundation to be a longer work – and whether the plot went in the way I want – but it did give me more confidence. I will make a decision about Writing Month by the end of the day. I like that you can set your own goal rather than committing to a longer project such as the 50,000 word total that NaNoWriMo formerly set.

Thoughts, wandering
----------

* After a Homebrew Website Club meetup this week, I worked on a feature in [Artemis](https://artemis.jamesg.blog) that lets users opt in to see boosts from an ActivityPub account. Previously Artemis only showed posts originally authored by the ActivityPub user, but now there is a checkbox to opt in to see boosts in the settings for a subscription. My implementation shows the title of a post to be “Shared a post by @accountname”, rather than showing the actual post. This was inspired by [Colin](https://pointinthecloud.com/) who has a similar implementation in their web reader. I much prefer this minimal approach to showing materials from an account I do not directly follow in my reader.
* A little snippet from this morning: *“The joy of the first sip of coffee — of how that may beget words, words that beget more words for there is rhythm to be found after starting. I am listening to electronic music. Despite having few words I am starting to appreciate the music for its own qualities — for the repetition, for the moments of change, for the pitches that I seldom hear. There is joy to be found in so much music. I started my morning thinking about ambitions and potential adventures — where I want to go, what I want to try.”*
* I opened a box of chocolates that I received as a gift a while ago. I love chocolate. Especially the ones in the box that is open now.
* I am hoping to “lock in” to my fiction writing next week. I am really excited to see what I learn. I have a feeling that I am going to go through the phase of writing stories that aren’t *great* while I find my feet, fiction being so new. With that said, yesterday I did a writing exercise and I ended up writing way more than I expected. I found something of a flow state that I feel with other kinds of writing that I enjoy. That was a real delight to feel.
* I am starting to get used to the darker nights again. It was nice to see the stars so early last night. There was a forecast that indicated the Northern Lights might be visible later in the night, but I didn’t see them. One of my dreams is to see the Northern Lights. I really hope to see them one day.
* I have had this idea of “building bridges” in my mind related to the indie web for several weeks now. I don’t really know what I want to say, but I think the message is how I wish the indie web was more interconnected with other interest groups. My zine “[A Creative’s Guide to Personal Websites](https://jamesg.blog/2026/09/05/creative-web-guide-zine)” is an experiment in communicating the value of websites to creative people (artists, photographers, painters – anyone who thinks of themselves as creative) and how to get one set up while trying to avoid as much technical language as possible. If the words “building bridges” mean anything to you as it relates to the indie web, do let me know. I am still trying to piece this idea together.
* I want to make another zine in the next few weeks!
* I had a note that is almost a year old nearby with three questions I thought I’d put here: “How did Michelangelo feel when he painted the Sistine Chapel?”, “Has anyone painted HTML code?”, and “Is reading a creative art?”. I don’t plan to actively search for answers to these questions, but I thought I’d give them space here both for future reference and in case they are interesting to you, dear reader.

Miscellanea
----------

* I read [Ralph Waldo Emerson’s *Nature*](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/29433/29433-h/29433-h.htm) for the first time. I think I will have to re-read the work before I would feel comfortable sharing any thoughts, but I certainly thought this was worth reading. I have known Emerson’s name for a long while as he was friends with Thoreau, so it was good to finally read something written by him.
* I love Frances’ link-roundups, and was overjoyed when I saw a few of my recent posts linked in the [last edition](https://francescrossley.com/links-fun-projects-joy-hope-community-and-more/).
* I love the idea of showing a count of how many images have alt text as detailed in the article “[Number go up](https://medienbaecker.com/articles/number-go-up)”. This is one case where I am in firm support of “number go up.”
* I was listening to the episode of the *In Our Time* podcast on Logic earlier this week. I think it was in that podcast I heard the term “[syncategorematic term](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syncategorematic_term)”. The term was so full of character I knew I had to bookmark it.
* I love the [Why Cheap Art manifesto](https://breadandpuppet.org/cheap-art/why-cheap-art-manifesto) by Bread and Puppet.
* The 3D virtual museum linked in “[Virtual museum for my wife's artworks](https://dostoynikov.bearblog.dev/virtual-museum-for-my-wifes-artworks/)” is *unbelievable.* I have never experienced a museum on the web like I have that one.
* The “[Historical www-talk archives made complete](https://www.w3.org/blog/2026/historical-www-talk-archives-made-complete/)” publication by the W3C is worth perusing. The “[Guido van Rossum writes a web robot that "tries to map out the entire web", distributed along with python 0.9.8](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/www-talk/1993Apr/0035.html)” link caught my attention in particular.
* I haven’t read this piece in full, but I bookmarked “['It's offensively cheap': How solar power is quietly becoming bigger than nuclear](https://www.bbc.co.uk/future/article/20261023-how-solar-became-bigger-than-nuclear)” so I could link to it. Quoting from the piece: “By 2030, the IEA thinks solar power generation will have reached double that of 2025's levels, with solar overtaking hydro to become the world's third-largest electricity source.”
