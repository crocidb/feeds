Rain falling from the sky, I looked downward to the concrete path. The concrete became a solid, watery mirror, reflecting back thoughts and questions. *How can I romanticise while staying grounded?*

I appreciate the rain: falling water after a dry season. When something is gone for a while you feel its impact more; when it comes back, you remember what you love(d). I felt that the two – romanticising and groundedness – could co-exist. Life is a place of both potential and reality, of dreaming and truth.

I am watched over by a Romantic whose presence, developed through words and time, flow with the centuries – whose words created a place, places. My feet stop. I stop. It is almost as if my question came from a silent conversation with the statue and the rain and the ground all at once. The beauty of the falling water contrasted with the staid grey concrete, bridged by the knowledge that here stands a writer, converged to make the moment. *This place means something to me.* I feel the solidness of the concrete and see the fluidity of the emerging puddles and hold on the weight of story.

On this ground I have wandered, waited, and wondered. Anchors to my last few years lie here – of holding conversations that want to last longer, of perpetually seeing the old anew; of growing. The story of the grass, I have learned, changes as people come and go. The story of the sky, too, is ever-changing; Nature – ourselves included – interconnects. Life is bound to movement. I change.

⁂ ⁂ ⁂

Near where I stood, on a day since passed, I could have stopped. It would be one of my last chances to say something. Would a few words have made a difference? *What could have been?*

I carry the rainfall just as I feel the worn concrete of “what ifs”, and, at the same time, revel in the blue skies from the intervening periods. As much as I want to stay still, the rain keeps me moving – there is only so much I want to feel before I seek a dry place. This is where I now find myself.

Rain may exist, but so too do clear skies, and the possibility for something to start serendipitously, like a raindrop falling from a sky once dry or a wave from a friend you never expected to see.
