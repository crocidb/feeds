When I started to study art history, I had a feeling that I may no longer spend as much time reading about and experimenting with technology. This has turned out to be true in many senses: I have spent countless hours reading textbooks and literary texts, and writing essays. I forgot how much I enjoyed writing essays.

This year I have spent a lot of time thinking about art. I have spent many weekends in art galleries, a kind of building that has a magnetism comparable to that I used to feel writing code. Except unlike software, art makes me stop and look and listen. When I study art, I am not trying to make anything. I am following my curiosities. I am letting my imagination run wild. I enjoy coming up with questions about what I see and sitting with them, without the obligation of trying to immediately find an answer. Art fills part of my life that I didn’t realise needed filling. I look at canvases like I do stars: with the greatest awe and curiosity that I know.

With that said, I still do read about and experiment with technology. I spend a lot of my free time talking about the web, running meet-ups about the web, and contributing to the web both on this website and in communal places, like the IndieWeb wiki. Earlier this morning, I was thinking about how I am now more of a hobbyist: I love making web pages, but technology doesn’t take up most of my time anymore. I am no longer striving to start many new projects. My favourite technical projects this year have been the ones that are single files on the web that I can use and/or others can see and read, or scripts I have written just for myself. <sup class="footnote-reference" id="f-1"><a href="https://jamesg.blog/longform-feed#1">1</a></sup>

Walt Whitman said “[(I am large, I contain multitudes.)](https://poets.org/poem/song-myself-51)”. I feel this deeply. Technology – more specifically, the web <sup class="footnote-reference" id="f-2"><a href="https://jamesg.blog/longform-feed#2">2</a></sup> – and studying and appreciating and understanding art co-exist as passions for me. Writing, too, co-exists with these passions. When I am on the web, I can exercise practical skills: designing web pages and information architectures, thinking about form and connections, learning and applying syntax. When I am with art, I am building practical skills of visual analysis and communication, but much of my time right now is spent on learning and admiring and thinking. I increasingly write about art to apply what I see: to delicately lift a handful of the stream of thoughts I have in art galleries and put them on the page, for others. When I am writing, I have the ever-fascinating challenge of trying to communicate ideas, tell stories, and, in the case of fiction, build worlds. I love all of these things.

This year I have been challenging myself more as a writer, both in school and here, on this website. When I think about where I want to grow the most, I want to explore more kinds of writing, to improve my introspective writing skills, and to read more from other writers to help me expand my understanding of what is possible with words. This is a change from the past: whereas in prior years I may have thought about technology as a place where I want to build my skills, writing and understanding art are where I want to spend my time now. Is this what change feels like?

I do want to spend more time exploring new creative pursuits. How does one decorate a room? How do I make a zine? How do I make the space around me more ergonomic? Should I start sending postcards to friends? What else can I make with my hands? I want to read more, too, although this can be a challenge. When I am excited about a practical project I often prioritise that over reading; university textbooks have comprised most of my reading this year.

I don’t know whether I expected my change from spending most of my time with technology to much of my time with art to be a moment when the latter mostly faded away. I’m glad it didn’t: I still love the web, and I think about the web a lot in terms of writing and publishing and, in some cases, art, too. Except, lately I have felt a bit of unease. Should I be spending this much time with technology? (But I love the web!) Should I be spending more time with art? Should I be trying to find new, more artistic hobbies? Are there music spaces I should join? The best answers I have to these questions right now, I think, is that these things will come with time. In the interim, however, if you can suggest an artistic hobby, please do let me know. I love making things.

This week I was chatting with a friend about planning to write up a bug that I had identified and fixed in a piece of software. The bug had been quite stressful to find and fix. They asked me if writing about it would bring me joy like my nature writing or more creative works. I replied that I still have fun writing about technology, but don’t do it as often now <sup class="footnote-reference" id="f-3"><a href="https://jamesg.blog/longform-feed#3">3</a></sup>. Admittedly, however, my more poetic writing is among my favourite to write, and if I think back to what pieces of writing I remember most from this year, the two that immediately come to mind are both poetic or introspective.

This question stood out to me because it is the first time in recent memory I recall someone asking a question that made me consider whether something I have been doing for a long time still brings me joy. This question made me pause and think about whether something that I would usually love still would make me happy, something I had not really been doing myself. Technology is always going to be a part of my life – maybe at some point more of a memory though? <sup class="footnote-reference" id="f-4"><a href="https://jamesg.blog/longform-feed#4">4</a></sup> (Note: I always want to have a website.)

I feel like at this time in my life I am growing in new directions, ones that make me want to turn my website home page into a poem, where I get excited about art books, where I continue to look around and see and appreciate colour and mornings and scrapbooking and poetry and and everything in between, and analyse music videos, and play guitar and sing as passionately as I can, and strive to write about those things because they bring me so much joy.

<sup class="footnote-definition-label">1</sup>

Last week I took on a bit more coding than usual and, toward the end of the week, I felt a bit deflated. I had done too much. I had pushed myself too far. My weekend touching grass – and spending time with books and art – made all the difference. I ran into an interesting point of tension where I was curious about how to simplify my site setup, but I became completely overwhelmed. I resolved that it would be best to stick with the system I have, as imperfect as it is, even though I feel I could improve some pieces. Admittedly, as much as I am interested in code, I am no longer as interested as I once was. I don't want to maintain a hundred different things. I want to focus on my website and my words, and the occasional other project.

<sup class="footnote-definition-label">2</sup>

In future pieces, I may try to refine my use of “technology,” but for now I’m using “technology” more generally. Most of my interest in technology right now is in interface design principles, culture, and the web.

How can I find the time to do everything I want to do? I think my answer to this is “I only have limited time. I need to choose to do the things I love.” And that means I can’t do everything I once did. And that’s okay. I’m finding new interests.
