Where there were leaves, there are now networks of branches. Occasional leaves are still attached in the most sheltered of areas; evergreens, meanwhile, hold onto their foliage and pines.

A squirrel runs down an angled branch between two trees. Tiny birds fly between branches, making where they land bounce as their feet find their grounding in the air. The winter light brings the forest to life. Nearby, crystal-topped blades of grass twinkle in the ever-moving light; ephemeral beads of white crystal create a frosty show made by the environment of Winter. Only in winter may diamonds be so light that they sit atop a blade of grass, not weighing down the grass, but, rather, making the grass more full. Earlier this week I saw water top tiny strands of moss, too; Nature's dance with the environment, ever-visible.

Salt-strewn paths make a crunch as I walk along concrete paths made safe, my eye looking around for any frosty fragments that I should avoid. I walk with care, noticing and feeling change. The air is chilly, not chilly enough to see my breath, but enough to wake me up in a way that only winter can, where, counterintuitively, the phenomenon of frost awakens while it freezes. I stop and look around, noticing a bed of browning leaves among whom are shades of yellow accentuated by the winter light; the bed of the last season illuminated by the next.

I love winter days like this, where the brilliant blue sky invites a sense of awe, where the fruits of autumn shine bright among the grey, wintering branches; where I feel invited to walk slower and appreciate all that I see around me: the place where the birds sing the loudest, the tree that holds a branch on which a single yellow leaf remains, the cool clime that brings back memories of peaceful walks in past winters where the sky is blue, the Earth is cold, and the day is bright.
