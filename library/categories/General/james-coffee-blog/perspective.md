*The perspective of the painting; the painting of perspective; the perspective of the viewing. The contrasts of the colours between canvases. The contrasts of light between rooms.*

I have seen Turner’s Sunrise several times this summer, and every time I see something new. Today, walking in, I immediately noticed a change in pace from the outdoors. Whereas I had spent the day walking busy streets, I was now in a place where there was both space and relative silence. My mind started to slow down. I did, too.

I am fascinated by how environments, especially art galleries, influence how we behave. Does the spacing between paintings in an art gallery influence our ability to focus on an individual piece? Do we walk slower in galleries because there is so much to draw our attention and we feel like we don’t want to miss anything? Why is it so easy to spend so long in an art gallery? Why, of all places, do I almost never feel the need to check the time when I am in a gallery?

On recent visits to the painting, I thought about the fog and the haze. Today, colours stood out more. The greys and the blue greys of the painting were fascinating. I thought about the periphery of the painting, and how, over minutes and over weeks, my attention ebbs between the castle in the centre and the fog and the grass around the edges. I then thought about the background. *Is that a second hill?* I have never thought about it before. I briefly imagine the second hill as a representation of the castle in the past – now even more faint, but still present.

I then notice that there isn’t a clear demarcation between the castle and the land on which it stands. Where does the castle end and Nature begin? I then start to think more abstractly. Where does the Earth end and the sky begin? I notice how the grasses in the foreground blends into the water. The fog blends into the background. The painting is both evocative but also enigmatic. Is it evocative because it is enigmatic? There is surely more to it. The colours are what first drew me to Turner’s Sunrise.

I take two steps back and feel as I did last time: the painting became even more ethereal and enchanting. I thought about perspectives: how moving a little bit – through space, through time – can change the impression I have in the moment of a work. I wonder what I will see next week.
