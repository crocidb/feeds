Although I am Chinese by ethnicity, Chinese is my second language. I never enjoyed it at school as I found it immensely difficult to recall the thousands of characters. Not to mention needing to juggle two completely different spoken dialects and also two written forms (Traditional and Simplified Chinese)!

But the language has grown on me since the pressure of exams has long gone. Often times, I find that Chinese can do away with expressing an idea or conveying a meaning with very few characters compared to English.

Chéngyǔ (or sing4 jyu5 in Cantonese) are fixed idiomatic phrases that are usually four characters long akin to English idioms. I remember having a sing jyu dictionary back in the day; where each set phrase usually has a little backstory which can be quite amusing in its historic origins.

Note that some of these are Cantonese in origin (marked with an asterisk \*), which means it came from regions (predominantly Southern China like Guangzhou, Hong Kong, Macau) that speak the [Cantonese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantonese) dialect, and are less well known, relatively speaking.

As James is into nature, let me go through a few idioms that I like, which use nature or animals as the theme. I'll try my best to write the literal meaning in parentheses followed by the actual meaning of the phrase.

* 一日千秋 (one day thousand autumns): Change is happening quickly, one day is equivalent to a thousand years.
* 畫蛇添足 (drawing snakes with feet): To do superfluously things, which ends up inappropriate or unhelpful. Make things worse. Overdoing something.
* 對牛彈琴 (playing [string instruments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guqin) to cows): Cows won't appreciate music humans enjoy, i.e., know your audience to communicate effectively, use the right method. Can also be used to describe trying to communicate with people who do are illogical or not willing to be reasonable.
* 雞同鴨講\* (chicken talks to duck): Two people speaking different languages are unable to communicate.
* 風和日麗 (mild breeze and sun beautiful): Calm wind, sunny weather, good weather, beautiful day.
* 花好月圓 (flowers good moon round): Everything is in harmony, flowers are blooming the moon is full. Describes perfection, beauty or happiness.
* 風平浪靜 (calm winds quiet waves): Calm and tranquil, situation is stable.
* 雞飛狗走\* (chickens fly dogs run): Mass confusion and terror, chaos and people are panicking.
* 綠草如茵 (green grass like mattress): Grass as lush as a carpet, describes vibrant and soft fields of greenery
* 騎牛搵馬\* (ride an ox looking for a horse): To settle with what you have now while actively looking for something better.
