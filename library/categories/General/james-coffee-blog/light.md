The golden setting sun shines through a break in the clouds on the horizon. Clouds linger in the centre of the sky but there is blue to be seen; blue encircles one outlook where there are hills are so high they almost touch the sky, but, collectively, the hills are just low enough for me to see the blue peeking through. Amid the gold of the setting sun, the blue shines through, too.

I like to write details while I sit or stand in the place where I see those details, taking notes that I can refine later, or, using my writing, in the moment, as the beginning of a new piece, as I have done here. From observation, I start to reflect; to see in the hills and the trees and the sun the next chapter of a story that I write by day, even when I am not writing – where words distill where I find myself in a particular moment: seeing the varying intensities of light from the golden sun; typing by my keyboard what I see and feel.

I close my eyes with the sun finding enough of a gap between the clouds to shine with the radiance of a summer’s day. I am reminded of an exercise I learned in meditation where you are supposed to imagine the sun getting brighter within you, and imagine that light expanding, reaching out into the world. Therein, with my eyes closed, I ponder a spectrum of lights – from the visible to the invisible; from the seen to the felt; from the inner to the outer.

I open my eyes and shuffle so as to better see the space around me, illuminated by the glowing light; paper looks graceful in the soft light; I see the stacks of books that represent my many interests. I see an instrument and, in my mind’s voice, hear music; for music, too, is within.

Writing about the world around me, I feel situated in space. I focus on the present moment, as opposed to any other moment; the tiny details become magnified by the ephemeral. The sun will soon pass for the day; I should capture this moment now. Herein, one of many motivations for writing – if a moment is impermanent, why not write it down to both think about the moment, and to have a tiny record that says “this happened”. (There are many other reasons I write, too.) Here, on this day, at this time, the sun began to touch the hills; being immeasurable distances apart, such phenomena is illuminated because it is seen, and because Perspective exists.

What follows situation? Grounding? A recognition of home? With the sun now passing the horizon line, and covered by clouds, I feel the inner discord that was with me before I started writing, one of feeling like, sometimes, I exist between two places; that two places pull me toward home. But it is in this third place that I really started to meditate on what home means to me; here is a place that gave me distance, Perspective. *There is no being in two places at once.* I know this expression intellectually, but not emotionally.

The yellow light continues to illuminate the sky. I know that, in this moment, I am unlikely to reconcile the feeling of being in two places at once. Is there a fourth place that would light up my world more? Is it normal to feel between places because to feel the pull of one place means that you are dreaming and dreaming is what brings more light to the day? I wonder all of this while sitting down, in the chair on which I have written for so many years, enjoying the countryside of home.
