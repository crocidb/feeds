I close my eyes and I can still see the blanket of stars that I saw moments ago when I was leaning as close as I could into the glass of the window such that I could see as many stars as possible. Such is the impression that the stars made that I can see them in my mind even as I am now at my computer, writing about what I saw.

Seeing stars out the window, I knew I had to spend some time with them. I stood close by the window for some time, allowing my eyes to adjust to the darkness outside, meanwhile leaving a faint condensing impression on the cool glass with my warm breath. The sky was clear enough tonight for me to see the stars for a second night in a row.

Before I go to bed I like to look to see if I can see any stars. On a cloudy night, there are none, on which nights I remember that there will be stars soon. On a clear night I am fascinated by the stary heavens.

Lately, I have been thinking about how I might see the stars more now that the days are getting shorter. When I go to bed now the sky is darker than it was in mid-summer and the stars are out, as long as it is not cloudy. This makes me think of how the seasons impact both Day and night; as the planet moves, so too does the amount of daylight change. And, now I think about it, the seasons impact colour (as trees change) and sound (as birds migrate) and temperature and more; the impact of seasons is myriad.

I was reading [Frances post on almanacs](https://francescrossley.com/almanac/) earlier this week. It came to mind as I looked toward the stars. Am I, too, writing something of an almanac on this website, just as I am also [writing an anthology of self](https://jamesg.blog/2026/07/11/websites-as-anthologies-of-self)? My site documents the seasons as it does documents my impressions of Day and night. I am less scientific than an almanac, but it feels like the world is best when science and poetry co-exist. I cannot report on the constellations I saw, but I can say that the stars were beautiful and magnetic this evening, as they always are.
