You feel like you have joined a community in a festival line. Everyone is together, waiting for music — we all came here with a common purpose, a purpose that we can see, a purpose that we can feel. We came here because we are human. Many people are dressed up, in all colours — blues and pinks and browns and purples. Time feels amorphous — isn’t is always? — where minutes pass like seconds and seconds pass like minutes. We are all surrounded by conversation, in the tone only made possible with anticipation in the air.

We stand near an airport and yet the centre of the stage is the line — we are in our own journey, one where the only distance we need to traverse is to the front of the queue. We are a rainbow among the hills; we are a bastion of colour on a grey day; we are the music. People wear red heart glasses and hold up phones to their foreheads to play the game where you have to guess what word has been assigned to you. We always find ways to pass the time — to find fun in the seemingly banal moments.

People wear custom made t-shirts. People hold their hands up while on the phone to signal their location to newly arrived friends. Stars on cheeks and sequins on shoes. My way of passing the time is listening to music in my headphones — time is carried forward with the melody. And with the smiles in the air as groups of friends in the queue have fun.

While waiting, there is the joy of looking around and seeing how far you can see. And of hearing the occasional sound from the stage — the loud bass coming from a sound check. Every beat builds anticipation for the first act. Of realising I will soon see some of my favourite singers.

⁂ ⁂ ⁂

*We are never too far from a castle in Scotland. There was one visible a couple of hundred meters away from the stage.*

While waiting, I enjoyed seeing the guitars standing on the stage, waiting for a performer. Music is played on the speakers while we all waited for the first act – Alessi Rose. The music is fading into another song. Guitars are being tuned on stage — all the prep work required to do a show. Backing musicians are emerging. Someone has a bright pink guitar. Someone has brought a bright blue dolphin balloon. (I would later see many more of these.)

How parts of the crowd join in to sing I Want it That Way even though it isn’t an official song. People are here to connect. To sing. I am singing along too, for no particular reason other than to join the moment. Someone has a tattoo that says “Love is art” that looks like a Polaroid. *The music sings of solitary company, is that what I seek?*

Today is the first time I have been to a summer festival like this. It’s amazing how much time can pass without your realising something is a first.

⁂ ⁂ ⁂

The crowd cheers as the first artist comes in the stage. Many people have their phones out, eager to record the moment Alessi Rose comes on stage. The sound of the guitar comes first. Smoke machines are running. The music is building. I feel the bass of every note. Then, a scream as Alessi comes on stage. The background is like a stained glass window.

You can feel the silence before the bass kicks in. I can barely sing over the bass. The joy as the crowd joins in as Alessi positions the microphone toward the stage. Alessi jumps and so many others do too. I am smiling so much right now. “Edinburgh, let’s fucking go.”

It feels almost surreal to be here right now. The joy of feeling like you are making music as a member of the crowd. Alessi thanks Lorde because she wouldn’t be here without her. *There are so many people without whom I wouldn’t be here.*

⁂ ⁂ ⁂

“I encourage dancing.” — Alessi. People are waving their arms to the song. Someone is swaying the dolphin too. I am joining in by singing when I can. The joy of seeing how much Alessi’s dancing is in sync with the music. I am in a place where is is okay to sing as loud as one wants, to dance. Of being fully in the moment. Is that what euphoria feels like?

The act over, I sit in the afterglow of the music, feeling as if my eyes could close for a little moment to feel the songs once more — their echoes in my eyes and my head and my heart. It is amazing how people want to sing to the music between performances — Mr. Brightside. I find myself doing it too. Music is contagious.

Someone is carrying an Eras Tour tote bag. There is a family playing a card game in the grass while we all wait for the next act. Someone in the crowd is acting like they are a superstar, occasionally shouting to try and get others to cheer for them. A few people did – all of these serendipitous moments made the night even more special.

*We all contain so many songs, so many stories. So many colours, so much energy. We are all so connected.*

Two people just kissed, and embraced each other in such a wonderful hug. It is a joy to sit and watch the world go by here — to feel the atmosphere that a festival makes; the colour and the music and the resting and the waiting and the passion and the joy and the anticipation and the first moments, of being with and among people, of seeing so many people dance. Of feeling how words flow now because I am happy and curious about the environment around me.

⁂ ⁂ ⁂

Audrey Hobert performed next, coming on stage in a trench coat so long it covered both her legs and the ladder under which she stood as she sang her first song. Underneath the trench coat she was wearing another, smaller, trench coat.

Hobert’s performance was a delight. I sang – shouted – along to as many songs as I could. You could tell when the crowd knew a song better. “sue me” was sung particularly loud. Hobert danced with such passion and energy that it made me feel like I too could dance.

Between songs, Hobert played a little trick on us, saying “I’d love to be on that ferris wheel.” People look back. There is a brief pause. “There is no ferris wheel. Made you look.”

*I haven’t screamed like that in so long. Singing every word that I could, and humming where I couldn’t. Every song — every moment — was magical.*

Someone has a Lover heart around their eye — their outfit is all pink.

⁂ ⁂ ⁂

Zara Larsson’s concert is best summed up by the lady near me: “oh my god that was so fucking good.” *That was incredible.* I was shouting along to lyrics I don’t fully know. It was so fun and colourful.

During the performance of *Lush Life*, Larsson invited an audience member up on stage to do the dance associated with the song – there were many people around me doing the same dance, too. (Should I learn it?) The person invited up was incredible, dancing almost in perfect sync with Larsson. There was something wonderful about how someone from the crowd could so naturally perform in front of so many people. That was one of the highlights of the evening.

⁂ ⁂ ⁂

“Dear person behind me, the world is a better place with you in it.” was written in white on the dark green jumper.

Cruel Summer plays on the speakers in the break after Larsson’s performance. I immediately start singing. I am writing this while singing, so well do I know the song. Many of us are singing along. Many of us were closer to miming — maybe we are saying our voices? I feel my throat getting sore after singing to the last three artists.

*Lorde will be performing soon.*

Two groups of friends just ran to each other cheering. Maybe one of the groups was a late arrival? Two friends were dancing a dance where they each tried to tap each other’s feet while dancing — what a joy to watch, until one of the friends fell over, but they did so with such grace it was almost as if they knew it would happen.

⁂ ⁂ ⁂

The sky was darkening. It was past 9pm. The crowd lit up with excitement as Lorde came on stage, who was playing a synthesiser in the corner of the stage. The stage illuminations came. The performance began.

I didn’t realise how many of Lorde’s songs I knew until I started singing along. I shouted to all my favourites: What Was That, Ribs, Green Light, and followed along with many more. I wasn’t expecting to hear Green Light. *That Green Light, I want it. (shouted)* I loved The Louvre (the photo below was taken during the performance of The Louvre):

The last time I heard a scream like the one I heard when Girl, So Confusing came on was at the Eras Tour – the kind of collective scream that sounds like a loud white noise for a moment. Then the music takes over and everyone is singing along in unison, jumping to the beat as the song reached its peaks.

I was fascinated by Lorde’s use of technology in her performance. Lorde would look to different cameras to create an effect that played on the televisions. She was rendered as a point cloud when she was dancing at one point, which created a delightful effect. Lorde wore a jacket that beamed out red lights at one point, as if her jacket were an external, glowing heart that reflected her inner self.

*Lorde’s performance was electric: the volume, the lights, the vocals, the sounds, the dancing, the screams.*

*After Hobert’s performance, in the break between performances, a person near me got a notification that Dolly Parton had passed away. They shared the news with all their friends, and it spread around further. There was collective disbelief. It was strange to be hearing the news in a crowd of people. I’m not sure Dolly’s passing fully registered until this morning, when I reviewed all my notes from the concert, many of which made their way into this post.*

*I listened to Islands in the Stream after breakfast, and read the news in full. Rest in peace Dolly. Thank you for bringing so much music into the world for us to sing, and feel.*
