Last evening – and for the last few days – I have been feeling a little bit down. But music was with me, and helped me see through the grey clouds and look up to see a sunset unlike any other I have seen before. The way the reds and the ambers cast across the cloudy sky, and the way the light shone through the clouds onto the hills, are fixed in my memory this morning. It was a truly special sunset.

Toward the end of the evening, I listened to a song I love: Kid by The Revivalists. I first found out about the band after hearing one of their songs on a television programme. Following that point I worked my way through many of their songs and albums. Last night, Kid, along with the sunset, infused me with an energy last night I didn’t know I had. I danced around the house like nobody was watching and listened to every note, appreciating each as they came. I listened to the song over and over: the studio recording, the acoustic recording, a live performance. I was finding the energy in my heart, and, as the song says, “living for the spirit now”.

⁂ ⁂ ⁂

When I think back to my memories with music, I see a constellation as vast as the night sky. I think back to being a kid and listening to my parents’ *Grease* CD on a music player that, on reflection, I wish we still had. I think to all the times I spent listening to music by The Beatles and all the 80s songs I loved in my early-to-mid teenage years. I think back to all the indie artists whose music I started to appreciate in my later teens: Paramore and The Lumineers and MisterWives and Of Monsters and Men.

And then, in my early 20s, I started listening to Taylor Swift’s music, starting with her *Midnights* album. Years later I am still making memories with those songs. I remember sitting on the floor in Moynihan Train Hall listening to The Tortured Poets Department. I remember passing many evenings with my laptop precariously positioned on top of my electric keyboard and a video of an acoustic set from the Eras Tour playing. I remember playing and re-playing songs – practicing – and knowing that there were still many more songs that I could learn to play.

Indeed, my memories of music are not only of listening, but of playing, both privately and in public. I carry music with me wherever I go and when there is an instrument nearby I can always bring song where there was none. I have played on many public pianos: in a park on the most sunny day in a city far away, in train stations near and far. I even played a melodica on the street off Times Square in New York, a memory that I still cannot reckon with my anxious self. I hadn’t played in a year, and so I was, to say the least, a bit rusty. With that said, that particular moment – and looking back on all my times playing music in public – makes me think of how much music makes me feel more confident. I may be imperfect, but I feel better when I play music. I know it’s okay to play a wrong note as long as I keep listening to the music and pick myself back up. *Music makes me feel more like me.*

⁂ ⁂ ⁂

Despite all of the colourful memories I have of music – of listening and of playing – what excites me most is that there are so many more memories to be made. This year many of the artists I love – Paris Paloma, The Revivalists, Angus & Julia Stone, Bon Iver – released new albums, each one suffused with memories. Paris Paloma’s new album makes me reflect on the intersections of art history, my relatively newfound field of study. Bon Iver’s songs have helped me fall asleep on nights where I cannot quite find slumber. The Revivalists have made me want to dance. Angus & Julia Stone remind me of a particular moment in my life.

Last night I went to the piano and started learning songs I have never played before, revelling in the ever-new process of hearing a song and figuring out the scales and notes that form the song. I enjoyed every moment. Whenever I go to the piano it feels special, even though I have been playing since my early teens. Which reminds me, the first electric keyboard I ever owned was an X-Factor themed keyboard for kids, which an auntie purchased for me. I am still sincerely grateful for that gift. Oh how vivid and powerful musical memories are. From that moment and still until today, I have loved sitting in front of a keyboard and creating music.

[Liza’s contribution to the Carnival](https://sociation.bearblog.dev/music-memories-the-same-favorite-band/) mentioned “nearly everyone's most intense musical loves happen between ages fourteen and sixteen.” I have heard similar stories of how the music that you hear when you are young stays with you as you age. This made me think that, at least right now, I am feeling music more than ever. I am making new memories every day. I feel like I understand the role of music in my life more, too. This morning I realised that maybe it’s okay that I listen to music instead of read books on the train because music fills me with energy and life and what else is leisure time for but refilling one’s batteries and doing what we love? (Although I would still like to read more.)

This morning I woke up to a musical memory from last night, which I summarised in four words in my notes: “Dancing around the kitchen.” I wonder what memories I am going to make today: what songs will help get the darkness out my head and bring forth the lightning in my heart. To start, I want to dance around the kitchen again.
