I love how a conversation can flow from dishes to Ted Lasso <sup class="footnote-reference" id="f-1"><a href="https://jamesg.blog/longform-feed#1">1</a></sup> to stickers. This makes me think that even if I am not wandering outside – exploring the world – my mind can wander inside, or with others as part of a conversation. I digress. Of all these discussion points -- dishes, Ted Lasso, and stickers -- one subject sticks out to be the most salient (forgive the pun): the joy of stickers.

⁂ ⁂ ⁂

My morning, oddly enough, started with stickers. I was writing a response to a letter from a friend to which there were a few stickers enclosed. I keep my stickers in my scrapbook, where I collect mementos from travel and letters and life. I have pages dedicated to stickers people have sent me and ones I have picked up while traveling. Every sticker represents a memory, or a person, or a time.

This morning two childhood memories resurfaced relating to stickers. The first memory was my general love for sticker books that was accompanied by an apprehension to actually use the sticker books. I enjoyed looking at stickers, but I preferred to keep the book intact. I have vague memories of using sticker books, but stronger memories of looking at all the pages of stickers and enjoying how they were all in place.

The second memory was of my childhood wardrobe which was, in arrangement, the opposite of the sticker book. My wardrobe had a plain white colour. Looking back, perhaps childhood James knew that the wardrobe needed colour. And so I stuck stickers everywhere. I don't remember where I got the stickers, but I suspect some were from magazines, others – not many – from my sticker books, and, presumably, anywhere I could get stickers. I *loved* stickers. I still love stickers. Alas sticking all those stickers on the wardrobe meant I couldn’t keep them forever, but the art I made – the cacophony of childhood colour – is still in my heart and my mind.

This reminds me, I also used to collect stickers in my lunchbox. Some sandwich wrapping packs for packed lunches came with stickers. There was one certain brand whose stickers were particularly well regarded. I recall a few people in my primary school collecting the stickers. My collection, with time, covered more and more of the interior of my lunchbox. I am not sure how my parents cleaned it, or if they did; the stickers were too important to be moved.

Flash forward to today. If I am carrying my backpack, I am carrying stickers, usually related to both the indie web and my personal website. I never know when I will need a sticker. I might be at an event and want to give a few out. It is only today that I have realised that I have brought stickers with me on many adventures: around the city many times, when travelling back home, when visiting Germany, and on more adventures. I really do love stickers.

Do you have any memories related to stickers? I would love to read them!
