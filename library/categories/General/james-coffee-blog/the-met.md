I close my eyes and see the Great Hall. *This is where they host the Met Gala!* I can’t help but think about that when I stand and watch as tourists congregate, ask for information, and walk around to get to the exhibits they want to see. There are at least three ways you can enter the main galleries: by walking forward, past the information desk, towards the decorative arts, sculpture, and more (or, if you take the stairs, to see European Art, photography; or by going left and right, each taking you to a different era of art.

[The Met](https://www.metmuseum.org/)’s entranceway makes me think about how a building welcomes you. In the Great Hall, there are always people walking around, waiting. The space is full of life. It feels like the architecture is helping to convey the message that this is not only a place for art: it is a place where people come together to spend time; to reflect, to connect, to wander.

I started thinking about The Met after Thomas mentioned a [new exhibit at the Temple of Dendur,](https://vanderwal.net/random/entrysel.php?blog=2154) about which he blogged yesterday. It made me realise I should write something about the Met, to have a bookmark here, on my website, that says *this is a place I love.* (Also: I love my website too.)

When I last visited The Met, I remember spending at least one full day there, arriving at opening hours and leaving with the announcements that invite – well, rather, tell, politely – all guests that the galleries are now closing. I have been lost in the Met more than once. I would still get lost today. Back when I visited, I was not yet thinking about what it would be like to study art history. A year and a half or so on, I am almost through my first year in my degree (which will focus on both art history and creative writing). It is interesting how places help us see how far we have come – how much has changed in a short time.

I could list room after room in which I had fallen in love with the art – of an exhibit on the history of American photography, of the beauty of the decorative arts, of the musical instruments that spark my curiosity – but I want to share three vignettes instead.

First, of my time with the Impressionists. [Monet’s Ice Floes](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/437123), hanging on, I think, the second floor, [about which I have written before](https://jamesg.blog/2025/07/01/ice-floes), is one of my favourite paintings. I remember my time standing there with great curiosity. This painting more than any other in the room caught my eye; it is still in my heart today. I see the beauty of winter in Monet’s brushstrokes.

Second, of the sculpture room. I remember there was a drawing class or workshop going on at the time, with tables and chairs laid out in one section of the gallery. I remember a sometime sketching a statue toward the centre of the room, and others, at different times sketching statues too. I remember walking around and admiring both the architecture and the statues: I love how buildings themselves can catch one’s eye even amid impressive sculptures, and how sculptures can catch one’s eye even amid impressive architecture.

Third, of ascending the staircase up to the European art. This would often be where I started after handing over my ticket. I love to start exploring galleries with European art, and work my way through as many different areas as possible (although my studies, particularly recently, have me excited about going to see African art whenever I can). Once, early in the morning, I was even able to get a photo where there was nobody ahead of me on the stairs.

![](https://editor.jamesg.blog/content/images/2026/08/IMG_2405.jpeg)

When I visited New York last year, I went to The Met so many times that I registered for a membership, figuring it would be cheaper than having to pay the entrance fee each day (regrettably, I did this toward the end of my trip, and so I don’t think I really saved anything, but I did get a membership card in the post!). My membership has since lapsed, but I still have my membership card: a memory of one of my favourite museums, and a reminder that I would love to visit again one day. <sup class="footnote-reference" id="f-1"><a href="https://jamesg.blog/longform-feed#1">1</a></sup>

<sup class="footnote-definition-label" id="f-2">1</sup>

On that note, free admissions to our national art collections in the UK is one thing I take for granted: I can pop into our national art galleries whenever I want, without admission or ticket. There is something really special about being able to walk into a museum and immediately go toward the painting you want to see.
