Yesterday – September 13th – was the 256th day of the year. Tantek [blogged about this in 2024](https://tantek.com/2024/256/t1/happy-8bitday-binary-byte) and in 2025 introduced a challenge to [make a web page in under 256 bytes](https://tantek.com/2025/257/t1/happy-8bitday-255-bytes-max). After [reading his announcement from yesterday his morning](https://tantek.com/2026/256/t1/happy-8bitday-ordinal-date-observations), I decided I would participate again.

Last year I made a [minimal HTML home page](https://jamesg.blog/2025/09/15/a-255-byte-web-page) using 253 bytes of HTML and 220 bytes of CSS. This year I made two pages, each of which only use a single HTML file with styles in a `style` tag. The single files are each under 256 bytes.

The first page I made this year was [a poem about websites](file:///Users/james/blog/playground/8-bit-day-2026.html). I used CSS, in particular the `color-scheme: light dark;` rule, to make the colour of one of the words in the poem change depending on whether the page is viewed in light or dark mode. The page is 215 characters long.

The second page I made this year was an [interactive web page to count the number of characters and words in a textarea](https://playground.jamesg.blog/8-bit-char-count.html). This took a fair bit of time to make because I had to use JavaScript to make the textarea work. The page is 254 characters long, and uses four HTML elements: two paragraphs, a textarea, and a script tag.

To save characters in the character and word count web page, I assigned the `document` value to a variable called `d` and the `document.getElementById` function to `d.g`. This syntax when I needed to retrieve elements or their values, or set new values for an element, I could do so in many fewer characters (although at the notable cost of readability). Of note, the word count splits at space characters, which means that `of – day` would be three words (`of`, `–`, `day`), even though it is two words. If anyone can suggest a way to make a more accurate word counter while staying under the character limit, I’d love to see your versions!

The constraints of 8-Bit Day have, in both years, encouraged me to make something I otherwise would not have. I didn’t start today thinking I was going to make a character and word counter in under 256 characters of code, but I am glad the day went this way.
