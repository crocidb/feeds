*A few weeks ago, the phrase "old faithful" came to me. I thought "that would be a good name for a ship!" This evening, I decided to do something with the name. I had an idea for a direction, but the story went in a completely different direction than I thought. I ended up writing a story in a version of Scots (that at least makes sense to me).*

*In writing this story, I realised I could hear the story but didn't necessarily know how to write it down. I'm not certain on my spelling in some places, but if I read it out it makes sense (does anyone have similar experiences?). It's not the most groundbreaking piece in the world, but I do like it.*

⁂ ⁂ ⁂

“Why did you ca’ ‘er Auld Faithful?” Alex paused. “When a’ was a lad, we were telt a’ways te be faithful. In the cauldest nights and the warmest deys, haud ‘yer faith. Haud it close. I did as a’ was told, and ended up following in the fitsteps o my fither. We went oot on what he’d ca’ the “high seas” — must’a bin nae mare than a mile away frae shore, mind ye — and fished, and fished, and fished. Yin day, the weather hardened; a fierce blast, a mighty beast, came frae the East. The boat rocked side to side, tilting way past ony point ye’d ca’ safe. We thought we’d capsize. But we didnae; every time, the boat rocked back up. We held onto the ropes with the arder i’ life we had, and made it through. We christened the ship Auld Faithful when we got back tae shore.”
