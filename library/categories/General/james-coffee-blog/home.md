I remember I was on a trip a few years ago to a place I had wanted to visit for years. Toward the end of the trip, I said to someone that I “missed the poetry” of home. I had come all this way to a place I had dreamed of, had the best of times, and heard the most wonderful of stories. Yet, my heart still yearned for home: here, in Scotland.

Yesterday, I was having a discussion with someone about city and country living which brought the concept of home back to the forefront of my mind. They lived in a city and mentioned they couldn't visualise a world living in the country. In contrast, I have lived my whole life in several places in the Scottish countryside.

I like to say to people that I grew up in and still live in the countryside: the countryside is so central to who I am. I love hills and trees and valleys and villages and coffee shops nestled down stone streets and the style of houses built a hundred years ago and the long-running festivals and everything else. I may be near a city, but visiting the city feels more like a trip. Edinburgh feels like home in a way, but there is a difference between somewhere feeling like and actually being home.

I have thought many times about whether there is somewhere else I would want to live. Would I want to move to a city? Every time, though, I think two things: first, of the stress of moving (thanks anxiety!), and second of how much I would miss where I am now. I love the trees where I am and watching them change through the seasons. I love being close to family. I love watching the place I grow up improve as new things are built.

Admittedly, living out in the country is more isolating than living in a city: many of my closest friends live in cities elsewhere and we only get to see each other a few times a year; making friends in real life is harder in the countryside. Yet I still believe in the potential of meeting people serendipitously; of how one random event can lead to the best of connections. I have always been a romantic at heart. And the landscapes here are breathtaking.

I love seeing other places and bringing the memories home – of retreating back to the trees I know well and thinking about where I have been and where I might want to go. Maybe one day I will move; maybe to a city, maybe to another small place in the country. In fact, it’s likely I’ll move at some point. But, at least for now, I’m happy here: studying at university (mostly remotely, except for a few days), watching the world go by, spending time with the people I care about when I can, and dreaming about the present and the future.
