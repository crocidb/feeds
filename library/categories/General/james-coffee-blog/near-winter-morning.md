A fog heavy enough to linger but not heavy enough to create snow fall envelops the landscape; said fog blends into the grey sky. A bird soars past, having seemingly found their bearings in the beautiful, chilly morning. The wintering air is still, accentuating the form of the now-silver trees whose leaves are almost gone. The trees stand tall in the fog. Some are so tall they almost scrape the sky.

Elsewhere, a blue tear appears in the white, cloudy plane – Sky shows its layers: blue calls through rippling greys and whites, the Earth still foggy but now aware that morning has come. With time, the fog fades. Grey becomes blue becomes green – a hillside becomes more visible, the fog having fallen into the forest below. The distant landscape is still grey, but, looking far into the distance, I see the blue tear in the sky is expanding, reaching out far as if to announce to the whole valley that the sun has risen, even if it is not yet visible.

A few autumn leaves remain, their colours becoming clearer with every passing minute. A bird rests on a bare branch, surveying the rippling land below; the silver of a tree’s bark becomes ever clearer; remnants of autumn line the air as, too, do formative feelings of near-winter – the time between two seasons, each powerful enough to assume the role of Environment; in their growth, the season – and seasons – become an ever-changing lens through which to see the world.

*Heavy morning fog*  
*Focus on essentials –*  
*Day emerges.*

⁂ ⁂ ⁂

*Lighter morning fog*  
*Clarifying colours grow –*  
*Sky becomes light blue.*

*This essay is my attempt at a [haibun](https://www.poetryfoundation.org/education/glossary/haibun).*
