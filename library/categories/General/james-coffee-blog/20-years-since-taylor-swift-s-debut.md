Today marks 20 years since the release of Taylor Swift’s self-titled debut album. In recognition of the occasion, on Reddit, [*thebeast\_96* asks](https://www.reddit.com/r/TaylorSwift/comments/1xe2t3m/in_honour_of_20_years_of_taylor_swift_when_did/):

>
>
> In honour of 20 years of Taylor Swift, when did you become a fan and how has it affected your life?
>
>

It must have been late 2022 when I first started falling in love with Swift’s music. *Midnights* had just been released and several songs on the album caught my eye. Anti-Hero, Mastermind, and Bejeweled are the three songs that I remember most vividly. In the case of Anti-Hero and Bejeweled, I enjoyed not only the music but the accompanying videos: each told a story. I didn’t know what that story was at the time – and I am confident many people could describe them better than I – but there was a flicker of something. Maybe it was the melody, maybe it was the lyricism.

From there my memory is somewhat (lavender) hazy, but I know that I started listening to more and more of her music. *folklore* and *evermore* became my favourite albums, with songs I still sing on many days of the week. I carry the songs of those albums – of all albums – with me wherever I go. I have played *exile* in train stations. I have sung songs to myself while in bookstores. I have felt along with *this is me trying*.

I could quote many lyrics in many songs that *hit different*; that have a resonance and an impact that I feel either from the words themselves or from the way they are sang. *You're using nostalgia as a weapon to hurt yourself*. ⁂ *I want auroras and sad prose // I want to watch wisteria grow right over my bare feet*. ⁂ *All that time I sat alone in my tower*. I admire Swift’s poeticism and way with words, and how they resonate beyond romance. Her music introduced me to the (new) romantics.

Her music also had me spending more time at the piano. I love learning her new works, especially ones that are acoustically written and thus sound great when played only on the piano. The Eras Tour Surprise Song mashups were a boon in that regard, a selection of recordings (albeit full of applause, but then again in that applause there is joy and that matters so deeply) where two or more songs are combined and performed on guitar or piano. *I Hate It Here x The Lakes* is most likely my favourite, but there are so many.

Also, with so many of Swift’s songs being good to play on acoustic instruments, I knew where I wanted to start my guitar journey.

With all that in mind, I still haven’t directly answered the second part of the initial question: *how has [Swift’s music] affected my life?* The short answer? In so many ways.

Her music has brought out so much joy in me, and made me feel so many other things, too. Her music has encouraged me to spend more time playing musical instruments, whether it is working out the melody of *ivy* on the piano or strumming along to a tutorial of *the last great american dynasty* on guitar. Her music has led me in all sorts of directions, making me reflect, inspiring me to learn about new things, and reminding me of how loud and meaningful our voices can be.

Of the over 250 songs that Swift has either sung herself or with others, I am yet to find one to which I don’t want to sing along. Here’s to artists that awaken within us something we didn’t know was there.

💚

*P.S. Increasingly this year – and especially these last few weeks – I have been listening to songs by more and more artists: CharliXCX, The Revivalists, Bon Iver, and more. I could write stories about how all their songs make me feel, too. Maybe I will one day (and in [some cases I have](https://jamesg.blog/2026/10/05/song)), for writing about musicians feels to be one of life’s many pleasures.*
