*On the 32-Bit Cafe forum, xixxii asked “[what are your webcrafting influences?](https://discourse.32bit.cafe/t/qotw-what-are-your-webcrafting-influences/5430)”. Herein, my thoughts on the question.*

⁂ ⁂ ⁂

A few years ago I took a tour of the United Nations Headquarters in New York. I love visiting Parliament and similar buildings, and part of the trip is still surreal to me. *I got to see the General Assembly room!* On that trip I learned that the [blue in the United Nations flag represents peace](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/un-emblem-and-flag):

>
>
> The “UN blue” colour was also chosen at this time and became an integral part of the visual identity of the Organization. Blue represents peace in opposition to red, for war.
>
>

Ever since that trip, I have been thinking about blue as the colour of peace, in addition to white. When a staff member in the Lego store made a comment on my (darker) blue phone case, I said I liked it because blue is the colour of peace.

I cannot remember the chronology – whether my site redesign came before or after that trip – but I do know that when I look at my website, I still think of blue in the same light. And so, whether or not I chose the blue before or after the aforementioned trip, I do know the blue remains an important part of my website. *Blue is the colour of peace.*

In the back of my mind, I am now thinking ”How should I feel when I look up at the sky on a clear day?”

⁂ ⁂ ⁂

Community is inseparable from the design of my website. My participation in the IndieWeb community has been the source of countless ideas. Talking about my website with others has helped me figure out what I want my site to be. Listening to others’ thoughts on their websites has helped me understand what a website can be. Seeing others’ sites has helped me visualise the possibilities in the design of a website. Reading about other sites’ philosophies has made me feel less alone. *We are all in this together, making something we love that we can share with the world.*

So intertwined are others in the design of my website that it is hard to point to a single feature and say that it came from a particular discussion. Instead, being part of a wider community is what makes having a website such a delight. With that said, I do know that the typography, use of iconography, text layout on my website, poetry layout, and more have all been directly influenced by conversations with people in the wider community.

*I look forward to reading what my friends write. I love when a friend says they have read something I have written. I enjoy hearing about how others build their websites.*

⁂ ⁂ ⁂

My home page is presently a poem. I had this idea while on a bus roaming through the Scottish countryside. *What if my site home page was a poem?* I started writing. Much of what I wrote on that journey was brought into the final version of the poem, now on my home page. I thought a poem represented the topic – and name – of the site well: *with words, wonder.* While I should probably read more poetry, I think of poetry not only as a form but as a feeling; one of coalescing thoughts, of discovery, of feeling, deeply, fibres of a story. I had no poem in particular in mind when I wrote my home page, rather the poetic form, and an inclination to see what it would be like to sum up my website within.

Where my site poem differs from others, however, is that it is ever changing – the titles of my most recent posts weave themselves into the second stanza. Sometimes, especially when I write about more technical topics, the titles are relatively disjointed from the rest of the poem. Other times, especially when I write about Nature, the titles intertwine with the tone of the poem. *My website is an ever-changing poem.*

⁂ ⁂ ⁂

*I try for my website to reflect me. I am always a work in progress. I try to be whimsical. I seek the poetic. I love stories. I love making people smile.*

Seeing so many people on the web expressing themselves in their own way – colourfully, or minimally; with many images, or mainly text; with essays, or with short notes – is probably what gave me the inspiration, motivation, and confidence to craft my website into what it is today, in a journey that proceeds day after day, week after week, as I change, grow, and aim to share tiny dispatches from the place that I call home.

In terms of specific web inspirations, there are so many websites I could not begin to list them. Instead, I will say one thing that I know to be true: *If you start a website, there is always a chance that you might inspire someone.*
