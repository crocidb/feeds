In my first art history module this year, I have been studying art and how it relates to the environment. The module has made me reflect on how we, too, could be considered part of the environment; we are affected by and affect the environment – and, now I think about it, environments (plural) – around us.

Winter changes both the environment around me and how I interact with it. Frances’ recent “[Rituals for winter](https://francescrossley.com/rituals-for-winter/)” post has me thinking about how to proactively adapt. What does winter mean for me? What do I need to change? Indeed, now that the weather has dropped below zero degrees for a few days, I am walking slower in the mornings, trying my best to sense for and avoid ice. I am not travelling as far. I am opting more to stay cosy. Equally, I don’t want to close myself off too much. There are still journeys I want to take and mini-adventures I want to go on, though I acknowledge now that perhaps I will not be doing as much.

In addition to the changes outside, this week I kept up with reading, about which I will talk about later in this note. I am appreciating my tea more for the warmth that is cast from the cup. Little details are magnified. I am staying in bed a little bit longer in the mornings. My routines are changing, both as a mix of adaptation (my bed is warmer, so it is tempting to stay in longer on the weekend), and as a mix of the natural ebbs and flows of life. Indeed, not only does winter change, but life, generally, changes too.

With that in mind, what hasn’t changed is that, since the end of the week, I have been looking forward to writing this weeknote. My initial fear that I might not have anything to write about in my weeknotes has proved to be unfounded – there has always been something to say, to reflect on, to curate. This practice has become something of an anchor in my week, for which I am sincerely grateful. And, now I think about it, just as multiple things can change at once, so, too, can we have many anchors – weeknotes anchor me to the rhythm of weeks to some extent, encouraging me to think back and look forward; my alarm clock anchors me to the day; sketches, too, have been anchoring me to the day. I will talk about sketching later, too.

The highlights
----------

I have been moving onto the next block of study in the art history part of my course (the other part being creative writing). The next block focuses on “Art in the making”, and explores, by looking at a single medium each week – sculpture, painting, ceramics, and more – the different mediums used to make art, and, also, how art is made. I am really looking forward to this study. So far I have been looking at sculpture and starting with ceramics. Next week I will be learning about how ceramics are made in more detail, helping to patch a hole in my knowledge: I appreciate ceramics, but I don’t really know how they are made.

I didn’t do as much reading as I would have liked for my modules this week, but this was because I ended up doing some other reading. I finished Yellowface. What a book. It was a bit outside of what I usually read, but that push outside my comfort zone was most welcome. I also read [Magnifica Humanitas](https://www.vatican.va/content/leo-xiv/en/encyclicals/documents/20260515-magnifica-humanitas.html), Pope Leo XIV’s encyclical on “safeguarding the human person in the time of artificial intelligence.”, which I had been wanting to read for a while.

⁂ ⁂ ⁂

Today, a question came to me: given all that technology can do, what do we want to make? I don’t have an answer to this question yet in terms of what I want to make, but there was something quite exciting about asking it. One such answer is that we should make things that bring joy. A philosopher reading this could surely immediately point out limits in that assertion, but, as a personal guiding light, joy is certainly something I want to keep in mind. Equally, I am interested in limits. What do I prefer to do without technology? I love making zines by hand. I am enjoying sketching by hand. I have been taking some notes by hand. But I prefer to write longer pieces that are oriented toward publication on this website on my computer. And I love surfing the web. Technology, therefore, is part of my life, but, like everything, is a part that makes up a larger whole – just as I enter a state of flow while programming, if I want to feel awe, for example, I love to look out at Nature, or visit a gallery.

⁂ ⁂ ⁂

This week, I went to an online talk given by [Sally Gunnell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sally_Gunnell), an Olympic athlete, in a continuation of my background interest in sports and how athletes think / sports psychology. I took a few notes. These are incomplete, but are starting points:

* If you push yourself too hard, you don’t even get to the starting line.
* “Run your own race”
* Learning a mix of different skills so you don’t stretch one muscle too much.

I think all of these to some extent have broader applications. If I push myself too hard in any area, I end up feeling overwhelmed. Learning different skills means if I get bored in one place, I can go to another. Indeed, I only want to play the guitar for an hour a day at most (though guitar playing is harder in winter because the indoor environment is cooler, so I find myself playing less right now). But after that, I can write, or play piano – a mix of creative pursuits is exciting to me. On Saturday, I woke up and decided to write some code, since it was exciting in the moment. But I didn’t want to spend the whole day coding.

Music corner
----------

*Herein, a few songs and albums I have enjoyed listening to this week.*

* I have been listening to some of Illy’s music this week. He has some songs I really love. I put on a mix and it was nice to hear the songs I am more familiar with mixed in with others I have heard less or not at all.
* I listened to “Cruel World… So it Ends” by Holly Humberstone. I admit I don’t remember much about the album – so many things happen in a week, it is hard to track them – but, listening to a single song from the album as I write this sentence (To Love Somebody), I remember that I enjoyed it.
* I listened to Taylor Bickett’s “[Quarter Life Crisis](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OFzOKKeB6OY)”, which matched my energy for a bit of this week.
* [Taylor Swift performed a mashup of I Knew It, I Knew You and Our Song](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LJC7mgjp5FQ) at the Country Music Awards. Is this a hint that we are getting a Debut album re-recording? Or a new country-focused album? Or was it just an excellent performance that celebrates her recent country song (I Knew It, I Knew You) and one of the songs from her debut album (Our Song)? In any case, the mashup got me listening more to “I Knew It, I Knew You”, which has a great rhythm. I enjoyed the staging in the video, too: music videos really are their own things.
* I watched a video where [CharliXCX and her producer, Finn Keane, spoke about the production of “Camera”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oLWBUC-x20M) from her latest album. I thought the producer talked quite a bit relative to CharliXCX, but, nevertheless, it was a fascinating listen. They both spoke about how effects and distortions were added as part of the production to create the effect they both wanted. This makes me think about how different layers of music all add up to build an overall impression; every minute decision adds up to an overall piece. So, too, could you say the same about writing – every sentence I write here adds up to this weeknote.

I didn’t listen to as much new music this week, but that’s okay: I spent a lot of time reading instead. And I had *so much fun* playing my guitar. I started playing a song or two without listening to a play along track in the background. I don’t do this often because I find it so much easier to find rhythm when there is a play along to which I am listening. So, being able to make the step up to playing myself (with reference to a chord sheet) is a big one, and the next natural step for me.

*Taylor Swift’s mashup is so good that I have been listening to it since I mentioned her above. And now I’m singing along.*

Thoughts, wandering
----------

* I went to an evening lecture this week that analysed the architecture of the Natural History Museum in Oxford. The lecturer mentioned the term “meet buildings”, in the context of how we meet buildings. I *love* this idea. What are our first impressions of a building? How do those impressions change when we enter? How do we change when we enter or exit a space? How do our impressions change as we move through a building? Does a building help steer us a particular way? What captures our attention most? I also wondered whether the sheer amount of architectural detail to be appreciated in a building like the Natural History Museum might be overwhelming. Did the architects consider this? Do architects consider this today?
* As I continue to reflect on the concept of home, I wrote a quick note in my digital notebook about how maybe it is okay for a concept like home not to to be easy to write about; some things are complex.
* I thought about how we all might see websites in a different way. When a friend shared a screenshot of my site recently, the site was in light mode. Because I use the dark mode version of my website, I don’t see the light mode. While the words are the same, we were both seeing different designs. Indeed, there are many ways a site may be experienced – through a screen reader, or zoomed in or out, or in high contrast mode, or in light or dark mode. Being aware of this feels important to me as someone who likes designing technology. What other things affect how we see a website? Our mood? Whether we have been there before? How much we have visited in the past?
* “Art and the web co-exist.”
* I love being creative.
* I love Nature.
* “Trees hold all the seasons, revealed by environment — how different environments affect us.”
* For the last few days I have been [sketching a manga character every day by reference](https://jamesg.blog/2026/11/21/sketching-manga-characters). I haven’t done a sketch today as I have been a bit busy, but I hope to get back into the routine tomorrow. I have felt really proud of some of my works, even if they are imperfect. Most importantly: I have been having fun while drawing.
* Now that we are getting toward the end of the year, I am starting to think back to all I did in this year. I finished my first year of university. I am in my second year. I didn’t travel as much, but I have been able to try many creative hobbies. I am not sure whether I will write a full piece reflecting on the year, but this was on my mind this week so I thought I’d include it here.
* I have had a hankering for coffee over the last few days, so I have just ordered some beans with which to brew. “hankering” is not a word I usually use, but for some reason I have really wanted to have some coffee lately. Maybe it’s the changing weather and something in me looking for more energy to ease me into the day. Will this be a new ritual for the winter?

Miscellanea

* I have been watching “Midnight Diner” on Netflix. I accidentally started with the sixth season (which I feel like was down to a UI error, for I didn't intentionally pick the sixth season to the extent I understand) but, nevertheless, I have been enjoying it. I am now watching the fifth season. The storytelling is a bit different than what I expected. I imagined something with a bit less conflict: something cosier. But said conflicts tend to be healed or in the position of healing by the end of an episode, which I really like.
* Speaking of the unexpected, this is not the kind of thing to which I would usually link, but it feels relevant in the context of technology design: [a video on the frustrations of QR-code based menus](https://mstdn.ca/@dbattistella/117640150178794947). The video does contain some swearing, but I think it gets to the heart of something important. As the person in the video points out, we have had menus for a long time and they have worked fine, whereas there are many potential points of failure for a QR-code based menu. This isn’t to say necessarily that QR-code menus are a bad idea, so much as they need careful design. From my perspective, I definitely prefer paper menus. To the extent I can keep technology away from a table on which I am going to eat, the better.
* Via the BBC: “[Phoebe Bridgers banned phones at her gigs, so fans are using art to remember them](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cq7821d0pv49o)”. I *love* this.
* Quick boost for Zachary’s excellent “[Send (Or Make) A Get Well Card Already](https://zacharykai.net/notes/getwellcard)” post.
* I enjoyed reading “[Tactile Art](https://www.poetryfoundation.org/poetrymagazine/articles/150914/tactile-art)” by John Lee Clark. The subheading for the piece is “A DeafBlind poet on art he can touch”. The whole piece was fascinating. The description of the works designed by Robert Sirvage, “a DeafBlind architect and design consultant”, in the XI section had me rethinking what art can be. If I may pull out a particularly vivid point to attract your attention in the XI section: “What he had done was to temporarily assume the place of God, leaning over to hug the figure, which now represented Sirvage himself, his hand the Hand of God holding the handle, perhaps the hilt of a sheathed sword.”
* I enjoyed reading [Joe’s “what’s the point?” post](https://artlung.com/blog/2026/11/20/whats-the-point/), which responds to [Olu’s post which asks the same question](https://olu.online/whats-the-point/). The concluding line in Joe’s post stuck with me: “And that’s the point. That indeed is my goal, to have a worthwhile, creative life.”
* I enjoyed [Stule, a web application that lets you make digital sandwiches and take and share photos of them](https://stulle.app/). I wish there was a way to put a second slice of bread on the top though. The bread-like topping is *not* a slice of bread. It is [Leberkäse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leberkäse).
