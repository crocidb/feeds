The horizon is bathed in orange and a faint pink hue as the hills in the middle distance turn blue with the haze of the summer’s evening. Light white clouds are scattered across the sky. Looking at the clouds, I see art; looking around at the hills and fields I see art, too. I think of the colour Turner used in his paintings. How many times did he look to the sky to find what colour to use? How do I find the words to describe what I see and create an impression in your eye?

The orange glow on the horizon is most colourful in the slight incline between two hills. The colours change with every moment as the sun moves further away; with every moment we see the potential of Day, up until the moment where the moon becomes clear and stars sparkle across the sky. I remember where I saw the first star yesterday. Will it be in the same place today?

I played piano as the sun set. I saw the colours of Evening radiate through the windows but I was in the throes of Melody. I have not been playing piano as regularly since I started learning to play the guitar. There felt no better time than evening, where I could give the final vestiges of energy I have to bring music to accompany the colour.

Listening attentively to the music, I tried my best to play what I heard. I experimented with simultaneous high and low notes, octaves apart, like the contrast between Night and Day. I played the notes in between, too. For, like the musical spectrum, Day is not represented by one note but all the notes between two points – the moments between sunrise and sunset. And on some days, Day leads into Night. I find these moments happen most with friends, Melody, or with an idea in my mind.

Last night I went to bed listening to the songs I realised I wanted to play today. I couldn’t emulate all the sounds, for I know only one of the instruments I would need. I couldn’t sing all the words. I know the sounds the words I would need to sing but I know not how to sing them. Our voices take time to find. In the mean time, I study: I listen and play, spending my evenings enjoying the sunset with Colour and Melody. I play and sing, with all the passion I can find.
