Storm Boelo has been passing through Scotland for the last many hours, and high winds are expected to remain for the rest of the day. This weekend has thus been one of staying inside and staying cosy: of drinking tea (and the remainder of the coffee I had in a bag in the cupboard), making zines, and watching some television.

The winds as I write feel slightly less intense than they were in the morning, but suffice to say I am much happier – and safer – staying indoors than I would be bracing the wind.

The highlights
----------

The theme of this week has been to try new things. I have been listening to new music, getting back into anime, and writing more zines.

In terms of anime, I started watching [Catch Me at the Ballpark](https://www.crunchyroll.com/series/GVDHX85EQ/catch-me-at-the-ballpark), a show about the life of vendors and patrons that work at a Japanese baseball stadium, as well as the players. I love the silent mascot, Sun-Shiro. I love wholesome anime and manga. The question I have on my mind right now is *How does Murata feel about Ruriko?*

With regard to zines, this week I followed through on one of the ideas that has been in the back of my mind for months: to make a mini zine with my typewriter. The way I did it was to fold the mini zine first, and then put the folded zine through the typewriter roller. I am not sure if this was the most effective method (the keyboard seemed to jump and skip a letter more often) but it was fun and had me finding joy in embracing the imperfections; the typos, the winding words (literally) and more.

I also got to use the scanning function in my printer for the first time (either in a long while, or ever). What magic! I put a piece of paper into the machine, scan it, and then I have a copy that I can view on my computer and print again. I might need to play around with the settings a bit as some of the text in the copied document is a bit fainter than I would like, but suffice to say such experiments will be done with great joy.

Making zines by hand and with my typewriter and folding zines by hand has been a refreshing break from the computer. While the computer remains part of my workflow – for looking up words, for scanning, and more – I can get really focused while using my typewriter, just as I do when I am typing a blog post on my computer. With regard to looking up words, I actually dusted off a few of the reference books on my shelf. I brought out the “pocket” thesaurus (there is no way it would fit in one’s pocket) and used it to come up with words. My interactions with this book even inspired a poem.

Part of me wonders whether I should do some writing that is exclusively printed in zines and shared either with friends or by trading. And I am also thinking about new topics about which I could write a zine. How to write HTML? A HTML element reference guide? A series of haikus? A photo zine?

Speaking of creative work in the physical world, last night I had an idea. Maybe I should make a “Keep imagining :)” sign in the vein of the “Believe” sign in Ted Lasso. It might be nice to have a little motivational message somewhere close. In tandem, I have stowed away in the back of my mind the intention to purchase a print of an artwork when I go to a museum so that I can bring a bit more colour to the walls around.

In terms of studying, I finished all the textbook reading I needed to do this week, went through our materials on evaluating information, worked on my first assignment, and looked ahead to the next one to help me prepare. The first assignment is due next week. I have almost finished, but have a few things to edit before I am ready to submit the work. I will also get my final grade for my first year next week, to which I am looking forward.

Thoughts, wandering
----------

* I need to fix the search feature on my website. I think I tried to add too many features at one time when I built it a few weeks ago and, as a result, the feature isn’t working especially well. The words *start small* echo in my mind.
* I have been thinking about re-building a DVD collection over time. I purchased *The West Wing* on Amazon before I deleted my account there, and so now I am having to re-purchase episodes I want to watch on Apple TV. If I had kept my DVD boxset, I would have saved a lot…
* I am interested in how songs are written. I tried to write one this week, sort of. I wouldn’t say what I wrote was any good, but I did try, using a cadence from a line of a Bon Iver song to help give me a rhythm along which I could write words. I am not good at singing, but it was nice to try something new.
* Even though I bookmarked a lot of links to include in my weeknotes this week, I still feel like I read so much more.
* I am slowly working my way through [The Story of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Story_of_Art). It is a fascinating read.
* I have closed down rainfall [dot] scot, my website that reported how much rain fell in different areas in Scotland with comparisons across the same month in different years (e.g. see all rainfall for all March months in the last five years). I used this website, but two factors came at the same time that would mean a complete rewrite. First, the site was my only site using PHP. I turned off PHP after the announcement of the vulnerabilities this week. I upgraded PHP, but then I realised it might be better if I rewrite the software to Python, a language with which I am more familiar. I started doing the rewrite, only to realise the government open data API I was using, from [SEPA](https://rainfall.sepa.org.uk/), has updated, and the government’s website itself has updated. It still doesn’t slice the data the way I was, but the update looks good. Alas, however, I was already not too excited about a ground-up rewrite, and having to navigate a new API added to the friction of moving, so I decided to retire the project. Maybe I will bring it back one day.
* I like slowly tending to the HTML of my website, making tiny changes when I see places that I want to improve.
* I keep my personal email inbox in an open tab at all times. This week I realised that it might be adding unnecessary stress to my days. I end up spending so much time getting into the headspace to really consider and provide a thoughtful response to an email that emails stack up for a few days, or weeks. I think I am going to try to keep the tab closed and check in less regularly. Any thoughts on how to think about this are most welcome.
* My process for writing weeknotes, which, this week, involved going through many bookmarks, feels like it requires a different working mode from my other writing; here, fragmentary thoughts formed as bullet points are part of the form, and the work on the "Miscellanea" section involves a degree of curation. I don't have any further comments, but suffice to say I am happy I am embarking on this experiment.
