Walking forward, the full moon catches me aglow, before the clock has even struck four. “Look at the moon!”, I thought to myself, my attention stolen by the sky. I was not expecting the moon to be so vivid and so clear, even though I had learned that there would soon – but not today – be a supermoon.

⁂ ⁂ ⁂

The moon moves slowly until, between observations made in intervals, its journey becomes evident. The moon announces and represents night, a time of rest, yet the moon is moving across miles and, in so doing, holds, poetically, a contradiction that is perceived elsewhere in the universe: it can feel like nothing is changing in a single moment, but the world still moves. *What does it mean to change?*

A new colour burgeons on the horizon, gradienring with the fading azure sky above, where the moon sits and, further still, a deeper blue that constitutes, for this present moment, more of the sky. Just as words flow, so, too, can their subject – the sky is changing at a rate impossible to capture in words; an impression, then, becomes the goal. *Is this how the Impressionists felt?* The moon continues to move.

What would happen if we cast our biggest questions to the moon? Would the sound waves reverbarate far in distance and time? Would they make us see something new? Is any change enough to show that change is possible? Do changes make more change?
