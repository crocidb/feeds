*What does the concrete path mean to me?*

In the mirrored ground I see many meanings. Here is a place I have stopped to think. Here is a place over which I have idly walked on my way to somewhere familiar or somewhere new. Here is a vein of the city, made by all those who walk through. Here is where I feel an invigorating air where I feel at home even among the rain – a place where, no matter how many times I have been here, I see something new.

⁂ ⁂ ⁂

If, in my mind, I go back to where I stood still, I see both mirrored puddles and the past and the future. I carry with me the hope that has accompanied many of my walks; the kind of hope that borders on romanticism, a kind of romanticism I like because there is energy to be had in knowing that there is a mere possibility of something new existing in places we know well. I walk on old paths while I find new ones; while I become more comfortable with the world I know. With time, I find the confidence to step somewhere new, revealing new worlds – new thoughts, new questions. *I have never seen the city like this before. I can’t remember the last time I saw a tree this way. I didn’t know trees could be this colour. Or, I did, but I had forgotten.*

Perhaps the reason I write so much about Nature is that to better understand Nature I need to exist with its presence over time. New shades of light and seasons and people and days reveal attributes that I had previously not noticed, like the progression of green to burgundy to orange trees on a familiar path (how amazing it is that a tree can have a colour as intense as a deep red wine) that entertained me on my last walk. The path was the same, but the surroundings had changed. I was changing, with the walk, with the seasons, with the environment whose air was chilling and in which I need to rely more on myself to make warmth.

⁂ ⁂ ⁂

I walk along treaded paths because they help me get to where I want to go; the path on which I stood frozen one day is one that has taken me to worlds of art and Nature, rooms of stories, and plazas where my mind feels clearer. I love places where I can see far, where I stand in front of an incomprehensible amount of detail and let myself live in the moment, seeing what stands out. I love looking out to hills I know well and thinking about them. Yet it was only recently that I started to ask the question “what do these hills mean to me?” I started with what I know, and I keep looking, waiting to see what I might notice.

In writing, I feel the intersection of time and knowledge. These words flow into existence as I formulate them into thoughts; as I process what I have seen and felt and think about their significance. I feel the weight of questions I cannot answer fully. *What does the concrete path mean to me?* I feel the weight of questions that have been with me for a long time. *What’s next?* If nothing else, each word gives a contour to a thought – a potential foundation for something new. In writing I feel the chance to sit with a question.

Paintings and art in galleries has a similar effect on me. In looking at a painting I know well or have never seen before, questions always come to mind, questions I may not yet be able to answer, but that I can stow away. I can imagine what a world might have been like. I can think about what the world is like as I see it. I can be with time. I can feel my limits, as well as the energy that drives me forward, fuelled by the part of me that sees something and wants to think about it more, like the concrete path with the mirrored puddles on which I walked months ago and in which I still see *something*.
