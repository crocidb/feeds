*Zachary and I are trading blog post titles. Zachary has written a blog post in response to the suggested title “*[*What everyone should know about Australia*](https://zacharykai.net/notes/australia)*”. I have written my post in response to the title “Why I wish people a happy day of the week”.*

For the last year or so, whenever I have been on a call with someone, or met up with someone I know, I have often said “Hello, happy [day of week].” On the day I am writing this, for example, I said “happy Thursday!” to people in a group chat I am in. I have done this in both professional and personal contexts and, in both, I often observe it brings a smile to people’s faces.

The origin of this comes from something I started to do a while ago (update: [three years ago](https://jamesg.blog/2023/05/09/starting-the-day)!). When I would wake up, I would say “Good morning world, happy [day of week].” I said this to nobody in particular: it was addressed to the world itself. I stopped doing it for a long while, but picked it back up again this week. There is something pleasant about the first thing you say in the day being a hello to the world.

I can’t remember the exact moment when I started greeting people with the day of the week in my greeting, but I know I have been doing it for around a year. And I love greeting people in this way. If I can make someone smile, even for a little moment, I am happy.

With that said, this greeting approach comes with its limits. First, I have to be aware of the day of the week. Very occasionally I get the day wrong. This happened a few days ago. Although, using the wrong day of the week didn’t create any problems in the flow of the conversation: indeed, it led to quite a fun interaction. Second, I have to read the room I am in. If I know someone may not be having such a good day, I will say “hello” or “good morning/afternoon/evening” instead. I don’t want to remind someone of anything that’s making their day harder to the extent I can, which I might do if I say “happy [day of week].” (Of course, I can never be sure of what someone is feeling, but I am aware of this when I greet people.)

During [IndieWebCamp Nürnberg](https://jamesg.blog/2026/07/05/indiewebcamp-nurnberg-2026), several people competed with me to be the first to say “happy [day of week]”, which I think both highlighted the extent to which this greeting seemed to stand out from the rest, and also added even more energy to the start of the day. There’s nothing like waking up to a message that says “happy [day of week]” that was sent several minutes after midnight, someone having stayed up too end it, or reading a message sent minutes after you have woken up saying “happy [day of week].” The little things in life – the way we greet, the way we say hello – make a big difference.

During the [Homebrew Website in Nürnberg that week](https://jamesg.blog/2026/07/05/a-week-in-nurnberg#homebrew-website-club), I added a “Happy [day of week]!” message on my home page, where the day of the week is dynamically substituted with JavaScript (and falls back to “Hello!” if JavaScript is not enabled). I kept this message when I redesigned my home page to take the form of a poem. Every time I see this feature I feel a little moment of joy. I hope you do, too!

And with that: I hope you have a wonderful day.
