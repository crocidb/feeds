Preparation is everything. I started my walk by pulling my hands over the sleeves of my jumper that I was wearing under my coat. If my hands were under my sleeves, I would have two layers of protection from the cold. Gloves seem to make my hands feel colder, whereas my sleeves hold the vestiges of radiated warmth that keep me more comfortable. With my coat sleeves extending over my hands, I was ready for my walk.

The shade foreshadowed winter: the air was cold, but not quite cold enough to see my breath. I walked at pace, paying less attention to the immediate surroundings and more on finding my stride – finding warmth – that would propel me through the walk. Acclimatising (to the extent one can in the cold), the sparse trees started to catch my attention, as well as the remaining golden leaves from the autumn season. When does winter begin, I wonder. Has it begun already, with fierce cold air during day and delicate frost lay over night?

I notice my shoulders are tense on this walk. I am tensing my muscles as if doing so will help me hold onto heat. Relief comes when I make my way out of the shade and into the sunlight, where golden warmth shines on my face and I start to gain heat. I have found my stride. I look up and around to appreciate the trees – the twisting red colourful ends on the top of one tree’s branches, the silvering bark that shines where leaves once fluttered in the air – which warms my heart. *It’s a beautiful day.*

Back home, I have the heating on for the first time this season. I am already thinking about gloves and layers and days spent at home reading and drinking tea, the latter dreaming about reading (books aside from textbooks) being more of a hope because I read in cycles and it can take me quite a while to get started with reading in my free time. Nevertheless, I have a few books and mangas I would like to read this winter – words that carry warmth and knowledge that will carry me through the cooling season outdoors.
