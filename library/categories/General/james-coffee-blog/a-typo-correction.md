Two [^3] people reached out to me to point out an error in my “[Wonders of Web Weaving, Behind the Scenes](https://jamesg.blog/2026/10/27/www-bts)” blog post, for which I am sincerely grateful (to those two people, I definitely owe you a coffee or a cookie or a similar delicious treat!). The original introduction concluded with:

>
>
> It has been the greatest pleasure to not have terrific conversations with thoughtful web weavers, and to bring those to you every week.
>
>

You might be able to see the error. My original sentence construction was going to be “not only have terrific conversations […], but also to bring those to you […]”. I made an edit to the sentence and didn’t double-check the construction. The sentence, now corrected, reads:

>
>
> It has been the greatest pleasure not only to have terrific conversations with thoughtful web weavers, but to bring those to you every week.
>
>

First: thanks to the people who pointed this out! Second, I am making this a separate blog post because: (i) the typo is significant enough to call out; (ii) I wasn’t able to fix the typo for several hours, and; (iii) weirdly, I feel comfortable highlighting this error.

I was thinking earlier about how this year I have been getting better at receiving feedback. I have never had a reaction to typo feedback – which I always sincerely appreciate – but I used to be quite afraid of getting feedback and not knowing how to process it. Feedback would feel like I did something wrong, rather than that I had a basis and I could build from it.

University has made me a lot more comfortable with receiving, and giving, feedback. I am sure I have much more to learn. This, I think, will come with time.

*P.S. If you ever see an error like this in my writing, please do not hesitate to email me!*
