I was listening to a [cover of Taylor Swift’s Babylon](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/YMgt4TjUy4U) and, almost immediately, something other than the melody stood out: the resonance of the notes. *This sounds familiar.* The recording reminded me of the sound my grandparents’ piano made when I would play it as a kid. I sat and thought about it and realised my grandparents’ piano may have been the first “proper” piano – not an electric keyboard – that I had played.

The piano was brown and stays in the hallway. It was seldom in tune, although it was more in tune when I was a kid than it is now. All I knew when I was young was that the instrument was playable, and that I could sit down to play it. Just as you can’t play a saxophone quietly, so too could you not play the piano quietly. The sound of the notes that I played must have echoed throughout the house. I cannot recall everything I played, but I vaguely remember at some point wanting to know the Sound of Silence.

The stool at the piano folded open. This is where books of sheet music were kept. I remember referencing one or two but I generally preferred to play songs I knew as opposed to reading them from sheet music. This method of play stuck. I revel in learning songs by ear. I love the challenge of figuring out what notes to play. It wasn’t always this way though: for a while in my teens I used tutorial videos to help me play, but, ultimately, I gravitated toward playing by ear, each performance different from the last – each performance new.

Aside from my grandparents’ piano, there were many pianos to play in high school. This would have been a few years later than when I was playing on my grandparents’ piano when I visited. Most of the school pianos were electric; two were acoustic. I can still picture all of the classrooms vividly: the rows of electric keyboards, the guitars on the wall, the teachers’ pianos. There was an acoustic piano in the hallway that I sometimes played. I remember playing Counting Stars by One Republic. And, separately, in class, I remember vaguely being told off for practicing playing the songs I loved to play when we had assigned music to learn.

Hearing the aforementioned cover of Babylon brought me back to sitting on the stool full of music, playing notes, and looking left to my granny in the kitchen to the left. It is amazing what memories a sound – not even the individual notes, but the way they make sound – can bring back.
