Elbow grease. Baking soda and water. *Care.*

The cup from which I drink most often, my [IndieWeb mug](https://jamesg.blog/assets/pages/mugs/iwc.jpeg), has been accruing stains at the joint that connects the interior walls to the bottom of the cup for several months. These stains are left by the occasional drop of tea that is left at the bottom of a mug. I wash the mug every two days or so, but my washing has not been thorough enough to get to the hard-to-reach rim at the bottom of the cup. This week, however, I resolved to finally clean the cup. At least, to the best extent possible.

I tried cleaning with a scourer, which rubbed away some surface level stains. I researched how best to clean and learned baking soda and water helped. I let the bottom of the cup steep in a small amount of a mix of baking soda and water for an hour, then emptied the solution and scrubbed again. Between a few sessions, most of the stains on the bottom of the cup came off. There are a few small dots of tea stain left to address, but that can come another day.

I have wanted to write about this all week. I wasn’t particularly sure what the story was – what is interesting about cleaning tea cups? When I started writing, I think I found the answer for which I was looking: this was an act of care. I got to make a tiny thing a little bit better. Since I cleaned the cup I have been thinking about how much cleaner it is. And, importantly, I didn’t decide it was time to use a new cup, or to purchase a replacement. I wanted to keep the story going for the one I had, a cup full of memories, a cup I have been using for years, especially this year.
