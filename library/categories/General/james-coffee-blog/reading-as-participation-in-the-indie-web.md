In the past, I have used the shorthand “if you have a website, you’re in the indie web community.” I have used this because I don’t think there are, or should be, any other qualifying factors (i.e. using particular tools, having a certain site layout). While I think this shorthand is useful in ensuring people don’t feel like they have to do things a certain way to participate in the indie web, it misses one constituency that I don’t think are talked about enough: readers.

Before someone starts a personal website, they will probably read, or at least encounter, personal websites. Every personal website someone finds is a chance for them to see what it means to have one’s own website. Every personal website that someone sees has the potential to evoke the thought “maybe I could have a website too?” That is to say reading – or at least exploration – precedes making.

While the indie web community is welcoming to anyone who is on the journey toward starting a website, my shorthand fails to encompass this. You can still participate in the indie web community even if you are not making a website, by having the intent to get started or, to go further, the inclination to ask questions about what it means to have a website.

As I think more, perhaps the best articulation is that reading, for someone new to the indie web, is a precursor to participating. This perhaps gets me closer to my goal: to open the aperture of what participating in the community means. I am still fascinated by ways of participating that are not necessarily starting one's own website. What comes to mind first is joining discussions about websites. Asking questions or listening in to discussions is a valuable part of participating in the community, even if you aren't actively working on a website. (Although the intent or at least curiosity to have one is presumably a prerequisite.)

From a different angle, as someone with a website, reading forms an important part of how I engage in the indie web community. If I didn’t write on my site for a week, I’d still count my reading others’ works as participating in the community. Indeed, I don’t want to only publish my own ideas. I want to engage with others’ ideas. I want to read stories that I may otherwise not have encountered. And when I get the chance to chat with someone who has a site, if I have read one of their pieces I will mention it. It always feels good to know someone has read something you have written, and even better if they have taken an interest in the topic, or been inspired to make something by what you have written.

I wanted to write this to advance a discussion I would love to see happen around reading as not only a valid, but essential form of participation in the indie web community. If you have thoughts on this topic, I would love to hear them.
