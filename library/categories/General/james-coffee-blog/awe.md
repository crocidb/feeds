Two people slightly in front of me, to my left, crane their heads to the sky, following the one-hundred-and-eighty-year-old structure to our right. They slowed down as they walked. *Does awe have a pace?* I walk past the monument often, a reminder of the literary history of the city, and of Scotland – dedicated to Sir. Walter Scott. Was this the first time the couple had seen the statue? What a special moment that must be.

As the couple slowed, I found myself slowing down, too, looking around to see so many people around. I think about all the people who have their photo taken here, of all the musicians whose notes have graced the air of this place, of how many memories must be made here every day. Seeing two people so interested in a building that I too love, I couldn’t help but smile at the joy of witnessing someone else in what looked like a state of awe.

⁂ ⁂ ⁂

I later found myself looking up to the sky in the same park, thinking of how tall the Scott Monument is, and of how impossibly beautiful the clouds are – clouds that span the sky through which the sunlight shines. I too was in awe. Shortly thereafter came the inevitable moment when my gaze turned from the clouds to the world immediately around me, my mind moving from the beauty of the skies to the beauty of public gardens where people can wander and rest and take up space on a sunny day.

Connected through space and seeing different things, at least three people, I think, felt awe in Princes Street Gardens today. I wonder how many more people did.

*How brilliant it is that Edinburgh has at least three art galleries and a monument to a famous writer in its centre. I love that art and words and beautiful architecture -- all capable of creating awe -- are so central, in the heart of the city.*
