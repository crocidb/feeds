When I opened the window this morning, I was greeted by a white and grey disk, the grey blending into the light blue of the surrounding skies. Clear as Day, and full, the morning moon made me feel a little bit of awe. *Wow.* When I went to bed last night, the moonlight cast beams of silver into the house. I didn’t expect to see the moon again so soon, on this cool, still autumn morning where increasingly sun-soaked branches and leaves sway delicately. In front of where I sit I see the yellowing leaves of autumn atop a tall tree. That tree, by co-incidence and the wonder of human perception and imagination, almost appears to point to the white orb that sits stoic in the sky. The moon is so still it almost looks like it will never move again. But I know that, by tonight, the moon will have travelled far – like us all – and rise to create once again its peaceful silver glow.

*To the west, the intensity of the sun rising across blue skies and through light white clouds increases, marking the beginning of a new day, the moon both bringing back memories of last eve and leaving me to ponder the beauty of the sun and moon together, decorating the sky on this new day.*

I look back up to the aforementioned autumn branches and notice the moon has moved relative to said branches. We are forever in movement, discernible especially with a point of reference. The leaves helped me track the movement of the moon; the colours and temperatures help me track the progression of the season.
