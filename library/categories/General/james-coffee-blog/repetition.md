I repeat the same, imperfect walk on most days. I have to walk on the road for a little bit, there being no path, but it’s quiet enough not to be a problem. The trees there are beautiful, anyway – radiant with such colours that I couldn’t imagine not going on that walk tomorrow. Leaves are falling still in the frosty air; at the bases of trunks there are puddles of colour that once dotted the sky as green, yellow, and red stars. I want to be with the trees as they change, and to see what new life there is in winter.

On my walk today I heard a cacophony of bird song, as loud as I have heard such music. I looked up to the tree above, bare with leaves but coated with sound. There must have been a dozen birds across several branches, each making their mark on the place. In other seasons I seldom notice the birds. Today, I have looked to the trees at least thrice and been greeted by avian presences that look over the land. I wonder if the birds have a favourite path to fly, favourite tree, a favourite spot.

I repeat the same, imperfect walk on most days, a walk etched in my heart. I looked ahead yesterday and saw a hill in a new light; to the left was a bright blue sky with the faint, silky white coat that seems to be ingrained in the November sky at a certain time of day, and to the right was a green hill covered in trees. I have seen the hill dozens – perhaps hundreds – of times, yet yesterday it caught my attention and almost stopped me in my tracks. I forget how the seasons change the light.

I keep coming back to the seasons in my writing, and I only have so many unique words I can use. But writing with the seasons is what I want to do right now. The trees outside look anew in the morning fog, and the clear early afternoon light, and the early evening when the sun peeks through the clouds and I wonder whether I should turn on the living room light when I play guitar to myself and, by product of the time, the setting sun, at 4pm – a ritual made in repetition. My music is accompanied by ever-changing lights. I come at the same time; the sun changes.

I change too. I have been playing many of the same songs this year on my guitar, but this time last year I played no songs – in the repetition there is practice; there is the realisation that I am continuing to make music; the whole-body experience of playing music; of leaning into song; of trying your best to project a note in the way that you want; in a way that matches your feeling. Practice begins somewhere, and continues with repetition. On some days, I play with ease but with great passion. On other days, the music subsumes me, and it feels like the song is an essential part of processing the day – of processing life.

In repetition, I feel the periphery – the edges of what I know and what I don’t: of the notes I know and those I am yet to learn; of remembering that I can identify few birds, and so I have to, when writing about them, convey their presence to the best of my ability without naming them; of realising that I can’t yet fully describe why the walk I do every day means so much to me. Is it because of the repetition, of the routine I have ingrained with time? Is it because it goes so close to one of my childhood homes, one so distant in the past I don’t remember it? Is it because of the beautiful colours that change with the day? *Is there meditation in repetition?*

*What will I learn with the next repetition? What will I see?*
