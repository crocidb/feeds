*This is my contribution to the* [*September 2026 IndieWeb Fiction Carnival*](https://jamesg.blog/2026/09/01/iwfc-moonlight) *on the theme of "Moonlight".*

“Fields glisten under the moonlight.” This is what John used to say whenever anybody asked why he liked to walk so late at night. “You can see way more stars if you are outside than if you look out a window.” John spoke with a passion for the peace of night like nobody else in the village. Only one other person in the world knew exactly what he meant.

When others would be talking about the upcoming fair and the paper shop being closed for a week as the owners were away on holiday, John would be thinking of his late-night walks. He thought of Polaris and Libra and Orion’s belt. All that time John spent studying encyclopaedias when he was young and astronomy books with his friend in adulthood were well spent: he could name any constellation in the sky.

John’s walks were peaceful, the loudest sound being the rustling of trees on a windy evening, or the sound of the occasional sheep “baa”ing from a nearby farm. But one night, as clear a night as any John could remember, there was a sound. *Clink*. It was unlike any other sound he had heard at night – a noise like something you would hear in a workshop. John scanned all around, seeing nothing that might make a sound. But, there was something out of the ordinary: a white block in a nearby field that seemed to glisten with the moonlight appeared. It wasn’t there yesterday. *What is that?*

John meandered toward the white block. He always walks slow at midnight, for there are stars to see. Stargazing teaches you to slow down, to stay in one place and admire every little detail. But this time John’s gaze was not up above but ahead, trying to decipher what the block is that he sees as he draws ever closer. He keeps walking, his shoes slightly wet from the mildew that forms in late autumn. *Is that… marble?* “That must be what the shining is!” John said aloud, to nobody other than himself, the blades of grass and the quiet owl perched on the only tree in the field.

In no time, John was standing up close to the tall, rectangular, marble cuboid. It was about the size of him. The soft glow of the cold moon reflected off the block and onto John. He felt a chill. *What is this doing here?* Surely Lake or Andrea or Bill or any of the other people in town would have mentioned if they knew something like this was going to be put in Dennis’ old field. John must have been the first to discover it. Studying every edge, there was no sign of who may have left the block – no signature, no notice, no letter.

Night fell and John went back home. The latest page in his stargazing journal, usually full of observations from a night of watching the stars, was left blank. He had forgotten all about it, occupied instead by the mystery of the marble block. Thinking harder didn’t get John any closer to a resolution. But he did have an idea: he would go back tomorrow night and see if the block was still there.

A day passed and the block was indeed still there, but this time the block was no longer a perfect, tall, rectangular cuboid. There were chunks missing. Small pieces of marble lay on the ground. “Hello?” said John, this time heard by no-one – the owl that usually perched on the tree in the field was gone. If marble could listen, however, maybe John did have an audience. John opened up his astronomer’s notebook to a clean page and wrote, in block capitals, “MARBLE DIARY”. He resolved to write down everything he noticed as time passed.

The days in the following week seemed like they lasted forever, but the nights went by fast. Every evening, John walked to the same field and diligently wrote records into his MARBLE DIARY, with the same precision with which he recorded the stars.

> November 2nd: Top right corner hammered out. Large chunks of marble on ground. Dust remains.  
>   
> November 3rd: Top left corner hammered out. Chunks and dust remain.  
>   
> November 4th: Top left corner on other side hammered out. Chunks and dust remain.  
>   
> November 5th: Top right corner on other side hammered out. Chunks and dust remain.  
>   
> November 6th: The centre of the top of the block is rounded off, as if chiselled.  
>   
> November 7th: The front is hammered out.

The evenings grew colder, although John was used to the chilly weather. Two decades of stargazing teach you how many layers to wear in each month of the year.

On November 10th, the block started to take shape.

> November 10th: The front of the block is smooth. Could there be a face emerging? It is as if this statue is destined to be a person.

The story of the marble was unravelling before John’s eyes. The statue was a person, evolving day by day, appearing through the marble that was being chiselled by someone whose identity was unknown to John. He had tried to ask some of the people in the village about the marble block, but nobody knew what he was talking about. “In Dennis’ old field? I walk past there every day. I would have seen a huge chunk of marble. Maybe the farm has finally sold and new tenants are moving in? That would be exciting!” said Andrea. “Nah, there’s nothing there.” said Bill, dismissively. Lake was even less helpful. “Maybe all those late nights are finally catching up with you. Get some proper rest.”

Over the next month, an arm, and then another arm, and then a torso started to take shape. All the details started crude at first, but, with time, became more refined. The large chunks of marble that had been hammered off in early November were no longer dotted around the base of the statue. Traces of their presence remained as the nearby grass was dyed a chalky white, and henceforth shone under the moonlight with a wonderful glow. The statue was actively being worked on, but John had no idea who was doing it. He didn’t have time to check during the day to see if there was anyone sculpting – going home to check on a statue was not an excuse his boss would accept. And so instead he wondered. Maybe the farm had been sold and the new owners were sculptors?

On December 28th, everything changed. “Scott, is that you?” Scott was John’s best friend. He had passed away a year earlier, after a long battle with an illness that gradually made it more difficult for him to go stargazing. Scott had been on John’s mind all month. He knew the one year anniversary of Scott’s passing was coming up. He had never forgotten the moment when Bill and Dennis came to his door, looking lost, their faces filled with sorrow. It was 7:02pm on January the 4th. “John, we have some news.” The following weeks were the worst John could remember.

John and Scott both went stargazing every night. It was their hobby. They both loved the stars, and read avidly so that they could better understand what they saw every night. Scott’s bookshelves were filled with almanacs that spanned two decades, as well as his notebooks reporting what he and John observed. Scott was always the scribe; John preferred to announce what he saw, and do measurements for Scott to note down. When Scott passed away, John took on the notebooks and committed to becoming the record-keeper. It was his way of honouring his best friend and all that they had seen.

On January 4th, the white marble shavings on the grass had disappeared. The grass was green again. The statue was complete.

The left hand of the man whose brusque appearance so accurately represented Scott held within it a star. The right hand held a white envelope. On the front of the envelope was one word, written in a script that Scott learned when he took calligraphy classes. John never understood why Scott was happy to miss their stargazing sessions every second Thursday to attend those classes, but all the effort seemed to make sense now – Scott had learned a craft. “John”, read the front of the letter, within which there was a short note.

> Thank you for walking with me, and for keeping up with the note-taking. Your friend — Scott.

In the distance, four lights brighter than the moon shone on the grass, casting a long, white glow. Voices emerged. It was Bill, Lake, and Andrea. “Hi Scott, fancy seeing you here,” said Andrea. You could see everyone's breath as they came closer. Thus was the weather in January. Seeing his friends out so late at night – all who are usually tucked up in bed at this hour – left John looking distant, confused.

Andrea saw John’s expression and started talking. “You must be wondering whey we’re here. You see, Scott’s will contained specific instructions on what to do if he passed away. He wanted to say thank you for all the nights you spent with him. He asked for a statue to be made. A sculptor has been working here during the day. We asked them to leave before 4pm so you wouldn’t see them.“ Bill interjected, saying “Scott said he wanted this to remain a mystery because he thought it might remind you of the stars. Every night you got to see something – the statue – with fresh eyes.” “Scott left the letter that we put in the hands of the statue with him in his will. The solicitor said it was the most touching thing he had seen.”

John started crying for the first time since Scott passed away. “He touched us all,” said Lake. Now he can be with you whenever you gaze toward the stars. “Oh, and one last thing,” said Andrea. “This field is now owned by the village. Scott’s will said that the land should the land become available it should be purchased and given to the community. So you can come here every night and know that the statue will be safe. We’ll all help out to keep it clean.” Tears continued to well up and fell down both of John’s pink cheeks, cold from the January air. A tearful “Thank you” was all he could say for a moment. Then, he looked up at the stars and found a smile. “Thank you for walking with me, Scott,” he said aloud, and everyone came together for a hug.
