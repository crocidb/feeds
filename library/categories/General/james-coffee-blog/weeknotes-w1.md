Many of my pieces of writing, especially my creative non-fiction and Nature pieces, describe a single moment or the events that happened over the course of a day. With that in mind, one of the skills I want to develop in my writing is to consider longer periods of time in my writing – to tell stories that expand over greater lengths.

During the [Homebrew Website Club Asia/Pacific meetup this week](https://indieweb.org/events/2026-10-14-hwc-asia-pacific) – open to all, hosted at a time that works well in the Asia/Pacific timezone by [Naty](https://burgeonlab.com) – the topic of weeknotes came up. This got me thinking. Could weeknotes be a way for me to think about a longer time horizon in my writing? Shortly thereafter, I decided I wanted to try weeknotes again, this time committing to writing one week note each week for four weeks, ideally on a Sunday evening.

I suspect my weeknotes will be oriented more around bullet points than longer prose, but, in any case, I am coming at weeknotes afresh ([I have tried to author weeknotes twice](https://jamesg.blog/weeknotes), but on my last try I ended up spending more time making a custom page layout than writing, which may have made the practice less sustainable). I am keen to see what I write and share!

The highlights
----------

*Herein, events of the week.*

This was a busy week in terms of studying. I am continuing to work my way through the “art and the environment” block that I am studying. This week I spent a lot of time reading the textbook. I especially enjoyed learning about the Frost Fairs in London. Part of our work was focused on what we can learn about European attitudes to cold climates by analysing both art and prints from the London Frost Fairs as well as art and documents pertaining to the Arctic.

We did a “virtual gallery visit” where one of our lecturers presented photos of and historical background about artefacts from the Uffizi Gallery in Florence, discussing them in the context of our module. This was my second time attending such a visit, and I got a lot out of it. I didn't realise the extent of the excess wealth the Medici family had.

This was a busy week for events, too. I hosted a Homebrew Website Club (HWC) on Wednesday ([notes](https://indieweb.org/events/2026-10-14-hwc-europe)), a HWC Writing discussion on Saturday ([notes](https://indieweb.org/events/2026-10-17-hwc-writing)), and the 32-Bit Café Web Weaving Workshop today, as well as helping out with a non-technical event in a different community. Reflecting on these events in aggregate, they all have their own identity: I ask different opening questions in each, discussions flow in different ways. Indeed, when I am hosting an event I have to be “on” as much as possible; even if I take a few moments to find a link or write notes, I still need to be attentive to the wider room, thinking about how to make sure discussion continues between several participants. *Perhaps I should write something about hosting online events. Reader, what would you like to know?*

I had one day this week where I had *four* events I wanted to go to that were scheduled for the same three hour window: [Front End Study Hall](https://indieweb.org/Front_End_Study_Hall), the aforementioned virtual gallery visit, another university event, and a writing club event. It was hard to choose, but I decided to attend the first 30 mins of Front End Study Hall, which started before all the other events, then the full gallery visit. This worked out well: I had a great evening, and I didn't end up pushing myself to try and be in too many places at once.

This afternoon I watched the last game in the NFL London series on the television. Stroud’s passes were amazing. The Jaguars had me on the edge of my seat many times due to flags (a problem in their game in London last week too). I missed the final minute, at which point the game was equal. I just read the final score and the [Texans won 34-31](https://www.skysports.com/nfl/news/13600610/nfl-london-games-2026-houston-texans-beat-jacksonville-jaguars-after-kaimi-fairbairns-walk-off-field-goal). I am not sure how many more games I will watch this season – the London games are on at such a convenient time – but I do want to learn more about how to “follow” a sport without watching the games. Any suggestions would be sincerely appreciated.

Music corner
----------

*Herein, music I have enjoyed listening to this week.*

* Courtney Barnett’s music is amazing. Thank you [Thamara](https://thamara.co.uk) for the recommendation. I have listened to almost two whole album after learning about her music yesterday. Barnett is one of many Australian singers whose music I really appreciate, others being Hilltop Hoods, Montaigne, and Angus & Julia Stone.
* Taylor Swift released two new versions of Patient Zero: the acoustic and piano versions. Of the two, the piano version is my favourite. I shall have to listen to it again after finishing this post.
* I am on the lookout for hip-hop / rap bands, ideally that are more words-focused in their songs (and don’t sing too fast). If you have any recommendations, send me an email!
* I listened to a few songs by Sigur Rós this week whose music I loved. Any more recommendations along these lines would be sincerely appreciated!

Thoughts, wandering
----------

*Herein, wondering thoughts as-yet not explicitly included in my writing.*

* How titles change a work.
* “Reading the texts of time.”
* I haven’t been doing many technical changes to my site lately. I haven’t had much inspiration for the coding side of my site of late. I feel both satisfied with the design of my website and also feeling like I want to make something new. Maybe a [new page in my playground](https://playground.jamesg.blog)?
* I am interested in why bookshops are so fascinating to me even when I know I don’t want to buy any books because I have some to read already. I’m thinking of bookshops as a place for visual inspiration.
* I would like to make more zines, but I am also struggling to find the motivation. I saw a cool zine in a bookshop at the weekend that had a photo on the front cover and was tiny. I wonder if I could make a tiny photo zine?
* Blogging encourages writing to become part of life.
* I need new shoes for the winter. I always procrastinate on shoe shopping because I struggle to find shoes that are comfortable and that I like.
