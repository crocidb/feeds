*Blue light passes through the edges of the black-out blinds as I lie holding a pillow, on the edge of dreaming.*

When I look back on my choice to study art history, I feel delighted. This year I have been pushed in new ways: to write essays short and long, to write stories, to read about topics I have not yet encountered. I feel comfortable learning with the structure of university. Outside of school, I am challenging myself more, too: I want to read different kinds of books, the histories told in the podcast “In Our Time” make me ask big questions. I love looking at the world and, with every passing day, seeing something new that I would not have seen six months ago.

In my room I see the accumulation of my interests: books, mangas, coffee equipment that will not fit into the kitchen. Save for one poster, the walls are undecorated; white, blank, like a canvas. I think I like it this way because it creates space. The canvas of the walls encourages me to look outward: to notice how the trees battered by rain hours ago are now relatively still; to think about how the day started with a rainbow and ended with fairy lights; to notice the slight blue hue on the edges of the curtains.

I meditated for the first time in months this evening. I didn’t realise how much I missed it. “Breathe in through the nose, and out through the mouth.” The cadence is everything. I sat still and breathed in and out slowly, deeply. I imagined myself as a smaller part of the world around me; a world of villages and cities, trees and bridges. I did cry a little bit. I needed the moment to feel. Someone I wished to see once more has moved on. I look back with joy on the memories I have and look forward to all the new ones to come.

Now, lying in my bed, I look up to my fairy lights and think of the realm of hopes and dreams: of the delight in knowing that, with each passing day, there is something new to discover, or something old to rediscover. I think of the joys of stories – of telling them and hearing them. I delight in the warmth of the moment – under the covers, with a pillow by my cheek – as my eyes grow heavy and I realise soon it is time for rest. I think of how days start and end with colour: rainbows and hues; greens and blues. I am looking forward to tomorrow.
