Sometimes you wipe away words and their imprint – the place where you left them – remains inked in your mind.

*The feeling of writing.*

⁂ ⁂ ⁂

After I wrote “[The sound of writing](https://jamesg.blog/2026/08/07/the-sound-of-writing)”, I started to think about how writing feels. What does it feel like to write a piece of work? I do not have a definitive answer to this question, for I think the number of answers is as varied as stars in the sky. Indeed that is what makes writing enjoyable. Just as every time I look at the stars I see something new, so, too, do I see something new when I look at the page. I feel an opportunity to try something new – to practice my alliterations, to hear the cadence of my words in my mind as I write and see if they sound like me, to write about something about which I have never written before.

*I can talk with the remaining romance of Day.* I wrote this down in the twilight before midnight – in the time when I knew I had words in me but my energy was waning. It was time for rest. But I still felt a recognition that there was life in the day yet to write. And so I wrote about how I could keep going for a little moment longer. The elocution in the sentence is not perfect, but it means something to me, just as “the feeling of writing” meant something to me, too. Or should it be the "feelings" of writing?

*I reached for change and it was a bit far away but it was night and day is yet to come.* Around the same time – or perhaps that evening – I scrubbed away all that was written on my whiteboard and replaced it with “change :)” I was in the mood to try something new. These past few days have been a collection of new experiences: of trying a new Magic: the Gathering set, of practicing my communication skills, of getting back into the rhythm of things, of visiting new bookstores, of realising that this is a time for beginnings – the introductory lecture for one of my modules this year is this evening.

On that night, I reached for change and it was too far, my bed being at the opposite end of the room. But I had already reached for change, and continue to do so, for all movement in Day is change. My walk yesterday took me to places I have never seen before in a city I know well. The morning welcomed me with a sunrise that will forever be distinct because the dance and melody of Morning involves so many interactions that there always seems to be something new to see. This essay is change: an evolution from an idea wiped away on the whiteboard to soft words where I can explore how I feel.

Last night in bed, with the remaining romance of Day, I started jotting down how I felt, knowing that even though I should be sleeping there were a few sentences I had to write down, configurations of words I had to capture because, in the morning, they would be forgotten. There would be new words – these words – but there is no way for me to sample from the river of evening until that time comes. I have to seize the moment. Under the soft glow of my phone’s light, I wrote.

>
>
> The euphoria of writing a good sentence. Writing words into the night. The words are the light. Wordsmithing in bed. My words might not change the world but they will change my world. Of looking at the red leaves outside and knowing you should write about them because it marks the passing of the seasons.
>
>

I have written with a tear on the precipice of my eyelid. I have written with great excitement, knowing that I am ready to write something new. I have written with great curiosity, knowing that writing has a magical ability to make me see something I have never seen before, just as Morning, although it comes once every day, has the same ability – evening, too, and, indeed, all moments of Day.

I live in and within the stream of everyday dreams. Words let me document the joy of music in public places so that the notes can live on, explore who I am and who I want to be, and provide a comfort that exists in both my pocket and my mind at all times, ready for when I see something and have the urge to write it down.

[I asked a few other people how writing made them feel](https://forum.webwriterscollective.com/t/qotd-how-do-you-feel-when-you-write-03-09-2026/173/4). In that discussion, I said writing made me feel fascinated.

>
>
> I love the feeling of writing a sentence and realising it is leading me in a new direction I did not expect. I love figuring out how to use techniques like the power of three, and considering them in the context of the words I love and the words appropriate for the piece. I enjoy thinking about what word to use, and reflecting on why a particular word or phrase might not work.
>
>

Sitting here, on my comfortable, familiar grey armchair where I have written countless words, while gazing out at a new day, I have a new answer to this question. When I write, I feel alive.

*He ends this blog post with a small smile on his face and the joy of writing on his mind.*
