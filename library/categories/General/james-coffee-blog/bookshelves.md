I woke early — it has been warm lately. It is strange how you can find a burst of energy at 5am, but today it came, for I decided to organise my bookshelves, sorting what books I am ready to donate to the local charity shop.

I picked up books — stories — and thought about the good memories I had with them. I also thought honestly about some books that have been on my shelf for a while. Did I read this? Will I read this? Do I still want to hold onto this? In some cases the answer is yes. There are books I have that I know I am going to be ready to read one day. In other cases the answer is no — to let the dream go means to make room for new dreams.

Having sorted out my books, a dozen or so ready to be donated, I went to the magazines and clippings — the miscellanea. I found the newspaper extracts I have saved: stories I read that were so meaningful I knew I wanted to keep them.

I don’t know whether I found the story that helped me think about something that means a lot to me this morning or it found me. The piece spoke to me and said “it’s okay” in just the way I needed. That must have been why I saved it. Was past James doing future James a favour? Did he know there was more to read in the article — more to see?

Here, now, I am sitting on an uncomfortable metal chair in a bus stop, waiting for my journey of the day — one to which I have been looking forward all week. I hold a warm coffee — with caffeine, for I am tired — between my legs, there being no other place to hold it for I need my two hands to type. Maybe one day I’ll find this clipping from my stream of idle thoughts on the first rainy Sunday in weeks on my bookshelf, ready for me when I need it.
