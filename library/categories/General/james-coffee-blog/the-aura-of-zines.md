While reading some of the zines I purchased at the Edinburgh Zine Fair, and subsequently filing away those I have read <sup class="footnote-reference" id="f-1"><a href="https://jamesg.blog/longform-feed#1">1</a></sup>, I started to reflect more on the “aura” of a zine. [The Tate Modern summarises “aura”](https://www.tate.org.uk/art/art-terms/a/aura) as:

>
>
> Aura is a quality integral to an artwork that cannot be communicated through mechanical reproduction techniques – such as photography.
>
>

I have a separate stack where I keep the original versions of all of my hand-made zines. I like to keep these in their own place both for practical and sentimental purposes. For practical purposes, the original zines will not have been subject to any degree of fade or potential cut-offs at the margins that may appear in the scanned renderings that are used to print additional copies. Sentimentally, I want to keep the originals together, as they form part of my collection. I don’t necessarily think about this for my zines that are made on the computer first, though – in that case, the file used to print zines becomes the main artefact.

That is to say that the hand-made originals have some kind of “aura” – they were the first, and contain more detail than any scan. My typewriter zines, for example, contain the artefacts of having been made with a typewriter, like the impressions made on the other side of the paper.

Curiously, `ines I have received from friends seem to have their own “aura”, even if they are photocopied or part of a wider print run. I think this is because every zine sent to me by a friend, or as part of a trading arrangement, has a wider story: how I met the person who made the zine, whether I chose the zine or it was sent as a gift. This aura is close to sentimentality, but nevertheless deserves noting.

Whereas zines that I have printed don’t have the same aura, they still have great meaning. These are the ones I am ready to give out to people, the ones that exist because I want to share something I have made with the world, the ones that might raise someone’s spirits or spark their curiosity or have another effect – the latter being a potential present in any creative work, too.

My thoughts on aura and zines are nascent, but I am definitely keen to reflect more on this topic.

<sup class="footnote-definition-label" id="f-2">1</sup>

I am running out of space to store mini zines in the letter rack I bought a few months ago. I will need another storage system, ideally that lets me keep things better organised. It is quite exciting to think that I am making a tiny zine library of my own.
