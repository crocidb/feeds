Eula wrote a response (“[Collecting reference books for creativity](https://blog.loreleice.net/reference-books-creativity/)”) to my post on [inspiration from physical artefacts](https://jamesg.blog/2026/08/16/inspiration-from-physical-artefacts) and [organising them](https://jamesg.blog/2026/08/23/organising-physical-artefacts). Ironically, I didn’t appreciate the continuity between these posts until now. I digress. In her blog post, Eula wrote about how she organises her bookshelf, which made me think about how I organise mine, too.

At the moment, I have one bookshelf which I am actively outgrowing. My bookshelf has three shelves, and I keep more books on the top. My books are organised (roughly) as follows:

* Top surface of the bookshelf: Textbooks, novels for school, and miscellaneous books. I am also experimenting with keeping my scrapbook here; I want to keep the paper things I have made in the same place I keep my books.
* Top shelf: Translated Japanese fiction (one of my favourite genres of books) and miscellaneous fiction.
* Middle shelf: Biography, poetry, and art and design.
* Bottom shelf: Reference books. This includes style books (i.e. the AP Styleguide), a copy of Bartlett’s quotations (maybe I should open the book at a random page and see if that inspires a piece of writing?), and my collection of Tufte’s books. Admittedly, I rarely touch most of my reference books, but I am glad I have them.

I keep my other books on my desk, a table, and, really, anything that will hold a book. My other books are not particularly well organised: there is a mix of fiction, non-fiction, and reference. I keep school textbooks I am actively using on my desk or on the table next to my armchair, depending on where I am working. I keep longer-form fiction and manga in my bedroom.

I have been reticent about purchasing another bookshelf because I would like one that is second-hand. The last time I looked around local used furniture shops, there was not one that was suitable. I got my first bookshelf second-hand, which was cheap, well-made, and holds up well, so I would like to keep the tradition going by purchasing another second-hand one. But, I will need to get one at some point. Then, I will be able to sit down and organise my books more thoughtfully.

My dream is to have a second bookshelf where I can move everything, and turn the area where my current bookshelf is into a little book corner. Specifically, I would love to have a shelf dedicated to arts and design, another dedicated to textbooks, and one dedicated to fiction.
