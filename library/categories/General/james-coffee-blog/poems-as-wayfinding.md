When I look at a painting, or read a poem, or a story, I feel like I am entering a new world, one created by the artist, the writer, to evoke a particular feeling, to highlight something meaningful, to document a moment, and for many other reasons too.

I love the feeling of building a world in my mind when I engage with a work of art: the scenery in a novel or a poem, for example, or of the images in a painting that come together to help me understand a moment in time. I love when one work opens my mind to something else; when I read one novel and realise I want to explore the genre more, or when I realise I love the style of an artist and I feel motivated to learn about the time in which they were painting.

As web weavers, we can build worlds, too.

Stories, poems, paintings, songs, photographs – all of the arts – can help us find our way, to discover something, to navigate and explore moments in time. This is the essence that I have carried forward into the new design of my home page. Structured as a story in poetic form, my new home page both carries its own message in words and links out to places on my website to explore.

The poem now on my home page was first drafted while listening to folklore, while going through the countryside — looking on to castles and hills, passing through villages, as I daydream about my website — and refined at home, in my armchair, late into the evening: a blend of the energies of day and night. All inspired by one thought that came to mind: “Navigation bar as a poem. Home page as a poem?”

There are five kinds of links throughout the poem:

* Links to category pages, to explore a topic.
* Links to articles, that focus on a specific topic (i.e. website ideas).
* Links to my most recent blog posts.
* Links to things you can do (say hello, wander, search, make your mark).
* Links to meta pages (my category list, about me).

Presented as a story in the form of a poem, with hyperlinks, the text becomes both a story and an explicit way of finding information: a navigation tool in itself. The page then ends with my short bio, a welcome message, that ends on the note that I have kept through the last two designs of my home page:
