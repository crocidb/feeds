Over the last few weeks I have been making more paper zines. I love the whole process of making zines, from the writing and designing to the printing, folding, and sharing.

In terms of format, I enjoy the eight-page mini booklet format most, where you can make an (almost) pocket-sized booklet with only a single sheet of A4 paper. Sometimes I make these zines with my computer although, in many cases, I like to write something out by hand. I even made a zine with my typewriter recently. It was hard to balance the folded zine in my typewriter, so some words toward the end of pages came out angled. With that said, in the moment I saw it as creative serendipity – my tools and the medium (and, presumably, physics), were pushing me to make something in a different way than I expected. I had so much fun.

If you are curious, you can see the zines I have made digitally available in the [zines category](https://jamesg.blog/zines) on this website. Although, there are some zines I am reserving to share with friends directly, which I discuss later in this post.

⁂ ⁂ ⁂

My zines so far are mainly about writing, since I am more comfortable with writing than drawing. I like writing zines that are streams of consciousness. In some senses writing has been my “bridge” into zine making. Just as this website is relatively text-heavy, so too are my zines. With that said, I have, with the aid of a computer, made a zine with some photos I have taken, *[autumn (essence, of)](https://jamesg.blog/2026/10/19/autumn-essence-of-zine)*. I would like to experiment with making more photo zines, but for that I will need to remember to take more photos!

Drawing little illustrations appeals to me as an entry-way for adding more visual resources to my zines. I was doing a creative writing exercise a few weeks ago and ended up, while waiting for the exercise to begin, drawing a pattern in the corner. Perhaps I should start with patterns and see where I go from there? I have a pack of colour fine-liners I have almost never used. Perhaps this would be a good time to start using them? I brought the fine-liners out of the shelf they were on, hoping that their more prominent presence in my environment will encourage me to use them. (I wish I had writing desk with places for all my stationary!)

⁂ ⁂ ⁂

Whereas digital writing like a blog post does not have a strict end, paper does: there are edges. From a creative perspective, it means I only have so many words I can fit on a page before I have to move onto a new one. The medium builds in a pause where I can make a decision about whether the new page continues on from the previous one, or whether I start a new paragraph.

⁂ ⁂ ⁂

One aspect of zines that appeals to me is that the distribution mechanism is different. Whereas my blog posts can be read by anyone on the web, I can make zines that I only share directly with friends, or as part of a zine swap (where people exchange zines). Indeed, it feels like zines are complementary to my overall creative activities – blog posts are for the web, and are usually made entirely using computers; zines are for paper, and may be shared on the web, but may also only be shared with a few people and, when shared, are shared by hand (or by postal mail).

There is something quite special about making something and handing it to someone. So, too, is this true of web pages, but web pages require someone to retrieve a device to read them, whereas zines can be read in a moment. A friend shared a zine their partner had made a few weeks ago and I was so excited to look through it. (They ended up giving me a copy later, for which I am sincerely grateful, too.)

⁂ ⁂ ⁂

On distribution and sharing, this year I have generally been growing more comfortable with keeping more writing to myself as part of my creative process. I have been thinking about this for a while in the framing of having creative works that are “[stories for me](https://jamesg.blog/2024/06/29/the-stories-for-me)”. This year, however, I started expanding this framing. I am now more aware of works that are explicitly part of the process of learning, rather than the final embodiment of a project that is ready to share.

⁂ ⁂ ⁂

*Random note: The document scanning tool in some printers is amazing. I am astounded that I can put a piece of paper into the scanner, and get a digital copy ready for sharing.* Although, I need to figure out why my scans are cut off a bit at the margins.

⁂ ⁂ ⁂

Perhaps what excites me most about zines is there is so much to learn. I can experiment with pattern making. I can try to write new things that work well in the format. I can experiment more with the use of white space since it is easy for my hand to jump to different sections of a piece of paper, leaving parts blank if I would like. I am excited to learn more about the culture – what people make, why people love making what they make. And I am excited to be working more with paper again. I was recently thinking that before I started coding, I made things with paper. I have come full-circle in some regard: this year, I have been learning how much I appreciate making things with paper.
