Last weekend was so busy that I was too sleepy to write my weeknotes at my normal time. Thus, herein is a condensed version of my weeknotes.

Editorial note
----------

In Weeknotes W1, I said that weeknotes would be a four-week-long experiment, with my writing one weeknote every week for four weeks. I have been enjoying the process, and so I think I am going to keep this going. I am not setting another goal, but will continue with this format for as long as I find it interesting.

Highlights of the week
----------

Last week I:

* Attended an academic conference online for the first time. I don’t think I was completely the target audience – there were so many words used that I have not yet encountered in my studies – but I enjoyed almost all of the talks to which I listened. The event was two days long. The only downside was that lunch was 30 minutes, and there wasn’t really another break between the rest of the talks.
* Went to the Edinburgh Zine Fair. There were at least 20 vendors, collectively selling the widest variety of zines. I bought one zine that lists supermarket reviews of sardines, another on the introduction of Britain’s decimal currency, and a few more miscellaneous ones. The highlight of the event was getting to talk with other people who make zines. I asked one person how they made zines so small, and they shared that they like to use the pieces of paper left over. I love this concept, and feel inspired to try and make a few smaller zines.
* Decided that I would participate in Writing Month. I am setting myself the goal of publishing one poem on my website every day this month. It has been going well so far. A side effect of this goal of trying to publish a poem every day is that I have many drafts of things that intrigued me but are not ready for publication. For the last few days, my poems have been haikus, a format I may write about in the future.
* I made a few changes to Artemis:
  * I moved to use the “[lingua](https://github.com/pemistahl/lingua-py)” Python package for language identification, which is being used to add the country code before a post title in Artemis if the post’s language is not my primary language. See “[Brainstorming the multi-lingual reading experience for Artemis](https://jamesg.blog/2026/10/21/brainstorming-multi-lingual-artemis)” for a write-up with background. This is only implemented for me right now, but I would like to roll it out to more people. To do so, I will need to add a feature for people to be able to set their default language, and a feature flag so people can toggle this on and off.
  * I am working on code to turn words in a post title that appear all in upper case to sentence case, while trying my best to ensure abbreviations are not affected. I think it is going to take some time to get this right.
  * I fixed a bug in the reading interface, and then fixed another bug introduced by my fix.

* Visited a free exhibition at the Royal Academy of Scotland. I liked Paul Furneaux RSA’s “[Sound of Water](https://www.royalscottishacademy.org/artworks/30945-paul-furneaux-rsa-sound-of-water/)” (no date) and Waller Hugh Paton RSA’s “[Ardentinny on the Clyde](https://www.royalscottishacademy.org/artworks/31042-waller-hugh-paton-rsa-ardentinny-on-the-clyde-1872/)” (1872). The latter painting used colour in a way that reminded me vaguely of Seurat, although the ground was much more real than that in Seurat’s pointillist renderings.

This week I plan to focus on my upcoming art history assignment, look through as many of my notes as I can, and read some of the zines I got at the weekend.
