After twenty-five wonderful conversations, [Wonders of Web Weaving](https://web-weaving.jamesg.blog/) is, at least for now, complete. To everyone who has been listening over the last few months: thank you! It has been the greatest pleasure not only to have terrific conversations with thoughtful web weavers, but to bring those to you every week.

When I started the project, I had a goal in mind: I wanted a show that would be fun to listen to on a train ride. A few weeks ago, when listening to the edited version of the second-to-last episode on a train, I thought to myself *I did it!*

Behind the scenes
----------

In terms of how the project came to be, I emailed a mixture of people who I knew and people I didn’t, aiming to invite guests that would, together, represent a wide range of subjects related to the web. It was, and still is, important to me that the project was not a “tech podcast.” The web is about more than technology.

Before each conversation, I spent time with the guest’s personal website, scanning pages, looking at blog posts, and deciding on an “anchor” that would be the theme of the episode around which my questions would be oriented. For instance, I spoke about [accessibility with Nic](https://web-weaving.jamesg.blog/16), as Nic has experience with accessibility. I also asked a few standard questions. Over time, I realised all my questions fit an arc: history, present, and future.

Through the course of the show, I had discussions on everything from [writing on the web](https://web-weaving.jamesg.blog/22) to the “[web revival](https://web-weaving.jamesg.blog/19)” to [mathematics](https://web-weaving.jamesg.blog/13) to accessibility (ep. [16](https://web-weaving.jamesg.blog/16) and [20](https://web-weaving.jamesg.blog/20)) to [magazines](https://web-weaving.jamesg.blog/2) to [books](https://web-weaving.jamesg.blog/10) and more. And, in so doing, I was conscious to ensure that the podcast was not all men talking about technology. There were and are more voices to be heard who have stories I wanted to hear. To that end, I hope I played a tiny part in introducing you to some voices you may not have encountered on the web before.

In terms of the actual discussions, in every conversation, I started with five or so minutes of chatting with the guest and introducing them to the recording process, then we recorded for \~40-50 minutes – sometimes for less time, sometimes for more – and, in the remaining time, did wrap up and I communicated the logistics of transcriptions and releases. While I invited guests with the email subject “Podcast interview?”, I was conscious to communicate upfront and early on that I liked the episodes to be “conversations” or “discussions”, not interviews. After all, this project is all about celebrating the web.

At the end of every episode, I asked two questions, one on the theme of the future, and the other a “fun question.” As a result of the question about the future – which was some variant on “where do you hope the indie web will be in five years?” – we have a collection of potential futures. Indeed, especially now, I think it is important to imagine what we want the web to be. At this point I will invite you to be a part of the show and ask you: where do you hope the indie web will be in five years?

The fun questions went through phases, but eventually coalesced on a roughly-standard question where I asked the guest to share a fun fact about where they are in the world. I thought this was a nice way to end the show: with a smile, with something interesting.

Concluding the show
----------

At the beginning of the show, I promised myself I would do 10 episodes. Because I was so excited about the project, the number of episodes planned became 15, and then 20, and then at least 25. With that said, I always wanted the show to be limited in its number of episodes because I know that it is challenging to keep something going on for a long time. Having an end number of episodes made it easier to commit to the project.

This autumn, I had began a new run of recording, but it was starting to get a bit much in addition to the other priorities I had. There were other episodes lined up, but I thought it best to pause on a high note than try to rush.

There may be another season one day, but, at least for now, the project is complete.

Thank you
----------

To all the people who agreed to spend an hour chatting with me about the web, thank you so much for sharing your thoughts. I want to extend a special thanks to [Zachary](https://zacharykai.net) from [Lunaseeker Press](https://lunaseeker.com) for transcribing the episodes. I decided I wanted a transcript for every episode from day one, so that people who prefer to read, or for people who cannot listen to a podcast, had a transcript available to them.

To everyone listening, thank you for spending time with me and the guests. This project has been a real pleasure to work on. And, if you haven’t listened before, good news: you have [25 conversations about all things indie web](https://web-weaving.jamesg.blog) to which you can listen!
