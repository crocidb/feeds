*After "[The (My) Desk](https://jamesg.blog/2024/03/19/the-my-desk)" from March, 2024.*

As I was leaving my desk for the day, getting ready to have a cup of tea in bed and wind down for the evening, I caught a glance of a half-folded zine on my desk. The zine has been there for a few days, quietly telling a story that only this evening I was able to hear: this desk is becoming. *This desk is full of life.*

Situated in a cold, white room, [my desk has felt distant](https://jamesg.blog/2024/03/19/the-my-desk) at times. When I sit there, in its comfortable chair, I see all the essentials a desk needs, and yet perhaps the most beautiful part of sitting at the desk is not the desk itself but the view – how the space encourages me to look outside. I don’t mind the white walls because the green hills more than make up for it. But maybe now is the time I should find a print and add colour to the space.

I looked at my desk that I have seen a thousand times before with new eyes. The space has changed a lot over the last year. An unfolded zine – a representation of the idea of a work-in-progress – takes up space. My creative writing textbook, too, takes up space, within which there are 600 pages of techniques and studies and stories with which I will spend countless hours this year. Today, I have a reference book – Katy Hessel’s How to Live an Artful Life – sitting on my desk, to which I looked for inspiration earlier. Another book rests on the desk that I referenced earlier this week.

My desk is becoming a space for works-in-progress: a space that occupies books and stories, a place that reflects my ever-more artistic outlook on the world. My desk feels more like my own. An origami butterfly rests on the elevated surface of my monitor stand. A chatterbox sits next to it, too. *I love making things with paper. I love making things that people can hold.*

Earlier today my desk was home to my typewriter where I wrote a story in a way I have never written a story before. I let words and sentences blend together. I let myself go to the edge of the paper. I let myself work with a new form. The document became infused with time, representing my thoughts in one afternoon where the physical world was in the foreground and the digital world was in my background. I made something physical today, at my desk. *I love making things that people can hold.*

This year my desk has been the home of conversations and of poetry, of learning and essays, of lectures and community. I may prefer to write in my armchair – and occasionally in bed, illuminated only by the glow of a night-lit screen and white-lit keyboard – but my desk is starting to feel now, after years, like it is more of a place, a place from where I can see the world around, where I can make something people can hold; a place for my works-in-progress.
