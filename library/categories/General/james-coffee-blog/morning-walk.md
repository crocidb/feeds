Frost fell over night, coating the hills and trees in a soft, crystalline white. There is no wind; leaves lie still as if stuck to the ground by the temperature; branches barely move, accentuating the movement of the birds that fly around, making their way through the chilly air.

When I woke up this morning, I opened the window so I could feel the cold and all of its sensations. The window quickly condensed as warm air met cool. I decided to go for a walk, a short walk, but enough for me to be able to breathe in all that the day had to offer.

I equivocated on how far to go, but, in retrospect, it was as if my mindset was that going anywhere would be enough. Cold air changes how I see the world; long distances feel longer. I have to be careful in case there is ice. Thus my steps were filled with quiet trepidation and my eyes were focused for a while on the ground so that I could find my footing. There was little ice; the conditions were good enough for a walk. And so I proceeded through the winter day.

Winter forced me to focus on the essentials: first, the ground, and then my immediate surroundings. I became colder as I walked, but I was warmed by the beauty of the winter scene – of silver trees, of delicate frost, of beds of leaves who, days ago, were showing off their red colour, and now were covered in the blanket of frost created over night. Breathing in and out, I saw my breath for the first time this season. Every time I see my breath I think of life. *That’s my breath.*

I saw the low sun rising over the crests of hills, providing a little bit of warmth, but not much. I am glad I didn’t walk too far but, as my walk concludes, I wonder whether I want to go out again. Far from being a deterrent, the cool air awakens my mind. I ended my journey with the same question I have been considering all year: *what is it about winter that I find so alluring?* With the spirit of winter in my lungs, I might now ask: why does winter enrapture so, even though the conditions are so cold?

This morning, I started to piece together part of the puzzle, to remember why winter stays with me: the light and the world are so beautiful. This, on reflection, is true of any time of the year. But, it is the colours and feelings of every new season that continue to remind me of the beauty in Day.

Furthermore, in winter, it feels like the cold is enough to freeze time and space, for a little while – the world slows. As it does, I feel myself equivocate about whether I need to venture far or whether to take smaller journeys and make them count to the best extent I can. In short, winter transforms. Perhaps in that transformation there is another reason why I love winter: I ask different questions, I dream bigger thoughts, I remember, as I try to affirm every day, that small things – from the delicate frost resting on leaves to the joys of the short winter walk – are what make the world.
