For years I have kept a single running document where I take all my notes and write all my TODOs. I like knowing there is one place where I can write everything. I can always move and re-arrange what I need to. I often do. Over the last few weeks, though, I have been reflecting on how I maintain the TODO part of the document, which is always at the top. I have noticed some things linger on there for days or weeks, usually because they are not time-bound. This means I end up looking at them for some time, thinking “I really should do that” while I do other work, and yet I feel stressed when I haven’t worked through the list.

One of the potential peculiarities of my system – I need others’ views before I know if it is not as common – is that I write down a lot of personal tasks, too. If I need to do the washing, I will write it down. If I need to take out the rubbish, I will write it down. This means that I am always looking at a list of tasks. I was thinking earlier that I don’t really need to write down to do the washing: if the basket is full, I will know I need to do it. Maybe I need to change how I keep track of tasks…

I have always felt motivated by getting through tasks, but, admittedly, my current system is stressful, and isn’t really working. Rather than blame myself for the system, I want to adopt a mindset of kindness. *This is arbitrary. This can be changed!* And so I am experimenting with writing down fewer notes of things I will remember because they come up in the course of my day. I may even try to do more tasks in the moment rather than waiting.

I also need to look at how much I do. I tend to have a lot that I want to read, watch, listen to, and more. I can’t do it all. When I read Sophy’s “[Too many interests, too little time](https://www.sophy.be/blog/too-many-interests/)” post and saw the list of potential things to do she had enumerated, I thought “I can relate to this so much.” There is always another blog post to read. A book to pick up. A programming task to take on. A thing to do. With that said, many of the things I love most – writing, playing guitar, traveling, spending time with family – slot into my days naturally, and never need written down as a TODO to be fit into my day. *(I do write down blog post ideas, but these are ideas rather than TODOs).*

Perhaps what I need right now, in the vein of kindness, is to repeat to myself that it’s okay that I can’t do everything. Furthermore, I don’t need to let my TODO list get longer and longer with lists of things that I either will remember, or, realistically, I might not need to do immediately; the things that are important to me will always be in my mind. I feel like I am missing a piece of the puzzle here, and fear that some part of me is going to miss something important, or that of course I should be writing everything down. But I’ll hold onto the kindness and let it see me through to building a healthier relationship with tasks and time.
