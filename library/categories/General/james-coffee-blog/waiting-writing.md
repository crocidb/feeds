I love to write while I am walking around in the world. I keep a digital notebook in which I write down what I observe: sights, sounds, scents; emotions, hopes, dreams. The smile I see as two friends embrace each other in a hug as the person who has been waiting patiently at a table sees their friend walk in makes me joyful both in the moment, and as I reflect on the words in my notebook. Every word is part of a story of the day – a story, too, of life.

I take notes like this:

> Enjoying an iced latte in the park while the sun shines — hearing someone laugh from the heart; the rare kind. The sun illuminated the white pages of the book a lady reads in the park; an iced latte sits next to her. We are firmly in the season for iced coffee. Bagpipes resonate through the park.

I try to capture whatever details I can. I had to write down that the person was laughing from the heart because they were: their laugh was contagious and in bursts, and must have stood out in front of the faint bagpipes that had been playing around that time.

I also like to take notes while I am waiting, such as in line. I mentioned this at the [Homebrew Website Club Writing Edition event that I hosted yesterday](https://jamesg.blog/indieweb.org/events/2026-05-30-hwc-writing). Some observations that made their way into one of my recent blog posts were noted while I was waiting for my coffee to be prepared in a coffee shop. I had a moment before the coffee was ready and I wanted to note down what was happening in the world around. I didn’t have time to write a story, but I did have time to jot a few things down.

I like to take my notes back home and work with them; it's hard for me to write a polished piece on the go. I need space between the moment and the present to figure out how I want to craft the story; to look for details that emerge from the whole.

I like to preserve the chronology of my notes, for the order tells a story – from the joy of the morning light filling the cafe, to the almost-iridescent blue colour of bell-shaped flowers on a tree, to later, in another cafe, hearing Bon Iver’s “Everything is Peaceful Love”, next to which I wrote “Indeed.”: therein, the evolution from a focus on the light to a focus on sound.

In a particularly long coffee line one day, I even wrote a web page using [Athena](https://athena.jamesg.blog/), a mobile web page editor I was making at the time (and a project I surely want to revisit at some point). I can’t remember exactly what I made, but I did feel good that I was able to make something while waiting in a relatively busy shop with poor lighting (the coffee was worth it).

[Thamara](https://thamara.co.uk/waiting-for-big-thief/) wrote a blog post this week while waiting for a gig to start which made me think I should probably write down my thoughts on the topic too. Long story short: waiting can be a time to write. Maybe waiting – and the time inherent in waiting – is an apt space for imagination?
