You can use the web to share your poetry, or photography, or essays, or drawings, or sketches, or music, and everything in between. On a website, you have control over design, layout, and distribution <sup class="footnote-reference" id="f-1"><a href="https://jamesg.blog/longform-feed#1">1</a></sup>. You own your domain, so you own your links.

It feels like so many more creatives would appreciate personal websites. With that in mind, I have been thinking about how to build more bridges between the personal website community and other creative communities.

This inspired me to work on a new pocket-sized zine, [*A Creative's Guide to Personal Websites*](https://jamesg.blog/assets/creative-web.pdf)*.* The zine covers:

* Why you should start a website
* How to find hosting
* Why domain names are important
* Why having a website is important
* An introduction to the IndieWeb
* Where to go to find community
* 11 ideas of things to do on your website
* 11 ideas of things to write about on your website

I wrote this zine specifically for creatives who may not have any technical background. I used as little jargon as I could to communicate the key points, and made the ideas all focused on things like quizzes and zines and tutorials and music rather than technology.

The zine is available as a [PDF (A4 size) that you can print and fold](https://jamesg.blog/assets/creative-web.pdf), and as [text on the web](https://jamesg.blog/creative-web). Austin Kleon has a folding guide that covers [how to fold zines like this into an eight-page booklet](https://austinkleon.com/2020/03/04/how-to-make-a-zine-from-a-single-sheet-of-paper/).

I encourage you to print off the zine and share it with your friends who may be interested in having a personal website.
