*We’ve gotta keep going.* The words flowed back to me with unexpected fluency; the sound of the song was all it took. I sang word after word, words deep within that I forgot were there, a reminder of how much I loved the song, of how much I listened, and, in this case, of how much I played it on piano; a sound steeped in blurry significances for I can’t remember when I first heard the song but I do remember how much I loved it. I still do. *When do things begin?*

This year I have started to think of new dreams. Some are related to music, their presence before now -- when I am writing them down -- a faint ambition I mention in conversation. “I would love to play guitar in public one day.” “I would love to be able to sing better.” Dreams that may not come true but I love them because it is fun to have dreams, and it is fun to imagine myself practicing enough to have the rhythm to play guitar in public, and having a voice with more depth. With regard to both, much practice is needed. I don’t want to be a professional musician though: I just want to play a little bit for others.

I want to play music with someone else, too. I worry that maybe my style is incompatible with others, that maybe the liberty I find in playing by ear would not integrate as well with others’ styles. But I would like to try, just as I have in my writing, practicing with others and creating something new; therein, a new dream. *Maybe one day.*

I think back to the moment when I played piano with someone in public. They came over to ask if I wanted to play. I likely mentioned that I play by ear and my understanding of musical theory is limited. [And yet we played](https://jamesg.blog/2024/01/25/moments-of-joy), *Chopsticks*. In a few moments, without having met each other before, we were able to make music, and left me with a story that carries forward through the years, just as *Keep Going* does. Reflecting on that time makes me think about how two sounds come together to become one. *How do musicians find each other?* I want to make music for moments and moments for music.

My ambitions are changing. I used to dream of technology and now more so I dream of words and art and stories and imagination and friendship and learning and music. Now, my ambitions feel more me-sized: ones I can build on with time, through practice – through thinking, listening, learning, talking, writing, playing, dreaming. *this is me trying.*
