When I walk around the world I like to take notes of what I see, hear and feel: of the rhythms and routines, of the moments of kindness, of the times when I examine and re-examine what the places where I spend the most time mean to me.

I often develop my notes into published works. I keep the notes intact, and bring their ideas and phrases and essences into a final piece. As a result of my process, I end up with two bodies of work: a published piece that represents all the time I have spent reviewing my notes and weaving them together, and the notes themselves – the bricks behind a work. I don’t like deleting original notes after their concepts have graduated into a full post; the notes are significant – they were and are essential to my creative process.

I usually work from a single text document in Apple Notes. I routinely move sentences and paragraphs that I recorded during a day into separate notes. This happens either when I have brought a note into something I have written, or when I have decided there isn’t anything I want to develop further from a note. <sup class="footnote-reference" id="f-1"><a href="https://jamesg.blog/longform-feed#1">1</a></sup> Some notes sit for a few days, or a few weeks just in case I need them; some ideas take time to develop.

For the moments that do become stories, I have started to create a closer connection between the notes and the works, a reference just for me, in case I find myself looking back through my notes one day and wonder if I wrote about them.

After writing my “Of Day” post yesterday, I added the following below the title:

>
>
> *Which became [https://jamesg.blog/2026/09/21/of-day](https://jamesg.blog/2026/09/21/of-day)*
>
>

For today’s post on autumn dreams, I added:

>
>
> *Became [https://jamesg.blog/2026/09/22/autumn-dreams](https://jamesg.blog/2026/09/22/autumn-dreams)*
>
>

Whether or not I keep up this practice I am not sure. I don’t regularly revisit my notes, but I sometimes do. Maybe this practice will feel like a favour past James did for future James. In the interim, it feels good to add a little preface to the beginning of a note whose existence says these words became a polished post. *These words grew into something.*

<sup class="footnote-definition-label" id="f-2">1</sup>

Indeed, sometimes the ideal end state for a note is the note itself – a place for a moment to rest that is its own. Not everything becomes a story.
