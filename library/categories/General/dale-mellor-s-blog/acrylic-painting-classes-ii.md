### Week 7 ###

#### Paul Kenton ####

![](paul-kenton.avif)The study artist this week was Paul Kenton. We started by looking at videos of him at work. He starts a new work by randomly splashing paint around, but then spends a lot of time doing very detailed and very deliberate refinements. Bear in mind when looking at his original that it was painted on a ten foot canvas; shrinking down to the size shown gives the impression that there is much more fine detail than there really is.

After two hours of effort I got to this point. ![](cityscape.avif) This is very hard work, much more difficult than a landscape. Putting the starting splashes of paint down is easy enough, but taming that into a presentable image takes effort. Getting the vibrant colours was a lot of trial and error, and getting some proper straight lines into the image, which I quickly realized was important, involved a technique with a scraper that took time to develop.

The main thing I learned this week was that you can throw paint around all day, but to make a convincing cityscape there have to be some very precisely placed, and very straight, lines. These form the visual scaffolding on which the rest of the image hangs.

Will continue to develop this painting next week, I think.

### Week 8 ###

![](paul-kenton.avif) The original again for comparison. Another couple of hours more effort… ![](cityscape-2.avif) Have to admit, after initially feeling quite dejected with this, like a five-year-old could have done it, it has grown on me much since. Really, could a five-year-old do it? Ten, fifteen-years-old? Nah, probably not.

In fact, the more times Iʼve cast my eyes upon this work, the more Iʼve come to like it, and Iʼm now at the point where I actually think it is really, really good, especially when viewed from the other side of a room. (I have since mounted this properly in a frame, and it is displayed quite prominently in my living room. I am beginning to see the real value of this course I did; £70 for the course is well below the price of a single original piece of art!)

The main thing Iʼve learned from this exercise is that there is a lower limit on the size of detail that you can get in the image (recall that the paper was approximately A3 sized, compared to the ten foot canvas of the original artist; I should add that the paints and the brushes provided by the college were basically all on the crap side of rubbish). And so the skill is in giving the impression that there is more detail than there really is. It is also to be noted that tidying the edges up, as per Teacher Simonʼs instructions, makes a big difference to the presentation.

On top of everything else, Iʼve come to realize that this is my painting, which, while inspired by the original work, is not supposed to be actually a copy but an interpretation. And Iʼm happy that that aspect—the evolution of my own style—is coming along nicely.

### Week 9 ###

![](street-corner--original.avif)We were invited to bring along our own canvas, to take something away which we can then continue to work on. So I went out and took this quite typical picture of the Rows in the centre of Chester.

 I distorted it to this which I have chosen to emphasise the rooflines. The colours are deliberately blown out so that more detail can be seen in the buildings.![](street-corner--modified-2.avif) Iʼll try to get the sky something like the original undistorted image above.

 Given what I learned in Week 8 of this course, it is clear that I am going to have to work hard to give the impression of detail in the painting, and that it will have to go on quite a large canvas to work at all.

 Here we go. The canvas is 30″×24″, and this after an hour and halfʼs work. ![](street-corner-1.avif) Only a small part of the overall image, but Iʼm pleased with the way this has started out: the base coat is fine and the shapes are going into the right places.

### Summary ###

 I have taken away much, much more from these classes than I ever gave myself, or the teacher, credit for at the time. I have seen myself learn to paint—it is not difficult, but also not natural like you might think—and get to know the style that comes out from that naturally. I have also been left wanting more, have acquired a nice big canvas and some paints, and am now honing my skills regularly.

 It was an excellent course, a valuable experience.
