Purpose as a Verb, a Force for Good

 This piece is a rise to the challenge of the [Indieweb Carnival](https://indieweb.org/IndieWeb_Carnival), August 2026: [Purpose](https://thoughts.uncountable.uk/august-2026-indieweb-carnival-purpose/).

There is a meaning of *purpose* which even the online Cambridge English dictionary misses: that is its use as a verb. A hammer is purposed to drive nails through floorboards, but then it can be re-purposed to act as a door stop.

The usual way to use this is with the *re-* prefix, but *purpose* on its own, as a verb, makes a very strong statement about the effectiveness of a tool being in how it is used—purposed—by the craftsman. It is also a strong statement about the realizable power of imagination of the human mind, particularly when something is purposed to good effect for a job it was never envisaged to do. Fire keeps us warm, but it is also a great way to prepare meat to kill bad bacteria and release the nutritional value.

Things can also go against us. The purpose of the nuclear bomb has always been sold to us as a way to keep peace, to keep our enemies away through intimidation. But a bomb can be re-purposed at any time to commit intentional unprovoked genocide, and that is the real fear we all live under with the spectre of a bomb hanging over us, and the unpredictable but strangely empowered loose cannons with whom we share the Earth.

The purpose of the Prime Minister of the UK, and indeed the leader of any nation, is to make changes to government where things are seen to be not working too well for us. This is of course an arguable definition; many more would say they uphold the will of the people in defining the country they live in, but equally many would agree that this is not true and there is no real democracy.

So, in conclusion, *purpose* as a verb is immensely powerful. If the pen is mightier than the sword, then *purpose*, as a verb, might be its strongest, sharpest edge. How can we put the word into action for good? Can we purpose the government to work for the betterment of the majority, and not outlandishly favour the multi-millionaires?
