### My take ###

Life is short. Some people put some thought into their RSS presentation, while others simply donʼt. Some choose to make their RSS presentation severely truncated, so that you have to make a choice whether to go to the web site or just move on. Some blog posts look good naturally as RSS, others donʼt (maths and technical presentations are usually quite poor). On the converse, some web pages are nice to read, others are horrible.

RSS is another facet of a blog, along with the web site and browser ‘read-mode’. Some of these facets are very good most of the time, whether theyʼve been thought about much or not; some are terribly bad.

Perhaps people should be encouraged to add extra text to their RSS facet to indicate whether or not the text is best viewed in a browser, or if the RSS is fine. There could even be hard protocol built into RSS so that readers can make the correct choice automatically. But would that lead to subversion and weaponization? Do we want to be told what to do?

I like RSS as it is. I read it, and if it all looks coherent and complete I can move on. If it doesnʼt then I can make a quick judgement about whether to open it in the browser or not, and if I do the triage starts all over again.
