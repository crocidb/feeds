Apostrophes and Closing Quotes in English
==========

The difference between ʼ and ’
----------

Dale Mellor [ https://khleedril.org/dale-mellor ](https://khleedril.org/dale-mellor)

 5th October, 2026

What is the difference between ‘ʼ’ and ‘’’? Well the first is Unicode 0x2bc, and the second is Unicode 0x2019; the former is an apostrophe, the latter a single closing quotation mark.

When to use them? If you are closing a quotation which was opened with a single opening quotation mark, then use the closing quotation mark. In all other cases (possessives, contractions) use the apostrophe.

By most accounts, these two items of punctuation appear identical on the page, especially in the hand-written word, but they are distinct items of punctuation. Humans distinguish them by context, but machines, including screen readers, can distinguish them when the proper Unicode is used.

On your keyboard you may have such abominations as ‘`’, ‘'’, ‘"’, often referred to as back-ticks, ticks, and dog-ears(!). These should never be used in English prose, unless you are stuck in the Dark Ages and are restricted to the ASCII character set instead of the full Unicode.

Just a tuppence worth of thought for perfectionist bloggers working in the English language (I canʼt speak for any others).

For reference, useful punctuation Unicodes I often use are

```
  |--------+---+----------------------|
  |  0x2bc | ʼ | Apostrophe           |
  | 0x2018 | ‘ | Opening single quote |
  | 0x2019 | ’ | Closing single quote |
  | 0x201c | “ | Opening double quote |
  | 0x201d | ” | Closing double quote |
  | 0x2014 | — | Em dash              |
  |--------+---+----------------------|

```
