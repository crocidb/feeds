The Death of Honour: Silos Must Not Be Used
==========

 https://khleedril.org/blog/2026-10-21--silo-vs-indieweb.html

 khleedril.org/2026-10-21--silo-vs-indieweb

Dale Mellor [ https://khleedril.org/dale-mellor ](https://khleedril.org/dale-mellor)

 22nd October, 2026

**“A silo is not inherently good nor bad. A silo behaves.”** Joe Crawford at [https://artlung.com/blog/2026/10/19/be-indieweb-enough/](https://artlung.com/blog/2026/10/19/be-indieweb-enough/).

In arguing that big tech has any virtue, that article fails to make a distinction between those big-corporate silos (all of them today) which behave differently to how they say they are behaving, and those from 30 years ago which held their promise (remember Googleʼs *Do no evil*?)

Especially in the U.K. where *an Englishmanʼs word is as good as his bond* was *de rigueur* 30 years ago, and also to a large extent in the U.S.A. and the world at least in cross-border diplomacy, there has been a quiet social upheaval where honesty and decency and civility and honour are no longer to be taken for granted, and distrust rules business bolstered with reams of small print and legalese.

I believe this change occurred, not coincidentally, with the rise of the Internet. Almost as soon as electronic digital communications started being used, security-conscious system administrators have had to take the view with regard to all technical standards that you cannot trust anyone, and that if the standards allow subversion then that subversion can be used at will, honour be damned (this is known as the [Byzantine generals problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_fault)).

Thus the security of your domain is entirely up to you and has to be based on the notion that everything and everybody is untrustable. As the years have rolled on, so has piled up protocol stacks of parity checks, certificate checks, encryption, and so on, to an increasingly inefficient—almost today at the point of stagnation—network. All railing against the notion that there is nothing to be trusted.

This is the one single reason I shirk from A.I. and all big tech. It simply cannot be trusted to do what it claims to do. Nor can it be trusted to continue doing only what it started. Even the most sincere businesses are prone to take-over, and then the deepest promises go out of the window (remember Meta taking over WhatsApp and breaking the promise, the single unique selling point, of end-to-end encryption?)

This is why I think it is important that indie web/smol web/IndieWeb be seen ultimately as a complete replacement for all parts of the corporate web. People who dilute their use of the web (even in such case as using webmention.io, in my opinion) contribute to the rising evil in the world, and should be encouraged to stop.

And so in contrast to Joe Crawfordʼs point of view, I think it right that everyone should be encouraged to implement independent standards all the way through, and those that do it less than others can fairly be described as ‘less indie web’. Of course, that is still a legitimate choice independent webbers have, and in any case anybody can only give their best reasonable effort.
