Pages
==========

 https://khleedril.org/blog/2026-10-12--pages.html

 khleedril.org/2026-10-12--pages

Dale Mellor [ https://khleedril.org/dale-mellor ](https://khleedril.org/dale-mellor)

 12th October, 2026

This is a response to the [Indieweb Carnival](https://indieweb.org/IndieWeb_Carnival)ʼs [*Pages*](https://marisabel.nl/public/blog/Indieweb_Carnival_October_2026_Pages) call.

The difficulty in discussing pages is stated right at the top of the call for articles: “Like a skeleton supporting the skin of a text, the page disappears in its very function, and in that unprepossessing nature lies its strength.” The page is like the air we breathe, the aether whose ripples are the march of electromagnetic energy. Vital, but invisible, and might be considered of philosophical interest only.

It is possible that the overall impression of a book can be through the feel of the wad of pages in your hand. While a thick paperback might make you feel nervous about the amount of concentrative memory you are going to need, there is nothing more luxurious than the paced perusal of a large, thick, hardback.

To authors of fiction, pages really are of no concern. All the intellect is in the content of the text on the pages.

Yet when reading a work of fiction, the pages are a gauge of how far into a story you have progressed. I think some of the most annoying books are the ones which seem to have a lot of open questions, yet the combined thickness of the pages remaining to read seems insufficient to do justice to the conclusion of the story. Conversely, when you feel the story is done, but there remains a thickness of pages enough for another story, one is left feeling that a reading drag is about to occur, an unwelcome over-long encore.

Still, the real indulgence of a work of fiction is the text, not the paper. A really good story will always make the pages, however they are physically manifested, invisible. If you are noticing the pages turning slowly, there is surely a problem with the writing?

Authors of technical books and manuals, on the other hand, must take more interest in the pages which will eventually support their work. Diagrams and tables, in particular, need to be prepared with a level of detail suitable for the page on which they will appear; the placement must be such that a reading of the text is not broken too much by the diagram, and yet the diagram has to appear as close to its textual context as it can, even though this might actually be over the page.

And then the wad of pages serves as a global index into the book. The pages of an old manual that fall open naturally are the ones with pertinent information on them. The pages near the front are introductory material, and the ones at the back are summary. With familiarity, one can find a particular piece of data they are looking for by knowing roughly the feel of the thickness of pages leading to that point, and having an intuition for the order of content in the vicinity of those data.

So on the whole, pages to me mean a measure of place within a story or within the flow of data dissemination. Like the fuel gauge on a car, the measure is crudely approximate, but at the same time gives a real feeling of the magnitude of the undertaking.
