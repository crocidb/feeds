A note for the partially sighted: none of the images here have ALT-text since they are described as fully as they could be in the main body of the prose. Hopefully you can appreciate a little of the flavour of my work!

 Right, time to get out of the comfort zone and do something left-field. Iʼll be honest that I expected acrylic painting classes to be more instructional than they were, and I also expected acrylic paints to be something a bit special to work with, but it turns out the paints are about as cheap and nasty as you can get and the classes are of the *‘throw it on and see how you feel’* variety.

### Week 1 ###

We were given the three primary colours, shown how to get secondary colours, and then practised mixing these by laying down two-colour swaths. It turns out that making true secondary colours is surprisingly difficult, especially green which always seemed to turn out quite murky.

#### Splotches: ####

Just a couple of examples from a set of all combinations of the six primary and secondary colours. The A1 paper was torn down to 16 A5 sized pieces for this weekʼs work.

Orange/purple ![](orange-purple.avif)

#### Textures: ####

 Picked up a piece of metal gauze thinking Iʼd get some good texture from it. Nah, not really, just an uncontrollable mess. ![](flames.avif) With retrospect it does look alive, and hot. Maybe I can use this in a fiery image some day?

 Splatting the brush on the paper was always going to be more predictable. ![](flowers.avif)

#### Inspired works: ####

Made after a very lush green photograph in which the frame was filled out with ferns, I find that mixing colours produces some surprising results. ![](foliage.avif) On the whole I completely failed to get either the colours or the textures anything like I wanted. But the surprising result made me think more organically, that the paint has a mind of its own and that the real purpose of the class might be to connect with it on an emotional level. If it is drawing emotion out of me, Iʼm happy to see that it is finding movement within structure within me. I like that.

This was the surprise of the day for me. I donʼt know what possessed me to paint reedy things, they were the result of light idle slashes of the brush. But once they were there the urge to put bright red flowers on them was irresistible. The fact that they look like poppies is pure chance. The background was just the result of a red/yellow swatch with random swaths of other colours, plus quite a bit of water making for very runny paint. ![](poppies.avif) I cannot believe how much three-dimensional structure came out of this painting.

Noticing that things are getting quite murky with not having access to white paint, I decided to try brighten things up by sticking to light colours and leaving plenty of white paper visible. The result seems a bit cliché, but at least turned out something like I expected, and the leaves I particularly like (canʼt for the life of me remember how I did that). ![](setting-sun.avif)

### Week 2 ###

Still only using primary colours we were making more sophisticated swaths with an improvised scraper and allowing some mixing on the paper to take place.

#### Splotches: ####

 A couple of random outtakes from an A1-sized page. ![](sandscape.avif)   
![](sandscape-2.avif)

#### Inspired works: ####

We were gradually allowed to use white, black, and then brown paint. All things being retrospective, the new colours tended to get added on top of existing imagery, and this started to create a layering, depth effect.

Inspired only by the swaths, this started with some random dabs and slashes with a knife, and then this scene gradually emerged. ![](cow-grazing.avif) The white was introduced late into this image, and, although the sky looks a mess, it has a veiled, translucent effect and effuses an energy unbecoming of the serene sunshine. The cows were added last, and provide a counterbalance to the energy of the sky with their docile placidness within the prairie. I like very much how this image turned out, especially the strange texture of the sky.

 Relatively low-level effort to make an obvious beach/desert scene with some random wild animals. ![](camel.avif) The flag shadows are an obvious mess, but the overall feeling is of a very warm midday, with the scene shimmering under a hard sun.

### Week 3 ###

 This was the week which started with the unfortunate occurrence of a Monday morning heart attack…

### Week 4 ###

#### Inspired ####

 From a photograph taken of grassy mountains near Innsbruck, my first effort at painting something not abstract or imagined. ![](innsbruck.avif) It doesnʼt look anything like the photograph it came from, but from a distance at least does look like a representation of something realistic, does carry a depth with it, and does present the emotions associated with a roiling landscape under a moody but happy sky.

This started out as literally slapping paint about with no purpose at all, and then segued into an abstracted mountainscape. ![](abstract-mountains.avif) But now I look back at it it is clearly a red river flowing past an overhanging sill of rock covered in either snow or bright sunlight. Over the river bankʼs shoulder large trees can be seen in the distance, and the bank itself is clearly covered in foliage and possibly grass higher up. I wish Iʼd had more time to develop this now, it looks like the beginning of something good.

### Summary ###

 Well, that was surprising. Surprising what came out of it, the unplanned images with their almost-totally out of control colours and living textures. I have to say the results are pleasing on the whole, and seem to get better every time I go back and look at them. It is funny how work effort accumulates generally in life, and I have observed this effect magnified in art.

 I found the whole experience to be surprisingly calming. Letting go of expectations and accepting that you canʼt control everything is an important part of that. A very satisfying course in the end.
