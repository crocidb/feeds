The Meaning of Freedom: Experience of an Intentional Communities Conference
==========

 https://khleedril.org/blog/2026-07-20--freedom.html

 khleedril.org/2026-07-20--freedom

Dale Mellor [ https://khleedril.org/dale-mellor ](https://khleedril.org/dale-mellor)

 20th July, 2026

Iʼve had the best weekend, losing myself and discovering and exploring new freedoms on so many levels.

Guiltlessly submersing myself in a broad brush—particpants and activities—*intentional communities* gathering, I found on driving into the venue that suddenly nothing mattered, the rest of the world was outside, and everything mattered. Everybody mattered. Because everybody cared. The Earth mattered; for some people there, the connection had to be literal.

I so much wanted, just for a short time, to leave my life behind, to find and open up personal space, and get away from the routine drudge: bills, car maintenance, cleaning and washing, and a seemingly impossible overhang of pending tasks all became a distant memory. I made the deliberate intention that I wouldnʼt stress, no matter what happened around me; this is aside from my real life, so what happens here happens without consequence (this is quite connected to the fact that I am currently in cardiac rehabilitation after a heart attack earlier in the year).

Home here was a back-packer tent (with a slightly inadequate sleeping bag despite the hottest weather the UK gets), food was whatever vegetarian creation somebody put on a plate for me, and intellectual stimulation was a good book and a gentle procession of deeply contemplative and quite participatory talks/workshops.

Actually, home here was the exuberant lands of a stylish mansion embedded deep in the Oxfordshire countryside, complete with a marquee, cavernous empty barn, and cooking facilities to provide for a conference of a hundred people.

The whole occasion felt like a flowing river, and I went easily with that. Small decisions here and there about which eddy to follow, but otherwise all paths led to enlightenment.

And yet, floating in full sight on top of the stream, two issues permeated the channel and never could go away: *money* and *conflict*.

My only criticism of these communities, really, is that they do get very introspective, very meta. For example, I observed a role-play of a problem-solving committee with the aim to understand conflict resolution in a particular scenario. But halfway through the actors were instructed to think about themselves as role-players, demonstrating that these communities cannot get away from thinking about themselves against the forces of human nature.

I hear of a vacancy arising in a cooperative housing environment: shared community facilities, gardens, effective ownership of a three-bedroomed house. A small three bedroomed house for £300,000; the realities hit hard. On top of that obtaining a loan to pay the lease is a great challenge; the realities hit very hard. Other cooperatives Iʼve heard actually end up leaving the community spirit behind and morph into a loose collection of independent households.

It was openly acknowledged that anybody living in a community needs a source of income, most practically a ‘second’ job or a pension pot to draw from.

However, with such impositions cast aside, true freedom comes through. The freedom to spend time with children, pursue hobbies, soak in the countryside, even indulge in the opulence of the grand manor house.

I definitely didnʼt miss hearing the news. Ann Widdecombe passed away without consequence to me (no disrespect), though I did feel compelled to tune in to the England-Norway football game! I couldnʼt care that week days turned into weekend; they were all the same, one carefree day after another.

Above all, I have set off on a new journey to seek out a less stressful lifestyle than that I currently endure, and feel excited by the adventures that lie ahead, tempered by the knowledge that a true transition cannot happen for a few years, both because of the magnitude of the commitment—financial, logistical, and social—and because of the amount of research I need to put in; Iʼm sad that I must persevere for such time in my capitalistic, consumptive, and generally unhealthy life.
