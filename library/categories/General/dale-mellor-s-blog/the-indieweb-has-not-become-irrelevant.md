The Indieweb Has Not Become Irrelevant
==========

 https://khleedril.org/blog/2026-08-14--pro-indieweb.html

 khleedril.org/2026-08-14--pro-indieweb

Dale Mellor [ https://khleedril.org/dale-mellor ](https://khleedril.org/dale-mellor)

 14th August, 2026

With reference to the article [*Has Indieweb Become Irrelevant?*](https://starbreaker.org/opinions/has-indieweb-become-irrelevant.html) and this Fediverse [thread](https://caneandable.social/@WeirdWriter/117082499715551480).

The article by an anonymous author is a very negative, energy-sapping one from 2024, resurrected by [Robert Kingett](https://sightlessscribbles.com/) on the Fediverse, and found much support there for the movement away from the Indieweb. It can ultimately be summarized as, “Just build a [static] site and rent a domain.” Here is a guy taking for granted HTTP, SMTP, RSS because they are shrink-wrapped, and eschewing Microformats and Webmentions on the grounds that they are too complicated, because they are new and not yet mature enough for ready consumption by the lay-person. There is zero effort to suggest a better way. These words come across totally as the words of one who has given up on progress, admitting their own failings, and preferring to stick in the mud of old technology.

Donʼt they realize that e-mail, which they totally endorse, is a very complex protocol compared to webmentions? (This author prefers web forms to e-mails, which are simpler, more secure, and do not rely so much on commercial services for their propagation.) The only reason e-mail is in widespread use is that it is immediately available in pre-wrapped application form, with a graphical interface. Even full-stack developers never bothered to roll their own implementation. Same with IRC. The indieweb protocols are really as simple as they can be, but there is not yet a pool of pre-wrapped applications.

RSS is weird in that the sender side (usually) has to be implemented by the developer, but is consumed by established pre-wrapped client applications.

To my mind all this is accepting failure as a pioneer, giving up the drive for progression and falling back on the work of the pioneers of yore.

The article also takes funny turns around simultaneously reconciling indie- and corporate-webs, and then aggressively excommunicating them. Just acknowledge that the corporate web is hostile, and walk away from it. Nobody ever said that indieweb sites have to interoperate with social media sites (apart from the ActivityPub crowd—and the problem with that is that it *is* a complicated protocol), just that they can if they want. The important point is that indieweb sites work with indieweb sites.

*This* website uses webmentions to provide a fully threaded commenting system, and uses microformats to import the pertinent parts of such mentioned web pages. It is kludgy I admit, but I will persevere in constantly improving it and making it work, hopefully helping the indieweb converge on some consensus about how it should all work. I believe in the cause.

My last word is: embrace the (slow) revolution; be the change you want to see in the world. The indieweb can be great! Just donʼt give up too easily.
