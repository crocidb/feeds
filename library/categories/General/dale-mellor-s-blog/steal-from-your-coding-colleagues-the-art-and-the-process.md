Steal from your Coding Colleagues: the Art and the Process
==========

A response to Cesar Aguirreʼs *Steal from Your Favourite Artists*
----------

 https://khleedril.org/blog/2026-06-07--steal-code.html

 khleedril.org/2026-06-07--steal-code

Dale Mellor [ https://khleedril.org/dale-mellor ](https://khleedril.org/dale-mellor)

 7th June, 2026

Reading Cesar Aguirreʼs<sup><a href="#aguirre">[1]</a></sup> *Steal From Your Favorite Artists: the Art and Process*<sup><a href="#steal">[2]</a></sup> made me think about advice I always used to give coders under my wing: spend plenty of time reviewing othersʼ codes, and learn from the experience (I used to press them hard to spend 20% of their time, one whole day every week, reading othersʼ codes).

This is by far the best way to learn the art and craft of coding, improves communication within the team, and helps the whole team to improve. There is a lot that even experienced coders can learn; even the mistakes of very junior coders can sometimes be eye-opening.

* Have they done something differently to how you would do that? *Think* hard before you criticise and rewrite; they might just have done a better job than you would have.
* Is there some code that you find hard to understand? Consider that the problem might just be that you are unfamiliar with the paradigm that they are using.
* Note where the code is particularly easy to read and understand. Why is that? What is the lesson to take away from that?

Reading other peoplesʼ code is almost always an eye-opening experience. By doing it you

* might eliminate some bugs which are lying around in there;
* enlighten your colleague with better ways to do things;
* enlighten yourself with better ways to do things;
* maybe can reduce the line count and simplify the code base;
* identify replicated effort and work together to use common code;
* modify the core architecture to make life a little easier for both of you;
* discuss conventions and between you make both your codes more consistent…

… and at the end of the day, as per the title, *steal from your colleagues*; this is by far the best way to learn to program.
