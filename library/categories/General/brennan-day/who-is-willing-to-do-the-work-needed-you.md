The transformative works website, *Archive of Our Own*, is back online after being down for over two weeks [due to a security breach](https://apnews.com/article/ao3-fandom-archive-transformative-9e752c2cf91409434fcde7188140d066). As you may be aware, I run a project that's a fork of the open-source codebase, [fanfiction.lol](https://fanfiction.lol).

In those two weeks, we went from 300 users to over 1,600, effectively growing 5x in half a month. I did what I could [to be welcoming to the influx of new users](https://fanfiction.lol/admin_posts/16). My amazing team of organizers were up for the task of properly putting the new works in their right places, and it gave me the opportunity to [make improvements](https://fanfiction.lol/admin_posts/18) to the site that had been on the backburner for me for a while.

I wrote about being [up for the task](https://brennan.day/i-am-relentless-and-undaunted/) of providing an alternative to people rather unexpectedly. I'm thankful I get some downtime as people migrate back towards AO3, but the outage gave me a lot of perspective, and time to take stock of not only what I'm capable of doing, but how so few people are really doing the work. Let me explain.

The Art of Restoration [](#the-art-of-restoration)
----------

I love watching restoration videos on YouTube, whether that's [vintage hardware](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uZ7Xm6z38mw) or [art paintings](https://www.youtube.com/@BaumgartnerRestoration), it is incredible to see the intricate, delicate work of repairing something to its former glory. More than that, though, it's the amount of care and love people are willing to put into fixing and healing something abandoned by others.

I recently found a couple [fire lookout tower restoration videos](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KqtwzHf4YbM) that are nothing short of incredible. These buildings are relics of the past. At the peak of the 1950s, there were thousands of fire watch towers staffed and operated across North America, and now [there are only a few hundred](https://canadiangeographic.ca/articles/last-of-the-lookouts/).

[Wander Will's video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=O8WWFLjl6xE) is particularly admirable, having to carry hundreds of pounds of supplies on a ten-hour hike in order to restore the tower. He runs out of food in the last few days, and simply fasts as he continues the work.

Is the restoration perfect? No, of course not. Will admits that himself. He knows his carpentry skills are lacking, and that the window installations aren't precise as he cuts glass from thrift shop frames by hand. It is an incredible, single-person initiative that only cost him a few hundred dollars and ten gruelling days of labour.

But this is the part that stuck with me—he says he's the best you'll get for the restoration, because he's the only one you got. And it's true—I mean, how many people are willing to spend their own money and time to clear the cobwebs and rat shit out of these historical little cabins at the top of mountains only accessible by foot? Beyond him and Rosa, I haven't found any other videos of people doing the same work.

Decentralize the Internet? [](#decentralize-the-internet)
----------

It's an important thing to remember, and something I tell myself. I'm aware that what I do—run websites—is orders of magnitude less impressive or cool. But it is still something so few people are willing to put the time and energy into. Is [fanfiction.lol](https://fanfiction.lol) a perfect replacement for [Archive of Our Own](https://archiveofourown.org)? No, of course not. But fewer than ten AO3-forks exist.

Don't get me wrong, I deeply appreciate that this was a wake-up call that spurred a lot of discussion and discourse about how important it is to decentralize online fan communities, and how much of an issue it is that a single site going down caused so much chaotic havoc. But what I didn't see were any new AO3-forks actually get made, or even planned.

How much of the living Internet is held up [by a few people in thankless volunteer positions](https://coywolf.com/news/web-development/critical-internet-software-is-still-maintained-by-just-one-or-two-people/)? The answer to that is frightening. But it is also hopeful. You don't need to wait for anybody to give you permission; you don't need to wait to be good enough. You just need to be willing to put in the time and energy to try, and give it your best shot. Because who else is going to?

The social media platform Pillowfort is [shutting down](https://www.pillowfort.social/posts/8413273) in a few weeks, but the creators have decided to make the codebase open-source. So, I'm going to try spinning up my own instance when that's released. I won't have any helpful guides the way I did setting up [fanfiction.lol](https://fanfiction.lol), and instead will be working with what I have, with the hardware I have.

Will it be an equal successor? No, of course not. I suspect only a few hundred of the original 200,000 users will register and use the site actively. But that's okay! Because I do think it's better than the alternative of nothing.

The world is full of intelligent, compassionate people who are aware of our many shortcomings. It is easy to be aware, and voice concern and complaint about these shortcomings. But the answer, really, is you. Do you want a more community-feeling neighbourhood? Install a Little Library, soften property lines, look into low-pressure porch culture.

You want a decentralized, living Internet? [Make a website](https://brennan.day/webmaster-a-manifesto-for-everyone/), search for the sites of others, [link to what you find](https://brennan.day/knitting-the-web-we-need-interlinkers/). The answer to our problems is verbs, not nouns.

I'd much rather see a patchwork of initiatives taken on by people who are total novices than the corporate monopoly we have now. To have opinionated, hyperspecific spaces by people who mess up, and to give each other grace and benevolence when that happens, because it will. And you want to make it sustainable? The answer to that is to [support each other](https://brennan.day/yes-buy-them-a-coffee-support-and-mutual-aid-on-the-indieweb/) and mutual aid, too!

It's clear nobody smarter or more impressive is coming to save us. We're the best we got. And that's okay, I think! I think we're good enough and capable. I know that despite what I think of myself and my skills, I'll keep trying. I'll not only scream into the void but also put in the hours of coding and maintenance, only because I can.

What about you?
