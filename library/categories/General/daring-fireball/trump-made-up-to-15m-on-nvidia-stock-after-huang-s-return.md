The Guardian:

>
>
> Donald Trump made up to $15m (£11.5m) on his holdings of Nvidia stock after the US chipmaker’s share price soared on the surprise return of Jensen Huang as chief executive. [...] The US president’s financial disclosures to the Office of Government Ethics indicate he might have owned as many as 370,000 shares in Nvidia as of 30 September, the latest available data, in news first reported by the Financial Times.
>
>
>
> Nvidia’s shares rocketed by 31.5% on Monday, adding about $950bn to the company’s value.

This whole preposterous saga would reek to high hell even if Donald Trump didn’t own a share of stock in any public company. It’s [a national security farce](https://www.theatlantic.com/ideas/2026/11/trump-huang-national-security-risk/689547/?gift=aQyUJR7AIw1mJWdQ6Ed6yMZXlDJkV7MdRh9tXNaeAe4&) and a full-throated embrace of “[state capitalism](https://daringfireball.net/linked/2026/11/24/nvidia-and-trumps-state-capitalism)” (per the well-known socialist sympathizers on the WSJ Editorial Board). But given that Trump *does* own stock in Nvidia, the corruption is prima facie. Who could possibly argue that this is *not* corrupt? The only possible defense is that everyone is corrupt and if everyone is corrupt, it’s OK when it’s your guy doing it.

This, after “corruption” was [the top issue on U.S. voters’ minds](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/23/corruption-midterm-election-cnbc-poll-trump.html) in this month’s election — an election in which Trump’s Republican Party took a historical drubbing. Huang’s reinstatement was obviously popular with a certain segment of investors and with [the AI-mania wing of the tech industry](https://x.com/gruber/status/2124214714825572861), but this whole abjectly corrupt affair is wildly unpopular with the electorate writ large.
