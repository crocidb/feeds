Charlie Warzel, writing for The Atlantic (gift link):

>
>
> It is curious, then, that, as news came out that the Nvidia CEO, Jensen Huang, had had a romantic relationship with a Chinese businesswoman who was also an asset of Beijing’s Ministry of State Security, notable factions of Silicon Valley and Washington, D.C., seemed nonplussed. “Even incredible humans make mistakes,” Bill Ackman, the billionaire hedge-fund manager, [posted](https://x.com/BillAckman/status/2119535982999748830) on X yesterday. “We should all support him and his family. Onward.” The venture capitalist Shaun Maguire [offered](https://x.com/shaunmmaguire/status/2119560017624158265) a five-point analysis of Huang’s situation, noting that the CEO did something “incredibly dumb” and “bad for America,” but that “my guess is there was no bad intention.” He then argued, “I believe Jensen is irreplaceable for America’s AI leadership.” Elon Musk agreed. From there, the posts, written before we know all the facts of the scandal, got only more [absurd](https://x.com/nikitabier/status/2119546515404554613?a=f64k7FpICXhH7V1BfLc&s=20), including [defending](https://x.com/techleadhd/status/2119574905159286985) Huang’s affair on the grounds that his kids are adults now. [...]
>
>
>
> Huang’s thinking on issues such as AI doomerism (he’s anti-doom) and chip allocation in China (he’s for it — a position that warrants examining, given this weekend’s revelations) influences U.S. policy. This [centrality](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DfhRrVWinGI/?obrf=MTI0OGtuc2V4YW1pdA==) is why one tech analyst on CNBC this morning implied that replacing Huang was a comical suggestion. Huang is a singular figure: the linchpin of the company that is arguably the linchpin of the American economy. Framed another way, Nvidia has much of the financial system in a hostage situation. Huang is too big to fail. Which may be why so many people seem motivated to downplay or protect him right now.
