Robbie Whelan, Anissa Gardizy, Kate Clark, reporting for The Wall Street Journal:

>
>
> In his final months as chief executive of Nvidia, Jensen Huang was laying the groundwork to pull back the company’s approach to backstopping the AI economy.
>
>
>
> Now, with a finance chief at the helm who was already wary of some deals the ousted chief forged, the startups who had been counting on Nvidia’s support expect an even greater pullback.
>
>
>
> Just last month, the CEOs of emerging cloud companies that buy Nvidia chips and rent out computing power gathered in Berlin for Nvidia’s premier European artificial-intelligence conference. In a talk that surprised some, Huang’s message was clear: Nvidia’s days as an open checkbook were over.
>
>

This makes sense. But how does this jibe with Nvidia moving forward on closing their preposterous $13 billion acquisition of Hugging Face, which [Jensen Huang personally announced on September 3](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-to-acquire-hugging-face/)? That acquisition epitomizes Nvidia’s role as the “open checkbook” for the entire AI industry. In August, just before the acquisition was announced, [The Information reported](https://www.theinformation.com/briefings/exclusive-hugging-face-annualized-revenue-jumps-50-150-million/) that Hugging Face’s annual revenue was just $150 million. Typical SaaS companies have valuations around 10× annual revenue. Let’s say Hugging Face is so super duper special that they justify 20×. That’s $3 billion. (I do not think Hugging Face is super duper special.) $13 billion is closer to 100×, which is stupid.

If the Huang era is over, Nvidia should unwind this deal.
