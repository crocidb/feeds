Bill Friskics-Warren for The New York Times:

>
>
> Her death was announced in a video posted on social media by her nephew Bryan Seaver. No cause was provided. Concerns about Ms. Parton’s health were raised last year as she [postponed](https://www.nytimes.com/2025/09/29/arts/music/dolly-parton-las-vegas.html) and later canceled a Las Vegas residency, and intensified to the point that she took to social media in October to announce, [“I ain’t dead yet.”](https://www.nytimes.com/2025/10/08/arts/music/dolly-parton-health.html) This May, she said that she had responded well to treatment after her immune and digestive systems had, as she put it, “got all out of whack” in recent years.
>
>
>
> From her hardscrabble beginnings to glittering stardom, Ms. Parton’s life was the stuff of make-believe. With her voluptuous figure, sparkly stage costumes and blond bouffant wigs, she was likened by the writer Chet Flippo to the Big Rock Candy Mountain, the Depression-era symbol of abundance. Her songs spoke to millions, confronting privation and loss even as they radiated possibility and hope.
>
>
>
> She was, quintessentially, a uniter of people. In 2019, The New York Times [ran a story](https://www.nytimes.com/2019/11/21/arts/music/dolly-parton.html) with the headline “Is There Anything We Can All Agree On? Yes: Dolly Parton.”
>
>
