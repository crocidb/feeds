I’m seeing some people object to these settings as being scarier “scare sheets”, and while I disagree, I think that’s a reasonable debate. But I think it’s indisputable that Apple put a lot of thought into these new settings and permissions. Again, you might disagree with their intentions and assumptions here, but given their stated intentions and assumptions — to wit: that many Mac users were granting Full Disk Access permissions to too many apps without realizing just how much access that entails — I think this is a very considered design. The previous settings for these permissions seem slapdash in comparison — like a lot of the “new” Mac System Settings panels.

Even this new support document seems very considered. A lot of times Apple doesn’t have support documents ready to go while the software is still in beta — here they have a good support document for the first beta with the new stuff.

**See Also:** This [Apple Developer News article](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=oqng0zjk), which states:

>
>
> Developers should be prepared to adjust how they onboard users and make sure their apps can gracefully handle having access to only a limited set of folders or apps when utilizing these APIs. Users will see these updates when approving new app permission requests. All app permissions previously granted by users will remain in effect.
>
>

So if you’ve already OK’d an app in MacOS 27.2 or earlier, you shouldn’t be prompted again after upgrading to 27.3.
