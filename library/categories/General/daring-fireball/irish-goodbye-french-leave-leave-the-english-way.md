Over the weekend [I speculated](https://daringfireball.net/linked/2026/11/15/legion-of-pcast) that Apple, Microsoft, and Amazon have “made their Irish goodbyes” from the Trump 2.0 administration (including having no representatives on the David Sacks-led [PCAST](https://www.whitehouse.gov/ostp/presidents-council-of-advisors-on-science-and-technology/)). I [learned today](https://pdx.social/@nedley/117623491685583828) that what we Americans call an “Irish goodbye” is called a “French leave” in the U.K., and “leaving the English way” in France. The rest of Europe is split between those two, but only Americans and Canadians call it an “Irish goodbye”. (And, somehow, the Germans call it “doing a Polish one”, which is not what I would guess that phrase means.)

Anyway, I’ve got one friend who is an absolute master of the Irish goodbye. He never says goodnight or goodbye from a group gathering. He always just disappears. And even when you know he’s going to do it, and you start keeping an eye on him to catch him in the act, the fucker slips away every time. I find it delightful, not rude.

**Update:** The Dutch call it “[taking the rat taxi](https://x.com/historyinmemes/status/2094643916066738601)” and that’s hilarious — but I don’t see how one can drop that into conversation here.
