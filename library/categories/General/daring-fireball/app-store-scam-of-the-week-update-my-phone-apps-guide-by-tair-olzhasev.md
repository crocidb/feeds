“Update My Phone & Apps: Guide” is an iOS app from developer Tair Olzhasev. By default, the only thing it does for free is give you a “Check for Updates” button that launches the system Settings app. It doesn’t even take you to the General → Software Update screen. It just takes you to the root level of Settings.

Everything else it purports to do requires a $15/week subscription. That’s $780 per year. (It does offer a three-day free trial before weekly billing starts.) These other features, gated behind the “pro” subscription:

>
>
> * Clear device status: iOS version and storage usage at a glance
> * One-tap shortcut to system update settings
> * Tip of the Day with practical hacks and best practices
> * Lightweight, private, and fast — no accounts or tracking
> * Optional Premium for extra guidance and tools
>
>

In other words, the app does nothing that iOS doesn’t automatically do for all users. Absolutely nothing. I came across it [from a user on Mastodon](https://aus.social/@shlee/117318918816027001) who discovered their father was paying $8/week ($416 per year) for it. It seems like the esteemed Mr. Olzhasev, savvy businessman that he is, changes the subscription price frequently.

Olzhasev’s other two apps are [Thermal Camera Filter Effects](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/thermal-camera-filter-effects/id6753982355) (a neat trick, given that iPhones do not have thermal cameras or heat sensors) and [Settings App: Simulator](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/settings-app-simulator/id6753862143), a $15 app that admittedly gives you just what it says on the tin. It’s an inaccurate mirror of the Settings app “so you can practice without changing the real system.”

**Update, next day:** These apps are no longer in the App Store.
