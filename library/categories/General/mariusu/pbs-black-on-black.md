**Disclaimer:** While this post is about the “Black on Black” keycap set specifically, it is also a more general review of the PBS profile.

In the [Q3 2026 update](/updates-2026-q3/#keyboard) I mentioned the set of PBS Black on Black, which has been on my [Kunai](/keyboard/#kunai) ever since. I said back then that I might write a dedicated review once I could better judge it over a longer period of time. A year and a half earlier, in the [Q1 2025 update](/updates-2025-q1/#elacgap-xda-transparent-black), I had complained about the lack of textured PBT keycaps in the KAM profile and expressed my hope that PBS would eventually take that place. As far as the shape goes, PBS turned out to be pretty much what I was hoping for. Unfortunately, the same can’t be said about the texture, which is a lot smoother than I would like it to be.

PBS is short for Penguin Belly Slide and it is a keycap profile designed by none other than Matteo Spinelli, better known as Matt3o, who is also the designer of the MT3 and MTNU profiles. According to Matt3o, the name comes from the *hitting area*, which is soft, round and oblong, and looks as if it was shaped over the belly of a penguin, while the *slide* is there because typing on the keys is fun. Unlike MT3 and MTNU, PBS is a *uniform* profile, meaning that every row has the same shape and height. Matt3o, whose first keycap set was in the DSA profile, calls PBS his tribute to flat, low-profile keycaps and an attempt at a uniform profile that is less boring than DSA.

Matt3o began working on PBS in 2022, alongside MTNU, and initially planned to produce it with KBM, which had suggested increasing the wall thickness from 1.5mm to 1.7mm. The project eventually moved to Keyreative, the manufacturer behind the KAT and KAM profiles, with CannonKeys as the global distributor and with the walls back at 1.5mm. CannonKeys released the first PBS blanks in August 2024 and the Black on Black set in October 2024, as an in-stock product for $65, which was restocked several times over the following year. In 2026 a second round added a Modi kit with colored modifier legends.

Profile
----------

[<img class="kg-image" src="/pbs-black-on-black/images/pbs-black-on-black-2342_hu_615f4940fc9dcdc0.webp" width="1440" height="810" alt="" loading="lazy" decoding="async">](https://cdn.xn--gckvb8fzb.com/pbs-black-on-black/images/pbs-black-on-black-2342.jpg)

At first sight PBS looks like a cylindrical profile, with the rounded front and back edges and the straight left and right edges that Cherry and OEM keycaps have. The *scoop* of the hitting area, however, is not a cylinder but a sphere, which Matt3o cuts at the front and the back with a cylinder. The result is a hitting area that is as deep and round as the one of a spherical profile like MTNU, but without an obstacle at the front and the back, as the cylindrical cut lowers those edges. Matt3o describes it as the depth of a spherical top combined with the ergonomics of a cylindrical profile, and he estimates the hitting area to be about 20% larger than the one of MT3.

The *concave* keys are 7.43mm tall at their highest point and the *convex* ones 7.91mm, which puts PBS slightly below DSA, at 7.84mm, and well below XDA and KAM, at 8.74mm and 9.05mm respectively. With a standard MX switch, the top of a PBS key ends up about 17.8mm above the PCB, compared to 20.5mm for Matt3o’s MTNU.

I like the profile and its sculpt, especially the way the scoop hugs the fingertips. People who don’t usually like uniform profiles will likely find the scoop deep enough to keep the fingers from drifting onto neighboring keys, with edges that are soft enough to slide from one key to the next. PBS is a very comfortable uniform profile, more than DSA, XDA, KAM and uniform SA.

Sculpt
----------

[<img class="kg-image" src="/pbs-black-on-black/images/pbs-black-on-black-2343_hu_1fd79b0d3165fdcf.webp" width="1440" height="810" alt="" loading="lazy" decoding="async">](https://cdn.xn--gckvb8fzb.com/pbs-black-on-black/images/pbs-black-on-black-2343.jpg)

Many profiles, like Cherry, MT3, KAT or SA, are *sculpted*, meaning that each row has its own height and angle, so that the upper rows face the typist and the lower rows face away. Sculpted sets need separate molds for every row, and each key only works as intended in the row that it was made for. A uniform profile like DSA, XDA, KAM or PBS uses the same shape for every row, so that any key fits any position of the same size, which, as Matt3o points out, makes every PBS set a free Colemak kit. It also keeps the cost down, as all 1u keys are identical, which was one of Matt3o’s goals with PBS.

As I described in my [Corne V3 build](/corne-v3/#typing), the Corne only makes use of R2, R3 and R4 of a sculpted set, and every key has to come from the right row to keep the sculpt consistent. With PBS any cap fits on any position of the [Kunai](/keyboard/#kunai), as long as the size matches. This finally gives me proper legends, e.g. for my Control key, or my Return key.

PBS is available with concave and convex keys, and it’s up to the designer of a set to choose which ones to use for the bottom row. Matt3o himself prefers concave keys for their flexibility on custom keyboards. The Black on Black base kit uses a concave bottom row, with convex spacebars and a handful of convex extras in sizes from 1u to 3u, which some owners use on thumb clusters. For *homing*, the kit includes F, J and numpad 5 keys with a bar as well as with a deeper scoop. Matt3o prefers the deep scoop, while, according to him, most people prefer the bars.

On my Kunai I’m using the bars, and for the right thumb (space) I’m using a concave key.

### Cherry ###

The Cherry profile goes back to the keycaps of Cherry’s own keyboards. GMK has been producing it in double-shot ABS since it took over Cherry’s double-shot tooling in 2011, and sells it as CYL these days. Cherry is a low, sculpted profile with four row shapes, namely R1 for the function and number rows, R2 for the top letter row, R3 for the home row and R4 for the bottom letter row and the modifiers. R1 is the tallest at 9.80mm and R3 the lowest at 7.30mm. According to Keyreative’s 3D models of its Cherry-profile caps, the lower a row is, the more its top is tilted away from the typist, from about 0 degrees on R1 to about 11 degrees on R4. The top of a Cherry key is a cylindrical cut that runs from the front to the back, so that it curves down in the middle when seen from the front, while it’s a straight line when seen from the side.

Cherry-profile sets come as double-shot ABS from GMK, with 1.5mm walls and a fine texture that turns into a shine over time, or as dye-sublimated and double-shot PBT from manufacturers like EnjoyPBT and Keyreative. I’m not a big fan of the Cherry profile, although on the Corne, which only uses R2, R3 and R4, it felt more balanced to me than on any 60%+ keyboard.

PBS shares the straight sides, the rounded front and back edges of the Cherry profile, but it replaces the cylindrical channel with a spherical scoop and the four rows with a single shape. At 7.43mm, PBS is about as tall as the Cherry home row and lower than its number row, and on the three rows of a Corne the two profiles are within about a millimeter of each other.

### MT3 ###

MT3 is Matt3o’s first profile, which he began drafting in 2015 in a discussion on Deskthority, where the consensus was to create something that resembled the keycaps of IBM’s beam spring keyboards. Massdrop, later Drop, produced it, starting with /dev/tty, which shipped in January 2018 as dye-sublimated PBT with walls of around 1.8mm, while the double-shot ABS sets started with Susuwatari in 2020. MT3 is a tall, sculpted profile with a deep spherical scoop, which Matt3o designed for keyboards with an incline of 5 to 9 degrees, at which the home row ends up parallel to the desk. It adds a steep function row, R0, and offers the bottom row in two versions, an angled R4 and a flat R5. Matt3o’s own 3D files put R0 at 16.2mm, and the letter rows are roughly 11.4mm to 12.8mm tall, which makes MT3 the tallest profile in this comparison. Drop, which owned the MT3 molds, closed its store at the end of March 2026 and turned drop.com into a hub for the collaborations of Corsair, which had bought it in 2023.

I favor MT3 for its sculpting, and it had been on the Kunai before PBS. PBS has a scoop that I would compare to the one of MT3 and a hitting area that Matt3o estimates to be about 20% larger, while it does away with the row sculpting and a lot of the height. PBS is quite significantly lower than MT3, making my Kunai a lot more portable.

### KAT ###

KAT, short for Keyreative All Touch, is the sculpted profile that Keyreative began designing in late 2016. Keyreative describes it as a *MiniSA*, with the slope design of the Cherry profile, taller rows, especially the function row, and a spherical scoop. Keyreative counts the rows from the bottom up, from R1 for the bottom rows to R5 for the function row. Its own chart puts the number row at 11.57mm and the home row at 9.03mm, while its 3D models put the function row at about 13.5mm. KAT is made of PBT, either dye-sublimated with 1.7mm walls or double-shot with 15% glass fiber and walls of 1.65mm, and Keyreative also lists double-shot ABS as an option. Its finish is the silky, satin-like smoothness that PBS has as well.

KAT is one of the profiles that I generally prefer. On the three rows of a Corne, PBS is between 1.6mm and 3.2mm lower than KAT.

### KAM ###

KAM is Keyreative’s uniform profile, which the company developed from the home row of KAT, starting in February 2018. Every KAM key is based on the KAT home-row key, with a shallow spherical scoop and a height of 9.05mm, or 9.94mm for the convex keys. KAM is made of dye-sublimated PBT with walls of around 1.65mm, either matte or glossy, and Keyreative claims that its glossy KAM caps, like the black KAM Blanks that I had on the Kunai, were the first glossy PBT keycaps.

Compared to KAM, PBS is about 1.6mm lower and has a wider and deeper scoop, or, as CannonKeys puts it, KAM is taller than PBS with a less pronounced scoop. Keyreative’s own 3D models put the center of the PBS scoop about 1.1mm below its rim, against about 0.7mm on KAM. The convex keys differ in the same way, as they rise 0.89mm above the concave keys on KAM, but only 0.48mm on PBS.

Material
----------

[<img class="kg-image" src="/pbs-black-on-black/images/pbs-black-on-black-2344_hu_fd6ed2e61bac15e6.webp" width="1440" height="810" alt="" loading="lazy" decoding="async">](https://cdn.xn--gckvb8fzb.com/pbs-black-on-black/images/pbs-black-on-black-2344.jpg)

PBS keycaps are made of PBT, while GMK and many of Drop’s MT3 sets use ABS. ABS is an amorphous plastic that softens at around 100°C, while PBT is semi-crystalline and keeps its shape up to about 220°C. PBT is also harder and denser, which makes a PBT cap about a quarter heavier than an ABS cap of the same shape, and it takes a lot longer to develop the shine that finger oils and wear eventually give to every keycap. ABS, on the other hand, yellows under UV light (shout out to GMK Bleached! *#scnr*), but it is easier to mold precisely, while PBT shrinks more as it cools in the mold, which makes long keys like spacebars prone to warping.

Keyreative itself writes that the PBT used for keycaps is usually PBT with added glass fiber, which reduces warping. In a stream about the PBS prototypes, Matt3o mentioned that Keyreative had added fiber to reduce shrinkage, which caused issues with the shift keys and the 2u keys that were to be fixed for the final release. Some buyers still report 2u to 2.25u caps that are tight on certain stabilizers, which however doesn’t affect the Kunai, as a Corne doesn’t use any stabilizers. At 1.5mm, the PBS walls are as thick as the ones of GMK’s ABS caps, but thinner than the 1.65mm to 1.7mm of Keyreative’s KAT and KAM, the roughly 1.8mm of the first dye-sublimated MT3 set, or the 1.7mm that Matt3o had initially planned for PBS.

As for the legends, *double-shot* molding, which GMK and Drop use for their ABS sets, makes every cap out of two plastics, one for the legend and one for the body. The legends can’t wear off, and both colors can be as dark or as light as the plastics allow. *Dye-sublimation* instead prints the legends onto a film and uses heat to make the dye migrate into the surface of the cap. This works on PBT because it keeps its shape at the temperatures required, while an ABS cap would deform. The dye can, however, only add color, so the legends have to be darker than the cap. With dye-sublimation, light legends on dark caps require *reverse dye-sublimation*, which dyes the whole cap except for the legends. The process is difficult to get right, and Keyreative stated that it tried to avoid it because it was unstable, with a defect rate of over 40%. Laser etching, which is common on cheap OEM keyboards, burns the legends into the surface instead and usually leaves them lighter than the cap and noticeable to the touch.

PBS uses dye-sublimation because Matt3o wanted the profile to be as affordable as possible. Dye-sublimation requires lower minimum order quantities than double-shot, and it doesn’t lock a set into a single typeface, as the legends are printed rather than molded.

Texture
----------

[<img class="kg-image" src="/pbs-black-on-black/images/pbs-black-on-black-2350_hu_617c7165dc38e596.webp" width="1440" height="810" alt="" loading="lazy" decoding="async">](https://cdn.xn--gckvb8fzb.com/pbs-black-on-black/images/pbs-black-on-black-2350.jpg)

My gripe with PBS is the texture. I have long preferred thick PBT with a noticeable, rough texture, like the [biip MT3 Extended 2048](/updates-2025-q1/#elacgap-xda-transparent-black) that I mentioned in the Q1 2025 update, and compared to that the PBS caps are way too smooth for my taste. I had hoped that PBS would be the textured PBT in a KAM-like profile that I was missing, and in that regard it’s a bit of a bummer.

The texture of a keycap is mostly a property of the mold rather than the plastic. GMK, for example, attributes the feel of its keycaps to the *spark-eroded* structure of its molds, and Keyreative even produces glossy PBT, like the black KAM Blanks that I had on the Kunai before and that turned out to be fingerprint magnets. The smooth finish of PBS is hence a choice, and as far as I can tell it is an intentional one. Matt3o designed PBS partially because of the rough texture of Signature Plastics’ DSA, and he described the surface of the PBS production prototypes as pretty smooth. Even his newer PFF profile for low-profile switches, which Keyreative manufactures as well, is only supposed to be one grade rougher than PBS, a difference that he himself calls hardly noticeable.

Compared to what you typically find on the other profiles, PBS is among the smoothest. GMK’s ABS Cherry sets start out with a fine texture that wears into a shine over time, and Cherry-profile PBT from other manufacturers ranges from smooth to grainy, depending on the maker and even the production year. MT3 in PBT, like the biip set, has a pronounced, rough texture, while Drop’s double-shot ABS MT3 is smooth from the start. KAT and the matte KAM sets come from the same manufacturer as PBS and have a similar finish, which people describe as *satin* or *silk* for KAT and as *velvet* for KAM.

PBS is at least as smooth as KAT, and it’s almost slippery. As a matter of fact, while removing the keycaps to replace switches I continuously drop every other PBS keycap because they are incredibly smooth, very flat and have rounded edges. On top of that PBS, just like KAM, shows oil and fingerprints a lot quicker than the textured DSA due to its choice of material and texture. Many like it that way, but I frankly don’t and it’s one of the most off-putting fact about the keycaps.

I’m not alone with my complaint, however, as some in the PBS community have apparently been asking for a slightly rougher texture ever since the first blanks shipped in 2024.

Color
----------

[<img class="kg-image" src="/pbs-black-on-black/images/pbs-black-on-black-2346_hu_c1e97721014be5b9.webp" width="1440" height="810" alt="" loading="lazy" decoding="async">](https://cdn.xn--gckvb8fzb.com/pbs-black-on-black/images/pbs-black-on-black-2346.jpg)

Despite its name, the PBS Black on Black isn’t black, but more of a dark gray. This is especially visible in bright daylight, and on the Kunai, which I [painted](/kunai-corne-v3/) in Tamiya TS-81, a light gray, it’s obvious that the caps aren’t black at all. If anything, it’s black legends on charcoal keycaps, so I would have rather picked something along the lines of *Black on Charcoal* as a name, because *Black on Black* is definitely misleading.

The reason for this is, as I explained above, is most definitely the legend process. As dye-sublimation can only add color, you can’t sublimate black legends onto a black cap, because nothing is darker than black. This is why black-on-black sets with dye-sublimated legends, from PFU’s charcoal HHKB to Drop’s Artifact Bloom Black on Black, use a dark gray body with even darker legends. Even GMK’s double-shot Stealth set gets its contrast from an anthracite gray against black. Matt3o, who calls himself a PBT guy, wrote back in 2022 that the only downside of PBT is that you can’t do dark sets with it. In his stream about the PBS production prototypes, he also mentioned that he had wanted to make the black of the Black on Black lighter, but that everyone told him not to, and for good reason.

To be completely honest, I don’t like the color at all. Unless I’m in almost complete darkness, the charcoal is noticable as such and really doesn’t go well with the rest of my specific board. As a matter of fact, even the (actually black) RAMA USB A-to-C keyboard cable is visibly different in color. Even worse, the two GMK Stealth RAMA artisans that I use as my upper left tab and my upper right backspace key are noticably darker than the keycaps. Hell, even the L-connectors of my TRRS cable are significantly *more black* than the keycaps.

The PBS Black on Black sadly sort of ruins the aesthetics that I went for with my Kunai, making it look rather cheap. It reminds me a lot of [KPrepublic’s $40 PBT Gentleman Black](/keyboard/#kbdfans-dz60-rev3) keycap set.

**Note:** A matte surface scatters part of the incoming light back in all directions, which makes the color underneath appear lighter than it is. In a measurement by Konica Minolta of a dark plastic part with a matte and a glossy area, made from the same resin and color, the matte area came out noticeably lighter than the glossy one. Hence a dark, matte cap looks grayer the brighter and more diffuse the light is, as it is in daylight.

[<img class="kg-image" src="/pbs-black-on-black/images/pbs-black-on-black-2347_hu_4b927382e8ecfac9.webp" width="1440" height="810" alt="" loading="lazy" decoding="async">](https://cdn.xn--gckvb8fzb.com/pbs-black-on-black/images/pbs-black-on-black-2347.jpg)

The legends are slightly darker than the caps and, depending on the light, barely visible. Matt3o said that you could almost consider the set blanks, and I can confirm that legends are easy to read in direct sunlight but completely disappear in low light. Given that the legends therefore serve no practical and almost no aesthetical purpose, it makes me wonder even more why Matt3o went for this relatively cheap looking charcoal color for the keycaps to begin with, rather than making them slightly darker.

**PS:** The modifiers use text legends throughout, with CODE instead of a Windows or Command key, which is what I prefer.

Height
----------

[<img class="kg-image" src="/pbs-black-on-black/images/pbs-black-on-black-2349_hu_ba5a2dcdb75c18c3.webp" width="1440" height="810" alt="" loading="lazy" decoding="async">](https://cdn.xn--gckvb8fzb.com/pbs-black-on-black/images/pbs-black-on-black-2349.jpg)

One key benefit of PBS over MT3, which I also favor for its sculpting, is the height. On the three rows that a Corne uses, MT3 keys are roughly 11.4mm to 12.8mm tall, while every PBS key is 7.43mm, which makes the Kunai about 4mm to 5.5mm flatter with PBS than it was with MT3.

When I [built the Kunai](/corne-v3/#is-the-kunai-comfortable-without-palm-rests) I wrote that, without palm rests, the board is on the higher end, and that I could imagine MT3 or SA making it too tall, which turned out to be true. With PBS the Kunai is significantly flatter than it was with MT3, bringing it back down to a slightly more manageable height. In addition, it also makes it a lot more portable, as it takes up less room in the [NuPhy NuPack](/minimal-yet-productive-travel-desk-setup/#keyboard--mouse) that I carry it in.

Verdict
----------

[<img class="kg-image" src="/pbs-black-on-black/images/pbs-black-on-black-2345_hu_3c5bbfaaa798158e.webp" width="1440" height="810" alt="" loading="lazy" decoding="async">](https://cdn.xn--gckvb8fzb.com/pbs-black-on-black/images/pbs-black-on-black-2345.jpg)

PBS has become one of my favorite [keycap profiles](/keyboard/#keycaps), as it is pretty much what I was hoping for. Compared to MT3, which I like as well, PBS makes the Kunai a lot flatter and hence more portable.

However, the caps are way too smooth for my taste, and the color of specifically the Black on Black set is pretty bad. They aren’t black but more of a dark gray, which in bright daylight and on the light gray Kunai looks rather unpleasant. If you like the silky surface of Keyreative’s KAT and KAM, and a charcoal instead of a true black doesn’t bother you then I guess PBS Black on Black can be for you. If, like me, you prefer a rough, grainy PBT, PBS will sadly feel too smooth. I’m not aware of any PBS set with a rougher finish and considering the author’s posts and preferences I’m doubtful that we will ever see one.

For me, PBS Black on Black definitely is kind of a love-hate relationship.
