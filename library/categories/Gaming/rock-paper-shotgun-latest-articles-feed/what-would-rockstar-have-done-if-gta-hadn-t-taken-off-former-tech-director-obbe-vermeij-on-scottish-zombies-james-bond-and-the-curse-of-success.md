It's bizarre today to think of Rockstar working on games that aren't [Grand Theft Auto](https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/games/grand-theft-auto). Frankly, it's bizarre to think of Rockstar working on games that aren't [GTA 6](https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/gta-6-everything-we-know), which rolls onto console hard drives this week. A decade in the making, with a development team of thousands scattered across an international network of studios, GTA 6 aims to be the Maximum Game. It's both a dense, lively open world and a monument to the exhausting impersonality of "triple-A" game productions. It contains multitudes: birdlife! Realistic cloud formation! Fuel mechanics! Romance! Soda visibly sloshing around in bottles! It's an imperial production [shadowed by labour disputes](https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/rockstar-developer-union-could-end-up-over-1600-workers-strong-and-encompass-nearly-all-uk-staff-below-senior-management-iwgb-claim), that appears to have given Rockstar's various teams little time or space to think about anything else.

It wasn't ever thus. Once upon a time, Rockstar weren't just known for GTA and to a considerably lesser extent, [Red Dead Redemption](https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/games/red-dead-redemption). Once upon a time, they had a reputation for slightly dodgy experiments. I recently interviewed former Rockstar North tech director Obbe Vermeij about a few of them, including Agent, the unofficial James Bond game Rockstar toyed with before putting all chips on [Grand Theft Auto 5](https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/games/grand-theft-auto-v).
