At first the kids jostle to get to the games that they know. "Welcome to the Retro Reset Bus," says Luke Stoner as the students push to get to the screen that’s caught their eye. Sonic on the Sega Mega Drive is the first they see, and the quickest student to get on board the bus looks triumphant as he nabs the stool in front of the console. Jason, Luke’s dad, asks him what he thinks about the graphics – slightly grainy and slightly pixelated – but he’s not at all bothered and soon engrossed. After a few minutes, though, his eyes keep moving to the back of the bus where "Jackson is a wally" is now emblazoned across a flickering screen in seven different colours. Jackson then gives out a chuffed yell when his code finally works after a couple of restarts. His retort to his mate appears on [the 1981 BBC Micro Model B‘s screen](https://8bitarchitect.net/machines/bbc-micro-model-b/) as the other students crane to have a look. This is, incidentally, the machine that turned a national broadcaster into a computer retailer (with help from Acorn), says Jason.
