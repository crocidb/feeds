 New multiplayer game Spirit Crossing offers up a visually blissful, but inwardly draconian world in which other people are not allowed to talk to you – indeed, not permitted access to the visible electromagnetic spectrum – unless you have given them a high five. By default, players appear to each other as shadows, all exploring a skybound landscape of magic animals and layer-cake mountains that reminds me of both [Sable](https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/games/sable) and The Legend of Zelda: Tears of the Kingdom.

 Deign to slap somebody's hand, and they'll pop into colour and be able to join you for various activities. For example, going fishing, playing instruments round the campfire, playing dress-up and pulling emotes in group photos, bouncing around on strangely inflated yaks, or questing forth to heal troubled spirits. There's some material gathering and crafting, and there are recurring storms that make the ambience more threatening, but overcoming a challenge doesn't feel like the point of Spirit Crossing. The "missions" are just an excuse to hang out together.
