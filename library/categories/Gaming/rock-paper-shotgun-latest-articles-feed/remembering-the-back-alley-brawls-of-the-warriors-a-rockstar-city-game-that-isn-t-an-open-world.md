The older I get, the more fascinated I somehow become by how videogames depict night, how particular technologies at particular times give rise to particular nightlives. I've already [gushed about the Starlight Zone in Sonic the Hedgehog](https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/what-can-a-34-year-old-sonic-level-teach-us-about-videogame-light) – the mild dissociation incubated by the soft, ceaseless glare of the construction yard, the eyeless brick at the level's nadir, the sleepless glitter of the skyline. Now, let me wind the clock forward, and backward, to Rockstar Toronto's The Warriors. One long night from the late '70s, sweaty and electric and menacing, fed through dank and sullen polygons from 2005.

Probably the best move Rockstar made with their adaptation of Walter Hill's film is to retain the element of phantasmagoria. Both the game and the movie open with a view of Coney Island's Wonder Wheel, its neon lamps forming a floating tracery of Morse code, cabins swinging almost imperceptibly against solid darkness. This is a kind of night in which light is more emblem than illumination, refusing to travel far, clinging to its source like a frightened ghost.
