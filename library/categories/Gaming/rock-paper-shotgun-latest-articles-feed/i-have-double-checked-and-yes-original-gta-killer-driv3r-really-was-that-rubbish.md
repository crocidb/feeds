At this point the tragedy of [Driv3r](https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/games/driver-3), or Driver 3, or my personal favourite Drive-thrur, is well thumbed. A familiar litany of unrealised ambition, heinous bugs, dull open-world design, and an expensive yet audibly bored voice cast, the accidental comedic highlight of which is Michelle Rodriguez flatly reeling off car theft orders from what sounds like the inside of a Michelle Rodriguez-sized tupperware container.

A more widely forgotten detail is that, for a while, there was a non-zero chance - a belief, even - that Drive Three Er was going to be the opposite. The 2003-2004 hype cycle had sowed the notion that this would in fact be the open-world crime thriller to knock [Grand Theft Auto](https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/games/grand-theft-auto) off its perch, and even 22 years before [GTA 6](https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/games/grand-theft-auto-vi)'s [idolising reviews](https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/its-hard-not-to-worry-even-as-gta-6-launches-on-console-amid-glowing-reviews-rockstar-devs-are-already-concerned-about-getting-laid-off), that was no small promise; Rockstar were still enjoying the balmy success of Vice City, and we could see San Andreas cresting the hill on a stolen, shining BMX. "Ah, but did they have Michael Madsen doing doughnuts in car parks?," publishers Atari asked the world, either unaware or in denial of the spluttering, clapped-out rattletrap they were about to wheel into the showroom.

You likely know the rest, though one of the dangers of familiarity is the subsequent breeding of complacency, which in this case can manifest as a thawing acceptance. I succumbed to this myself, having recently installed a legally unlinkable copy of Drivethreeuh, mind opened to the possibility that maybe it wasn't that bad. Six hours later, I am here to tell you: it was.
