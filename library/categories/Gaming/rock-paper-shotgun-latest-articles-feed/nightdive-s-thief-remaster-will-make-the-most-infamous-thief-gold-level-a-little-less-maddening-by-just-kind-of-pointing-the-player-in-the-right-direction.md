 1999's Thief Gold is widely considered the definitive incarnation of [Thief: The Dark Project](https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/games/thief-the-dark-project), adding three levels to the Looking Glass stealth sim while tidying up some bugs, but it has one grievous failing, [in the eyes of many](https://www.reddit.com/r/Thief/comments/4zqr2u/why_does_everyone_hate_the_thieves_guild/): the notorious Thieves Guild. In this all-new sixth mission, master catburglar Garrett must infiltrate the lair of a gang of pickpockets, after they filch a vase he had his eye on. The lair in question is actually very believable, as a hideout for criminals, in that it resists exploration even with a paper map. It consists of sunken, samey, obtusely arranged rooms that are made all the more headache-inducing by objectives that require a lot of backtracking.

 In much the same way that I can admire a finely wrought pair of thumbscrews, I think the Thieves Guild is a clever creation, one that arguably shines when you use every ability or tactic in Garrett's possession rather than trying to ghost it. But it very easily becomes frustrating, to the point that many grizzled housebreakers advise new players to skip the level entirely. The Thieves Guild should be less frustrating in Nightdive's forthcoming [Thief: The Dark Project Remastered](https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/games/thief-the-dark-project-remastered), thanks to a more revealing spread of environmental textures and various other, furtive adjustments. As with many mods or remasters that speak of boosting the "immersion", I do worry that something valuable is being sacrificed in the process.
