I don't play video games regularly anymore. Not because I've lost interest in them, but because I no longer make the time, the premium on which has seemed to exponentiate with age. I realised, however, in 2019 that I was a kind of sleeper agent: whenever I heard the opening notes of [Legends of Azeroth](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=800be1ZmGd0), the original [World of Warcraft](https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/games/world-of-warcraft) login screen music, I would feel the reactivation of a latent part of my mind laid down when I was 10 years old. And so, like a form of possession, whenever Blizzard announces a new legacy iteration of the original WoW, no downpayment in time nor money is too great to prevent me from returning.

Here I am, then, at the launch of World of Warcraft: Forever, seven years after Blizzard first revived the original 2004 game with [World of Warcraft Classic](https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/games/world-of-warcraft-classic) in 2019. Once more I set foot on Durotar, to spend as much time as I can levelling up a new Orc Warrior before I am forcibly unplugged by either my boss or, more likely, my wife, and so far it's been a welcome return to some of the frictional - but more involving and consequential - socialising that WoW's decades of updates had previously sanded down.
