Tech giant Intel are being taken to court for allegedly "misleading" their investors about the sheer scale of the impact of [generative AI-fuelled component price hikes](https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/valve-see-the-great-ram-shortage-getting-worse-just-in-case-you-were-clinging-to-hopes-of-a-cheaper-steam-machine) on PC sales. The class action lawsuit in question claims that Intel's distribution and manufacturing partners have had to deal with "a massive glut of unwanted PC inventory", and that the gains from Intel's investments in AI datacenters aren't enough to make up for slowing sales of PCs to regular Josephines who just want fancy boxes they can play [Deadlock](https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/games/deadlock) on.

All this follows Intel's last round of quarterly results, in which they announced a significant revenue fall; this triggered an Intel stock price drop from $92.07 per share on October 29th to $65.76 per share the next day, which this week's lawsuit characterises as "the largest single-day price decline for Intel common stock in over 50 years".
