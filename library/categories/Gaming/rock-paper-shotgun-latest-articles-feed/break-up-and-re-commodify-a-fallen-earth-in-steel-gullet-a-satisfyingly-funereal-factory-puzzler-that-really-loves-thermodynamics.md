 It's amazing what a difference a few lines of preamble can make to your understanding of a game. Out now on PC, Steel Gullet is a factory engineering puzzler that, while notable for its meticulous interest in thermodynamics, broadly compares to [the foundational work of Zachtronics](https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/topics/zachtronics), together with more recent and focussed conveyor belt wranglers such as [Modulus](https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/modulus-is-a-zen-factory-builder-in-which-you-build-pieces-for-other-factories). Resources come in, and you arrange machines on a grid to transform them into something else, before sliding them into an output hopper.

 The difference is that you're doing this inside a huge walking fortress city, which stamps across the surface of a largely unseen, devastated and uninhabitable Earth. Your resources are the refuse of a bygone human civilisation: knots of concrete and rebar, flaccid rubber hose and splintered plank, that must be separated out before you can manufacture anything.
