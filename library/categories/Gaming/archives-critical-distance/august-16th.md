Call-and-Response
----------

A story spread around very quickly in games press this week that Compulsion Games’ *South of Midnight* was no longer being published by Microsoft, at least on Steam, with the insinuation being that if you bought the game, Microsoft wouldn’t see the money. This is important given the fact that Xbox remains an [ongoing target](https://bdsmovement.net/news/boycott-microsofts-xbox) of the BDS movement. The situation as it stands with *South of Midnight* may not be as clear-cut as was previously reported. Here’s two writers exercising a little due diligence.

* [No Reply | Autumn Wright](https://autumn-wright.com/no-reply/)  
  Autumn Wright is not satisfied with the assumptions that go with *South of Midnight* having its publisher credit swapped out on one distribution platform (**Further Reading:** [Puke on BDS coverage in games press](https://medium.com/@daavpuke/when-do-we-start-giving-a-shit-a926540faf22)).
* [Why it matters who South of Midnight’s publisher is | No Escape](https://noescapevg.com/why-it-matters-who-south-of-midnights-publisher-is/)  
  Kaile Hultner goes digging and comes away without confirmation (**Further Reading:** [Ian Walker on the worker-owned model and BDS](https://tellthemidied.blog/2026/05/25/im-not-mad-im-just-disappointed/)).

>
>
> “The BDS boycott is not an ego-satiating purity test designed by white leftists with delusions of grandeur and a superiority complex. It is the rational, reasonable and conservative response to decades upon decades of Israeli apartheid and genocide against Palestinians in Gaza, the West Bank, and the rest of the occupied territories. The part of the BDS boycott that is targeting Microsoft is not all-encompassing and totalizing; it is targeted specifically at the easiest thing for someone to stop using; in this case, Xbox hardware and Microsoft-published software. Games like Call of Duty, Fallout, Keeper and South of Midnight. A confirmed and material change to the publishing contract of games put out by studios like Compulsion and Double-Fine under Microsoft ownership would mean that those games would no longer fall under the boycott. And that would be a good thing! Otherwise, if everything stays the same with those contracts, nothing changes with those games’ boycott status. And that’s why confirmation mattered.”
>
>

State Machines
----------

Our next pairing is also an industry-oriented one, but specifically oriented around inquiries of nationality and locality over time.

* [The ‘Geofencing’ Problem: Genres, National Identities and Global Games | Historical Games Network](https://www.historicalgames.net/the-geofencing-problem-genres-national-identities-and-global-games/)  
  Will Butler examines the long entanglement–and gradual disentanglement–between genre borders and national ones (**Further Reading:** [Ogre Run on *Clair Obscur: Expedition 33*](https://ogrerun.com/2025/12/14/anime-rpg-trash-or-clair-obscur-and-genre-trouble/)).
* [Relooted Review — Reclaiming the Future | Gamesline](https://gamesline.net/relooted-review-reclaiming-the-future/)  
  Crystal situates Nyamakop’s Pan-African futurity in relation to a wider, whiter (but changing) South African games industry (**Further Reading:** [Ash Parrish on *Relooted*](https://kotaku.com/relooted-review-heist-game-steam-game-pass-2000667754)).

>
>
> “Relooted is the largest single project by team scope, size and budget in Sub-Saharan Africa to date, perhaps only rivaled by Kiro’o Games’ ambitious 2016 RPG Aurion: Legacy of the Kori-Odan. The African games industry is growing, and Relooted will likely be remembered as a milestone in future histories of the growth of African game development. The in-game heist team returns cultural heritage of the past to Africa, but the Nyamakop team is creating new African cultural heritage for the people of 2099 to remember. Perhaps more importantly than the status of Relooted as an individual artifact, it serves as the first project for a new generation of game developers who will go on to make something even better.”
>
>

Operant Conditioning
----------

Two fresh picks this week on *Zero Parades*.

* [Suicide Mission | Bullet Points Monthly](https://bulletpointsmonthly.com/2026/08/13/suicide-mission-zero-parades)  
  Astrid Anne Rose situates *Zero Parades* in relation to the ouroboroi of espionage, real and fictional (**Further Reading:** [Reid McCarter on *Zero Parades*](https://bulletpointsmonthly.com/2026/07/22/mostly-i-think-about-the-mistakes-zero-parades/)).
* [This Sacred Body / This Bloody Mind | Stop Caring](https://www.stopcar.ing/this-sacred-body-this-bloody-mind/)  
  Artemis Octavio pushes past PR, Reddit bluster, and industry mythologization to try and see *Disco Elysium* and *Zero Parades* separately, together, as they are (**Further Reading:** [Jay Castello on *Zero Parades*](https://bulletpointsmonthly.com/2026/07/29/women-pinned-under-rubble-zero-parades)).

>
>
> “Zero Parades has a clearer vision than Disco Elysium: it is not mired so much in itself and the world that it ends up being a more personal story for Hershel. Harry’s fractured mind only allows him to see the world in what Deleuze would call schizophrenic logic; Cascade is conversely caged by the ideological trappings of her position and service to her country. And it was in those moments when she falters the most, when she has less hope for the world and her government, when she believes nothing matters, where I found Zero Parades to be more nihilistic.”
>
>
